# A FULL verification may accept a device behind a changed enclosure

An enclosure, the USB bridge or dock between a device and the computer, often reports its own serial in place of the device's, and the system then gives the enclosure's serial as the device's. The operator keeps the devices of archives in such enclosures and docks. A device put into another enclosure, or attached through another dock, keeps its filesystem while the serial seen changes, so identity matching gives `MIGRATED` (§B10), as it does for a sector-by-sector clone of the device, and the only way on was a relocation (decision 0004): `archive relocate`, or Relocate to this carrier... in the window, which reads the whole carrier and records it as revision N+1, an operation named for copying an archive to a new carrier, not for a device in a new box. Nothing could tell the archive's device from a clone of it either: the device's own serial, which the device gives to ATA IDENTIFY DEVICE, is read through most enclosures only with administrator permission, which no program of VeriArchive held (R-08, decision 0039 point 2). With the window's redesign the owner decided how the window goes on past a changed enclosure (decision 0046, owner point 5). This record states what was built, in three steps: the engine (42cc463), the device identity reader (54a1685) and the window (2479c58), and two texts of Devices that were corrected with this record (what the page's head says a read there leads to, and an archive behind a changed enclosure under its volume).

## Decision (operator, 2026-11-04)

The owner decided four points: (1) where an enclosure hides the device's own serial, the operator decides whether to give administrator permission (UAC) to read it; the main program never elevates itself (R-12), a small program of its own does, on request; (2) the operator is asked whether it is the same device, a FULL verification then runs, and only when it passes is the new carrier recorded (§B10's `MIGRATED` row); (3) "Confirm without reading the serial" (不讀序號，自行確認) stays, and is written into the hash-chained history, so that it can be audited later; (4) no schema change: an older build still opens the repository.

1. **A FULL verification by the archive's uuid goes on past a changed enclosure only with a carrier confirmation.** `VerifyRequest::carrier_confirmation` says what confirms that the carrier seen holds the archive's device: `device_serial`, the device's own serial was read and is the archive's confirmed device (point 4), so the program judged it; or `operator`, the operator's word (GLOSSARY: Operator confirmation), after reading the device's serial where the archive has no confirmed device to compare it with (the read is kept with it), or without reading it. It names the enclosure's serial as the latest revision records it and as it is seen now, as the operator was shown them, and holds for that change only. It is taken only where identity matching gives `MIGRATED` and the change is an enclosure's (`enclosure_changed`): the same filesystem on one device on each side, both on bus `usb`, or the device seen now behind an enclosure that hides its identity (point 5). Without a confirmation such a verification stops as every `MIGRATED` does (exit `12`, naming relocation). It is taken only by the archive's uuid, never for whichever archive a root alone matches (a confirmation names enclosures, not an archive), and is ignored where the carrier matches as recorded.
   - **Refused** (`VerifyStop::Kind::Refused`, before anything is read, in the engine's words, which the window shows as they are): a retired archive ("a retired archive takes no new revisions"); a verification that adds chunk hashes ("verify the archive first, then add chunk hashes"); while another operation that creates the archive's next revision is unfinished, a registration, a relocation or a verification that adds chunk hashes (decision 0033), in that operation's words; a confirmation of other enclosures than those seen ("confirm it again"); a volume that is, exactly as recorded, another archive's carrier at an overlapping root (decision 0001), such as a clone registered as a copy and seen behind the enclosure seen now; and for `device_serial`, a confirmation that names no device, an archive without a confirmed device ("only the operator can confirm it"), and a serial that is not the confirmed device's ("it is another device").
   - **Any other `MIGRATED` is no enclosure's**: two internal devices, a spanned volume, and a device taken out of its enclosure and attached directly (on bus `sata`, the serial seen now its own while the one recorded was its enclosure's). It stops as before, and a relocation accepts it.

2. **The run records the confirmation, hash-chained, whatever its result.** Its `verify_runs.counts_json` holds the member `carrier_confirmed`: `{"how": "device_serial" | "operator", "device": {"model", "serial", "wwn"} (only where the device was read), "enclosure_recorded": "...", "enclosure_now": "...", "from_revision": N}`, covered by `row_hash` as part of the counts (R-90), in a `FAILED` run too and in each `INCOMPLETE` run of the attempt, so that a wrong "same device" is audited as a right one is; `repo verify-history` checks it as any run. The run's record `run:<run_id>` carries it while the run has not finished, and a resume goes on with that confirmation and no other, from the window or the command line, `from_revision` the first attempt's, and only behind the enclosures it names: elsewhere the run "cannot be resumed". The run's media evidence notes how it was confirmed (`operator_note`): "enclosure changed, same device by its serial", "enclosure changed, confirmed by the operator after reading the device's serial", or "enclosure changed, confirmed by the operator without the serial".

3. **Only a pass records the new carrier, as revision N+1.** A run that ends `PASSED` or `PASSED_WITH_WARNINGS` with every entry matched as a relocation requires (decision 0004; a `MetadataMismatch` recorded with the new metadata, an entry unreadable since registration carried as it is, decision 0010) records revision N+1, written as a relocation writes it (`write_new_carrier_revision`: rN's entries and hashes, its chunk lines and Merkle root, the new carrier's names, metadata and identity), in the transaction that records the run, so that later verifications, unprivileged, match it (`MATCH`). It records none where a name cannot exist on this system and would drop out of N+1, nor where another operation recorded N+1, the archive was retired, or an unfinished registration is creating N+1 meanwhile: the run is recorded, and says why ("new enclosure not recorded: ..."). A `FAILED` run records nothing of the carrier ("the verification did not pass"); an `INCOMPLETE` one waits for its resume. No manifest key and no format change: the device's own identity stays out of the manifest, and N+1's `identity` is the carrier as the system sees it, the new enclosure's serial included.

4. **The archive's confirmed device** (`confirmed_device`) is the device of the latest run whose confirmation recorded N+1 with a read of the device (`device_serial`, or `operator` after a read), or the read recorded with a registration of the archive (point 6), whichever was recorded later, in the order of the media evidence; a relocation recorded after it ends it, as the archive is then on a carrier whose device nobody confirmed. It is read from the history and the media evidence alone. A serial that a manifest records is never taken for it: on bus `usb` it may be the enclosure's. An `import` of a later revision does not end it: an import records no run and no media evidence (an edge, left as it is). The setting `device-identity:<archive_uuid>` caches it (`{"model", "serial", "wwn", "confirmed_at", "run_id"}`), written by a registration with a read and by a pass that recorded a read, removed by a relocation; it is a cache only, and nothing decides by it. `repo rebuild-index`, which loses the history, loses the confirmed device with it, and the window asks again. When the enclosure changes again, a read of the confirmed device (the same serial, byte-pair-swapped as identity matching takes it too, or the same WWN) verifies by its serial with no question; a read of another device is "This is not the device of X", with no answer that it is the same.

5. **The device identity reader** (GLOSSARY: Device identity reader), `veriarchive-device-identity.exe`, Windows only, reads a device's own model, serial and WWN behind its enclosure with administrator permission.
   - **The program**: its own `wmain`, linked with `-municode`, its manifest `longPathAware`, UTF-8 `activeCodePage` and `requireAdministrator` (R-22, R-23), its version information naming it "VeriArchive device identity reader". It is linked statically, so that the elevated program loads no DLL from the program's folder, which the portable zip leaves writable to the user, only Windows' own; the package test checks that it is in `bin`, that it imports Windows' DLLs alone, and none that `bin` also holds, and the notice names the GCC runtime and MinGW-w64's winpthreads compiled into it. It takes a device's number (0 to 63, the N of `\\.\PhysicalDriveN`) and the full path of a `.json` file that is not there yet, and refuses anything else, a file that is there, and a path that a junction leads elsewhere, opening no device then. It sends the device ATA IDENTIFY DEVICE alone, which changes nothing: through ATA pass-through, and where that fails inside the SCSI command ATA PASS-THROUGH (16) (SAT, operation code `0x85`, command `0xEC`), as USB bridges take it, each command given 15 s, as a sleeping hard disk may first spin up. It reads nothing of the device's content, writes nothing to the device, opens no repository and no window, writes the model, serial, WWN and how it read them (`ata`, `sat`), or its error, and exits.
   - **Its start**: through `IDeviceIdentityHelper`, which only the window makes and reads with, on the operator's request: `ShellExecuteExW` with the verb `runas`, from the program's own folder, naming the program's window, where it is in front, as the owner of Windows' question; its result file in a folder made new for each read under the user's temporary folder, read and deleted, a folder that an earlier read left removed by the next; at most 60 s of waiting, off the window's thread, which a cancel stops once the operator has answered. A read is bound to its device: the device is described without elevation (model, serial, WWN, bus, capacity) before Windows asks and again once the reader has ended; one unplugged before the question starts nothing, and one that changed meanwhile is refused.
   - **Why nothing was read**, by the error's kind (`device_identity_failure`): `NotSupported`, no reader on this system; `PermissionDenied`, the operator declined administrator permission, and only that (an error of the reader, such as access denied when it opens the device, is another); the new `IoErrorKind::CommandNotPassed`, the enclosure passes no ATA command on to the device; `Cancelled`; any other, the device could not be read (gone, changed, no answer in time) or the reader failed. `available()` is false where this system has no reader, or its file is missing, as when an antivirus program removed it: the window then offers no read.
   - **Telling, without elevation, that an enclosure hides the device's identity** (`PhysicalDeviceInfo::enclosure_hides_identity`, runtime only, never identity, kept in the media evidence of the volume): on Windows, for a device on bus `usb` whose medium is not removable, its device identification page (SCSI VPD page `0x83`, through `IOCTL_STORAGE_QUERY_PROPERTY` with `StorageDeviceIdProperty` on a handle opened without access rights, R-09) names no device by an identifier unique in the world (an NAA of 2, 5 or 6, or an EUI-64; a locally assigned NAA 3, which a bridge makes up, names nothing beyond it); on Linux, from udev, a device on bus `usb` that is not removable and gave no ATA identity (`ID_ATA`). USB flash memory and card readers are never flagged: their medium is removable, and no enclosure stands between them and the computer. A device whose identification page names it worldwide while its enclosure still reports its own serial is not flagged either (open question 2).
   - **Linux has no reader**: reading the device's identity there takes a SCSI command through `SG_IO`, which needs root or `CAP_SYS_RAWIO`, and no program of VeriArchive asks for either; the window offers no read there, and only the operator confirms. Where udev's `ata_id` reads the device's ATA identity through a bridge, udev reports `ID_BUS=ata`, so the device's bus is `sata` and its serial its own: `enclosure_changed` does not take it for a device behind an enclosure on Linux. Between two such bridges its own serial matches (`MATCH`); moved from an enclosure that hides it into such a bridge, it stops as a plain `MIGRATED`, which a relocation accepts, as for a device taken out of its enclosure.

6. **A read at registration is evidence, not history.** Register Archive may read the device's own serial as the registration starts (point 7), and gives it as `RegisterRequest::device_read`: the registration's media evidence keeps it in its `volume_json`, the member `device_identity` (`{"model", "serial", "wwn", "how", "read_at", "device_ref"}`), its plan keeps it for a resume, which asks for no permission again, and it is the archive's confirmed device from then on, cached as point 4 says. A read on Devices is not recorded (departure 2).

### Deliberate departures from the approved design and from the specification

1. **The question always reads the device again**, and never takes a read kept from Devices or from an earlier question, where the approved prototype reused it, one question of Windows fewer. Behind an enclosure that hides the device's identity, all that the system tells of a device is the enclosure's, so another device of the same model put into the same enclosure or dock since, a sector-by-sector clone among them, is described exactly alike; a kept read could then certify it "by its serial" in the hash-chained history, which is the very case this decision tells apart. A FULL verify after a read on Devices asks Windows once more.
2. **A read on Devices is shown, not recorded as media evidence**, where the specification of this decision said that it is evidence. A read there belongs to no archive and no run, and proves nothing about an archive until a registration records it or a confirmed FULL verification passes; Devices says so.
3. **No picture of the question** (no `12-enclosure.png`): the sample archives are folders on this computer's own volume, which identity matching finds exactly as recorded (`MATCH`). The question needs a carrier found behind another enclosure, which only a made-up identity could give, and the window behind the dialog would show the archive attached as recorded (`tools/screenshots/README.md`).

### Not built from the approved design

1. **The details show no row "Device's own serial"**: the approved prototype shows in the group Device the archive's confirmed device with "confirmed" and how and when it was confirmed, or "not recorded: this enclosure does not pass the device's own serial on". The window shows the confirmed device only in the registration's report, the toast at the end of a confirmed FULL verify and History, so the operator cannot see in Archives, before an enclosure changes, whether an archive has one (open question 4).
2. **"This is not the device of X" only in the question**: the prototype also shows it in the archive's details after a read on Devices (the filesystem the archive's, the device's own serial another), with Register as another archive...; the window says it only inside the question that FULL verify opens, and Devices shows such a read as "no archive records this serial yet" or as another archive's device.
3. **The health pill is not qualified behind such an enclosure**: the prototype reads "Cannot be read (the enclosure does not pass it on)" where the window keeps decision 0044's "Cannot be read", just above the row Device's own serial, which may be taken to mean that the device or its serial cannot be read.

### Open questions for the owner

1. **Register Archive reuses a read that the window keeps**, made on Devices or in the enclosure question (the same device, not detached or changed, and watched without a pause); it replaces the switch, so such a registration always records it, as after Register as another archive..., where the question's read of the other device is recorded for the new archive: the blind spot of departure 1, at low risk, as the operator has just read that device. Keep it, or read again at Start registration (one more question of Windows)?
2. **A worldwide name that an enclosure passes on** (finding F15 of the reader's review): where the device identification page names the device by an NAA unique in the world while the enclosure reports its own serial, the device is not flagged as hidden, and nothing keeps that name. Should the platform keep it as a field of the device, runtime only, which `same_device_identity` would compare, so that such a device is recognised behind another enclosure without a read? It changes what the comparison takes for the device.
3. **The reader's note against Windows' real question**: the window says that User Account Control asks whether to allow `veriarchive-device-identity.exe` (the VeriArchive device identity reader) from an unknown publisher, as Windows names an unsigned program; it may name it by the file's description instead. It is to be checked against the real question on the owner's computer, in both languages, and worded as Windows shows it.
4. **What the approved design showed and the window lacks** (above): build it in a later step? The row Device's own serial in the details would show the archive's confirmed device, read from the history and the media evidence as point 4 says, as "S (confirmed) · how, local time", or "not recorded"; the notice after a read on Devices would compare that read with it; the health pill would say why its health cannot be read where the enclosure hides the device's identity.

## Why

The owner decided the flow (the four points above), and approved the prototype that shows it. A FULL verification is what the operator does after a change of enclosure anyway, and it reads every entry as a relocation does, so a confirmed one can accept the carrier in its place; recording the new carrier only as revision N+1, and only on a pass, keeps decision 0004's rule that the manifest is the record of the carrier (R-14), and never a row of SQLite alone, which a rebuild would lose. The confirmation goes into the run's counts because they are hash-chained and need no schema change, as decision 0044's `device_health` did; an older build reads the counts it knows and ignores the member, and opens the repository as before.

No verification can tell a device from its sector-by-sector clone: both hold the same content. Only the device's own serial can, and through most enclosures it takes administrator permission. The main program never elevates itself (R-12, and GTK refuses to run as root under Wayland, R-08), so a program of its own reads it, as small as it can be: one command, IDENTIFY DEVICE, which changes nothing; no content read and nothing written; linked statically, so that a program running elevated loads nothing from a folder the user can write to; bound to the device that the operator was shown before and after Windows asked. Windows' question for administrator permission, answered by the operator each time, is what authorises it, so there is no service, no IPC and no helper that stays running, which decision 0039 decided against. Where the reader cannot read (an enclosure that passes no ATA command, Linux, a reader removed), the operator's own word stays possible, and is recorded so, to be audited, as the owner asked; the window says each time that a clone passes too.

Linux has no reader in this decision: `SG_IO` needs root or `CAP_SYS_RAWIO`, and a polkit helper is what decision 0039 decided against. The command line gains no option: it often runs in scripts and on servers, and a confirmation is the operator comparing a serial with a label, which the window shows; it keeps naming relocation, which accepts every `MIGRATED`. A device taken out of its enclosure stays a relocation's: the serial recorded was its enclosure's, the one seen now is its own, and nothing but the operator tells that they are one device, while a relocation reads it all and records its own serial from then on.

Rejected alternatives:

- The window elevating itself, or running elevated: R-08, R-12.
- A Windows service, or a helper reached over IPC (R-11 as first written): decision 0039; a read on request needs neither.
- Recording the confirmation in `media_evidence.operator_note` alone: it is not hash-chained (it notes the confirmation besides).
- A new table or column for confirmations or confirmed devices: a schema change, which older builds could not open.
- Recording the new carrier in the settings alone, or at the confirmation before the FULL verification passed: R-14, decision 0004.
- A `--confirm-enclosure` option of `verify`: see above.
- Reusing a read kept from before in the question (the prototype), and recording a read on Devices as evidence: departures 1 and 2.
- Reading through `sudo` or `pkexec` on Linux: an elevated program the window would have to start through a second authorisation, for a case a relocation already accepts.
