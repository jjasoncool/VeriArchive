# A FULL verify adds chunk hashes to an archive registered by V1

Decision 0032 gave chunk hashes to every new archive and every new revision, and left an archive registered by V1 without them until its next registration: chunk size 0 in every revision, so a FULL verify of it says only that a large file's content differs, and QUICK and DEEP, which sample chunks, will have nothing to sample. A registration of a new revision takes a new baseline from what the media hold now, which also records whatever changed since; the operator wants the chunk hashes of what was registered. The operator decided (2026-10-16) that an archive registered by V1 gets chunk hashes only through a FULL verify that adds them, which records a new revision only when every file matched; that new archives, and new revisions of archives registered by V1, use 64 MiB chunks, and an archive that has a chunk size keeps it (decision 0032); and that QUICK and DEEP, which need chunk hashes, come later. This record states what the FULL verify that adds chunk hashes does, how it competes with the other operations that create an archive's next revision, and the technical details decided for it.

## Decision

1. **The option.** `verify --full <root_path|archive_uuid> --add-chunk-hashes` (CLI), and in the GUI's Verify Archive the checkbox "Full verify, and add chunk hashes" (完整驗證，並加入區塊雜湊). The glossary names it: adding chunk hashes. The checkbox is offered only while the drop-down names a registered archive to which chunk hashes can be added; for the folder above, and for any other archive, it is cleared and greyed out, with a tooltip that says what it does. Chunk hashes can be added to an archive whose current revision has chunk size 0, as its manifest says (R-14), and that is not retired (decision 0003): one rule, `why_chunk_hashes_cannot_be_added`, by which the engine refuses and the GUI offers. The engine refuses, before it reads anything of the media or writes anything (exit 12): "revision N of archive U has chunk hashes already; a FULL verify compares them, and there are none to add"; "a retired archive takes no new revisions (decision 0003)". The chunk size is 64 MiB, as a registration's (decision 0032): `VerifyRequest::chunk_size`, which tests set to 64 KiB.

2. **One read.** It is a FULL verify in every respect (§B11, R-104): the archive identified, the manifest of revision N validated, every entry read once and compared, the run result (§A6) and its row in the history. The read that hashes a file whole also gives the hashes of its chunks when it is larger than the chunk size (R-101, decision 0031 point 3); nothing is compared by them, as N has none.

3. **When it records revision N+1.** Only when the run did not fail and every entry of N matched as N records it: `Verified`, or recorded unreadable at registration (decision 0010), whether it can be read now (`Unbaselined`) or not (`Unreadable`, known since registration), which N+1 carries as N records it, still without hashes, as a relocation carries it; entries found in a folder unreadable at registration are in neither revision. A `MetadataMismatch`, an `Unrepresentable` name, and whatever fails the run (`Modified`, `Missing`, `New`, `TypeChanged`, an entry newly unreadable, `SkippedMountPoint`, the identity, the manifest) record the verification alone (point 5). Warnings of the run that are not about entries (`MATCH_WEAK`, a media copy that differs) do not keep N+1 from being recorded. Revision N+1 is:
   - **Entry lines:** N's, byte for byte. The writer gives the BLAKE3 and length of the entry lines it wrote, before the chunk lines (`WrittenManifest::entry_lines_blake3`, `entry_lines_length`), which must be N's body hash and length (N has no chunk lines); otherwise no revision is recorded: "the entry lines of revision N cannot be written again as they are; revision N stays without chunk hashes (`register --new-revision` records them)". Every manifest this program writes passes; one whose entry lines are valid but not canonical (R-64) does not.
   - **Chunk lines:** of every file with a `b3` larger than the chunk size, from the read (decision 0031, points 2 and 4), and the chunk size, `counts.chunks` and `merkle_root` the writer fills in.
   - **Header:** N's (label, `copy_of`, exclusions) with revision N+1 superseding N, made now by this program on this computer (`created`, `created_by`), from the carrier read: its `identity`, root subpath and `root_hint` (decision 0004: the carrier this revision was read from). The format version stays 1 (decision 0031); no media copy is written (R-03, R-89).

4. **Recorded as a relocation records its revision** (decisions 0004, 0023, 0026). The manifest is written and flushed without the write lock and gets its name in the transaction that records the run, which waits for the lock as a registration's record does (`record_lock_wait`). In it, N+1 is recorded only while the archive is still at N and not retired (another operation may have recorded a revision meanwhile, as an import, decision 0030), while no registration has saved files of N+1 (it keeps the revision, decision 0023 point 5), and while the run's record is there (point 8); otherwise the run records the verification alone and says why. Then, in one transaction: the plan of a registration and the record of a relocation that create N+1 go (decisions 0023, 0026), the archive's record says N+1, the revision is recorded with its header and Merkle root, its index with the chunk rows (decision 0032), the media evidence, the run as a FULL verification of revision N+1 (its counts say `chunk_hashes_added_from_revision`: N) with its items, the run's record and checkpoints go, and the manifest is renamed to its name last. A manifest that cannot be written, and a record that cannot be written, keep the run's record and checkpoints: "verification finished as PASSED but its revision with chunk hashes could not be written: C; resume the run with `verify --full U --resume R` to record it" (exit 11), and the resume reads only what the run did not match.

5. **Otherwise the verification alone,** as a FULL verify records it: a run of revision N, and the reason, which the CLI and the GUI show as "chunk hashes not added: …": "the verification did not pass; revision N stays without chunk hashes"; "not every entry matches revision N as recorded; revision N stays without chunk hashes (`register --new-revision` records the archive as it is now, with chunk hashes)"; "archive U is at revision M now, recorded by another operation while this verification ran"; "archive U was retired while this verification ran; …"; "an unfinished registration of archive U (root "R") is creating revision N+1; finish it with `register --resume`, or abandon it with `archive abandon-registration U`"; "the verification was abandoned while it ran".

6. **Resume (R-104).** The run's record (`run:<run_id>`, decision 0022) also holds `add_chunk_hashes`, the chunk size at which it adds them, `process`, the process that runs it (decision 0024), and `stopped` once an attempt ends without being recorded, as when its revision's manifest or its record cannot be written (point 4). That attempt writes it last, as a registration's attempt writes its stop mark (decision 0022), since its process may go on, as the GUI's does: the run's resume, abandoning it (point 8) and the Archive List (point 9) then take it for stopped, as they take a registration whose plan says so (decision 0024), not for running while that process runs. A run recorded `INCOMPLETE` has stopped by its row in the history, and its record keeps no mark. `verify --resume <run_id>` (the GUI's Resume) goes on adding them at that chunk size, also without `--add-chunk-hashes`; with it, a run that adds none is refused: "run R adds no chunk hashes; resume it without --add-chunk-hashes, or start a new verification with it". Verify Archive's Resume continues such a verification also after the window that ran it was closed, or crashed, or the computer lost power: once its archive is chosen in the view's list, Resume continues the archive's verification that adds chunk hashes, found by the archive's uuid, when the Archive List would show it stopped from one read (point 9; `app::resumable_adding_verification`), never while it runs or is held up, in this window or elsewhere. The run interrupted last in the window, which Resume continued before, stays the one it continues while the archive of that run is chosen, or the chosen archive has no such verification (`app::run_to_resume`). A checkpoint keeps the chunk hashes of the files it saves, in its transaction, in a table of their own, `verify_progress_chunks (run_id, path, raw_hex, stream, idx, b3)`: beside `verify_progress`, removed with it (`clear_progress`), and taken over with it by a resume. A resume reads again a checkpointed file only where its chunk hashes are not all kept (rows with a gap are read back as none, and fewer than its size gives are not enough): nothing else is read twice. The schema version is 3, which adds that table (§B8: V1.1 adds tables; R-92); a repository of version 1 or 2 is raised to 3 when this version opens it, in one write transaction, which first keeps a copy of its database beside it and upgrades nothing without it (decision 0032, point 3), and a program of an earlier version refuses it from then on. The history says which runs added chunk hashes, at which chunk size (`add_chunk_hashes` in their counts).

7. **One operation creating an archive's next revision at a time.** A registration of a new revision, a relocation and a verification that adds chunk hashes each create the archive's next revision. A verification that adds chunk hashes is unfinished while its record adds them to the revision the archive is at; one that adds them to an earlier revision can never finish (R-104). While one is unfinished, a registration of the archive's next revision (not a resume) and a relocation of the archive (not a resume) are refused in the transaction that would begin them, before they read anything (exit 12): "an unfinished verification of archive U that adds chunk hashes (run R) is creating revision N; nothing was changed (finish it with `verify --full U --resume R` first, or abandon it with `archive abandon-verification U`)". A verification that adds chunk hashes is refused likewise, before it reads anything, while a registration of the archive is unfinished (its plan's revision not recorded: in decision 0025's words, "an unfinished registration of archive U (root "R") is creating revision N; nothing was changed (finish it with `register --resume` first, or abandon it with `archive abandon-registration U`)"), while a relocation of it is unfinished (its next revision not recorded: in decision 0026's words, "an unfinished relocation of archive U (to "R") exists; resume it …, or abandon it …", or for one whose new carrier differs, that it cannot be resumed and is to be abandoned), and while another verification that adds chunk hashes to it is unfinished, naming that one. The check and the record of the run are one write transaction, so two started at once cannot both begin. A resume takes its run over in such a transaction too: the run's checkpoints and kept chunk hashes move under the resume's run, and the record of the run before goes. It is refused while the process of the run before still runs on this computer, unless that run was recorded `INCOMPLETE` (a cancelled run, whose process may go on, as the GUI's): "this verification is still running in process N (P) on this computer; resume it once that process has ended"; a resume of a run that another resume took over is refused, naming that one. The checks of decision 0023 at each record stay, for operations that do not look (an import, a version before this decision). A registration and a relocation do not refuse each other at their start: between them decision 0023 holds as it was.

8. **Abandoning.** `archive abandon-verification <uuid> [--confirm] [--json]`, and **Abandon...** on the row of a stopped one in the Archive List, abandon the archive's unfinished verification that adds chunk hashes, as decisions 0025 and 0026 abandon a registration and a relocation: in one write transaction its record, its checkpoints and the chunk hashes kept with them go, and nothing else (the archive, its revisions and manifests, media evidence, its `INCOMPLETE` runs in the history, the media). Refused (exit 12) when there is none ("archive U has no unfinished verification that adds chunk hashes"), while its attempt runs, judged as decision 0024 judges an attempt, a run recorded `INCOMPLETE` having stopped ("the verification of archive U that adds chunk hashes is still running in process N (P) on this computer; abandon it once that process has ended"), and, where its process cannot be checked, while its record said less than 3 minutes ago that it works; the GUI names the run it showed. Without `--confirm` the CLI shows what abandoning removes (the archive, the revision, the root, when it started, the run, the files and bytes matched) and stops (exit 12); `--json` gives it as `verification`, and after `--confirm` the verification abandoned. An attempt abandoned where its computer could not see it saves no checkpoint from then on, records the verification alone, saying so, and leaves no checkpoints, also when it is interrupted. A verification that adds no chunk hashes is not abandoned: a later verification supersedes it (decision 0022). The glossary names it: an abandoned verification.

9. **Where it shows.** The Archive List lists a verification that adds chunk hashes as an operation of its own words, "Verification adding chunk hashes" (驗證（加入區塊雜湊）): no later verification of its archive supersedes it (decision 0022, point 5), as it keeps the archive's next revision from every other operation until it finishes or is abandoned. Its state is judged as a registration's (decision 0024): by its record, a run recorded `INCOMPLETE` having stopped, and by the process its record names, so that one whose process still runs is held up, not stopped, and one whose process has ended has stopped at once; its tooltip names the process. A stopped one has **Abandon...**, which reads what abandoning removes with the CLI's check (`abandonable_verification`) and then asks, with **Cancel** as the default: "Abandon the unfinished verification of ARCH-0007 that adds chunk hashes? … Its saved progress will be lost: N files (S) matched so far. It cannot be resumed afterwards. The archive, its revisions, manifests and the verification history are not changed." `archive list` and `archive show` show it under the archive, as they show a registration and a relocation (decision 0026, point 7), judged and worded as the Archive List's row from one read: "Unfinished verification of revision N adding chunk hashes, from "R": P; S" (N the revision it verifies); their `--json` gives the archive the member `unfinished_verification`: `root`, `revision`, `run_id`, `chunk_size`, `started`, `updated`, `state`, `resumable`, `files_done` and `bytes_done` (null where the checkpoints are not counted), `files_total`, `bytes_total`, and `process` (`id`, `program`, `host`). The record of one that adds chunk hashes to an earlier revision than its archive's is not listed once it has stopped (decision 0022), refuses nothing, and goes with its saved progress when the next verification that adds chunk hashes to the archive begins.

10. **What is shown.** The CLI's text output gives, after the items, "chunk hashes added: revision N+1 records them (chunk size 64 MiB, C chunks, Merkle root <64 hex digits>)", or "chunk hashes not added: <why>"; `verify --json` gives a run that adds chunk hashes `chunk_hashes`: `chunk_size`, `added_revision`, `chunks` and `merkle_root` (null while none is recorded) and `not_added` (null unless a finished run records none). The run's result line names the revision the run is recorded under: N+1 once it recorded it. The GUI's report gives the same lines. `archive show` gives N+1's chunks from its manifest (decision 0032, point 8).

## Why

**Through a FULL verify, only when everything matched.** The operator decided it. The revision it records claims what a registration claims, the archive as read, and holds N's entries, which only a read that matched every one of them proves to still be the archive. A difference is the business of `register --new-revision`, which takes the archive as it is now and gives it chunk hashes too. Metadata is part of the entry lines: a revision with N's lines and other metadata on the media would record what the media no longer hold, and one with the media's metadata would no longer have N's lines.

**Entry lines byte for byte, held to N's body hash.** N's bytes are the integrity truth (R-14); the new revision adds lines after them and changes nothing before. Comparing the hash of what was written with N's body hash proves it for every entry, without reading N again or keeping its lines.

**Entries unreadable at registration carried.** As in a relocation (decision 0010): an entry that could not be read when the baseline was taken has no hashes to compare, and a copy of it read now cannot become a trusted baseline by a verification; the archive's next registration takes it.

**The carrier read in the header.** Every revision's identity is the carrier it was read from (decision 0004); this revision was read now.

**A FULL verification of N+1.** As the relocation's: every entry of N+1 was read and matched in this run, and its chunk hashes come from that read. "Last FULL verify" (R-21) is the archive's either way.

**Kept with the checkpoints.** A resume that reads again every checkpointed large file reads the largest part of an archive twice. The chunk hashes belong to the run's checkpoints, which move with the run at every resume and go with it; in a table of their own they touch nothing a registration saves under N+1 (decision 0032), nor the index that `repo rebuild-index` rebuilds (R-80), which never touched `verify_progress`.

**One operation at a time.** Each of these operations reads the whole archive to create the same revision, and only one of them can record it (decision 0023): two at once spend a full read for nothing. A registration also gives chunk hashes, and a verification that adds them to a revision another operation is replacing adds nothing. Refused at its start, an operation has done no work yet; refused at its record, hours. The refusal names how to go on, finishing the other or abandoning it, as decisions 0025 and 0026 refuse a second registration and a second relocation of an archive.

**Shown where the others are.** `archive list` and `archive show` are where the command line shows an archive's state (decision 0026), and the Archive List where the GUI shows what has not finished: a verification that keeps every other operation from creating the archive's next revision shows in both, as a registration and a relocation do, and its process tells whether it still runs, as theirs does, so that one held up by a slow read is not shown stopped and offered for abandoning.

**Abandoned on purpose.** A stopped verification that adds chunk hashes keeps every other operation from creating the archive's next revision, also when its media failed and it can never finish: the operator needs a way out, as for a registration (decision 0025) and a relocation (decision 0026), and it names the archive, as they do.

Rejected alternatives:

- Recording N+1 with the media's metadata where a `MetadataMismatch` was found, as a relocation does: its entry lines would no longer be N's.
- Recording N+1 only when the run `PASSED`: an archive with entries unreadable at registration, or without a hardware serial (`MATCH_WEAK`), could never get chunk hashes, though everything that can be compared matched.
- A FULL verification of N for the run that records N+1: the archive's history would show a revision that no FULL verification read.
- Keeping the chunk hashes of checkpointed files in `chunks` under N+1, as a registration's saved files: they would mix with a registration's saved files of N+1, and `repo rebuild-index` removes chunk rows without a file row.
- Keeping them in `settings`: one value rewritten at every checkpoint, growing with the archive.
- Reading the checkpointed large files again at a resume (the interim of this decision's first stage): nothing is read twice is what R-104 asks.
- Letting a registration or a relocation take the revision first and the verification find it recorded at its record (decision 0023's rule between a registration and a relocation): one of them reads the whole archive for nothing.
- A registration that removes the saved progress of a stopped verification that adds chunk hashes: throws away hours of reading unasked (decision 0025's reasons).
- `archive abandon-registration` or `abandon-relocation` for it: another operation, with other saved progress (decision 0026, point 6).
