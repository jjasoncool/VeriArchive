# Changing an archive's carrier writes a new revision

The manifest header's `identity` is the only trusted record of which media holds an archive, and a manifest is immutable (V1.1: signed). Recording a new carrier in SQLite alone would make the index an integrity source (violates R-14) and would be lost by `repo rebuild-index` (R-80); HANDOFF's `MIGRATED` row ("new device evidence is recorded") had this gap and would re-prompt forever. We decided that both relocation (archive copied to a new volume) and `MIGRATED` acceptance (same filesystem, new hardware) run one operation, `archive relocate <uuid> <root_path>`: a FULL verify of the new carrier against the latest revision rN that must yield `Verified` for every entry, after which the same read pass is written out as r(N+1) with identical entry hashes and the new carrier's `identity`.

## Consequences

- Any `Modified` / `Missing` / `New` / `TypeChanged` / `Unreadable` aborts the relocation; changing content is `register --new-revision`, not relocation.
- Amended 2026-10-27: the refusal says what to do, as the user guides do: "the new carrier does not hold the recorded content; nothing was changed: copy the archive again from its carrier to the new one, then relocate again" (exit 3). It said "(use `register --new-revision` to re-baseline changed content)", which is for content changed on purpose at the archive's own root: run on the new carrier it was refused ("--new-revision: no registered archive at this root", exit 12), a dead end. The text lists each entry that differs with its detail, as a verification does, and `--json` gives them as `items` (`p`, `status`, `detail`, as a verification's), beside `error` and `exit_code`, where it gave only those two. Tests: the refusal's words in the engine, and the CLI end to end with a sample copy in which one file changed: the words in either language, never `--new-revision`, the entry with its detail, and `items` in `--json`; a mutation check that left the entries out of `--json` failed the test. `MetadataMismatch` is allowed (copying commonly changes mtimes) and r(N+1) records the new metadata.
- Matching (§B10) uses the latest revision's `identity`. Media matching only an older revision's `identity` is reported as `PREVIOUS_CARRIER` (new `media_evidence.match_result` value) rather than `UNKNOWN`, and `register` on it is refused without `--force-new-archive`. It is not verified by default.
- No header key changes, so no format version bump; `identity` now means "the carrier this revision was read from" rather than "the carrier at first registration".
- Relocation is resumable like any FULL verify (R-104).
- Amended by decision 0010: entries already unreadable in rN do not block relocation and are carried forward unchanged.
- Amended by decision 0026: a relocation keeps a record of its own (`relocation:<archive_uuid>`) and checkpoints the entries that matched; `archive relocate --resume` continues it while the archive is at rN and the new root is on the volume and in the folder it started on; a carrier that differs ends it (an entry that could not be read only once the comparison has read everything), and `archive abandon-relocation` gives it up.
- Amended by decision 0033: a relocation is not the only operation that records a revision with rN's content hashes: a FULL verify that adds chunk hashes records r(N+1) with rN's entry lines byte for byte, read and matched on the carrier rN was read from, and their chunk lines, only when every entry matched, also in the transaction of its FULL verification of r(N+1).
- Amended by decision 0047: a relocation is no longer the only acceptance of a new carrier. Where the `MIGRATED` change is an enclosure's (the same filesystem on one device on each side, both behind a USB bridge, or the device seen now behind an enclosure that hides its identity), a FULL verification by the archive's uuid with a carrier confirmation (the device's own serial read and equal to the archive's confirmed device, or the operator's word) accepts it the same way: when every entry matched as this record requires, it records r(N+1) with rN's entry hashes and the new carrier's `identity`, written as a relocation writes it, in the transaction of the run; any other result records the run, with its confirmation, and nothing of the carrier. Every other `MIGRATED` is still a relocation's, and `archive relocate` still accepts an enclosure's too. A relocation ends the archive's confirmed device.
