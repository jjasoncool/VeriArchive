# Relocation resumes

Decision 0004 promised that relocation "is resumable like any FULL verify (R-104)", and §B16 shows `archive relocate <uuid> <root_path> [--resume]`, but a relocation kept nothing while it ran. Interrupted (Ctrl+C, the new disk unplugged, the computer restarted, a record that could not be written), it had read the new carrier for nothing, often for hours, and the next attempt read all of it again. Nothing showed it either: the Archive List (decision 0022) knows registrations and verifications only. And `repo rebuild-index` emptied the saved progress of unfinished registrations together with the index (decision 0025 noted it), so that their resumes read everything again.

## Decision

1. **The record.** A relocation keeps a record of its own in `settings`, under `relocation:<archive_uuid>`: one per archive, never a verification's `run:<run_id>`. So `verify --resume` cannot take it over, and decision 0022's rule that a later verification of the archive supersedes an unfinished one never hides it. The record is a JSON object; as with the records of decision 0022, members are added, none is removed or changes its meaning, and a reader ignores members it does not know:

   | Member | Meaning |
   |---|---|
   | `archive_uuid`, `label` | the archive |
   | `revision` | N, the revision it verifies against; it creates N+1 |
   | `run_id` | the run its checkpoints are saved under, and the id of its FULL verification once recorded |
   | `started` | when its first attempt started |
   | `root` | the new root, where its latest attempt reads (UTF-8) |
   | `root_subpath` | that root's folder on its volume at the start (§B2 form) |
   | `evidence` | the new carrier's media evidence at the start: `seen_at`, and `volume`, the `VolumeInfo` as `media_evidence.volume_json` holds it |
   | `files_total`, `bytes_total` | the files (and streams) of rN whose content has hashes, and their bytes: what the checkpoints count (as a verification's, decision 0022) |
   | `updated`, `stopped` | as in a registration's plan (decision 0022) |
   | `process` | the process that runs its attempt (decision 0024) |
   | `resumes` | how often it was resumed |
   | `differs` | when an attempt found that the new carrier does not hold the recorded content (point 5): from then on the relocation can never finish, and it is never resumed |

   The record is written in one write transaction before the new root is scanned (it waits for the write lock as a record does, decision 0023), again while the relocation works (`updated`, every 30 seconds), and once more, with `stopped`, by an attempt that ends without finishing. An attempt that finds the new carrier differing writes `differs` at once, and each later write of that attempt repeats it (point 5). A write changes the record only while the key holds this relocation's record (its `run_id`) and N+1 is not recorded.

2. **Checkpoints.** The entries whose content matched are saved as a verification's are (R-104): into `verify_progress` under the record's `run_id`, every 1 000 entries or 60 seconds, and the last ones when an attempt stops. Each checkpoint looks first, in its own transaction, whether N+1 is recorded (by another operation), the key no longer holds this relocation's record (abandoned), or the archive has a later revision than N while N+1 is not recorded, as after an import of N+2 (decision 0030, point 6); then the attempt stops, and saves nothing.

3. **`archive relocate <uuid> <root_path> --resume`** continues the archive's unfinished relocation. In the transaction that takes it over, before the new root is scanned:
   - There must be one; otherwise "archive U has no unfinished relocation to resume" (exit 12).
   - One whose revision N+1 is recorded by now (by an operation that left the record, point 5) can never finish: its record and checkpoints go, and the resume stops: "revision N+1 of archive U was recorded by another operation; the unfinished relocation can no longer finish, and its saved progress was removed" (exit 12).
   - One whose record says `differs` (point 5) can never finish either, and stays for the operator to abandon: "the unfinished relocation of archive U (to "R") found that the new carrier does not hold the recorded content and cannot be resumed; abandon it with `archive abandon-relocation U`, then relocate again" (exit 12).
   - One made for an older revision, whose archive now has a later revision than N while N+1 is not recorded (an import of N+2), can never finish either, and stays for the operator to abandon (decision 0030, point 6): "the unfinished relocation of archive U (to "R") verifies revision N, but the archive now has a later revision (M); the relocation can no longer finish: abandon it with `archive abandon-relocation U`, then relocate again" (exit 12).
   - R-104: the archive must still be at revision N, which it is not either when `repo rebuild-index` skipped the manifest of N and so set the archive back below N; otherwise "archive U is at revision M, not at revision N that its unfinished relocation verifies; it cannot be resumed (abandon it with `archive abandon-relocation U`)" (exit 12).
   - Decision 0024: never while the process that ran the attempt before still runs on this computer: "this relocation is still running in process N (P) on this computer; resume it once that process has ended" (exit 12). The record is judged as a registration's plan is (decision 0024, point 2); where its attempt is not known (another computer), the resume goes ahead, and the first to record the revision wins (decision 0023).
   - The new root must be on the volume the relocation started on, as identity matching tells it (§B10) against the evidence in the record: `MATCH`, or `MATCH_WEAK` where a serial or the filesystem id cannot be read; not `MIGRATED`, `MISMATCH` or another volume. And in the same folder on it (`root_subpath`, compared as decision 0001 compares roots). Otherwise: "the volume or folder of this root is not the one the unfinished relocation of archive U started on ("R"); resume it there, or abandon it with `archive abandon-relocation U` and start again" (exit 12). The path is not compared: the same disk under another drive letter or mount point resumes, and the record names the path it reads now.

   Then the record names this attempt's process, without the stop mark of the attempt before. The relocation scans the new root and reads every entry that did not match before; an entry that did, and whose size is unchanged, is not read again and is carried into r(N+1) with rN's recorded hashes, as a resumed verification skips it (its metadata is compared again, and r(N+1) records the new carrier's).

4. **One unfinished relocation per archive.** `archive relocate` without `--resume` is refused while a relocation of the archive is unfinished, whether it runs or stopped, and changes nothing: "an unfinished relocation of archive U (to "R") exists; resume it with `archive relocate U <root_path> --resume`, or abandon it with `archive abandon-relocation U`" (exit 12); one whose record says `differs`, and one made for an older revision (decision 0030, point 6), in the words of point 3, which name abandoning only. A record whose N+1 is recorded is not unfinished (point 3): it goes, and the new relocation begins. Two relocations started at once cannot both begin: the record is made in a write transaction that looks for another first. That transaction also finds the archive still at the revision the relocation read when it started, whose manifest it compares the new carrier with; one that another operation overtook meanwhile writes nothing, to be run again (exit 12, decision 0030, point 6).

5. **How a relocation ends.**
   - **Recorded.** In the transaction that records r(N+1) (as decision 0023 records a revision: N+1 found not recorded, a registration with saved files of N+1 left its revision, the plan of N+1 removed, the manifest named last; and the archive found at no later revision than N, decision 0030, point 6), the record and its checkpoints go. Rows under N+1 that no plan names are no registration's saved progress (point 8): left by a crash, or by a registration of an older version abandoned while it ran, they go in that transaction, as an import's record removes them (decision 0030, point 3), and the index of r(N+1) replaces them; only a registration whose plan creates N+1 keeps the revision from the relocation. The FULL verification it records keeps the relocation's `run_id` and ran from the relocation's start (`started`): it spans the `INCOMPLETE` runs of the attempts before it (below), and its counts and `coverage_bytes` are the whole relocation's, theirs included; its `counts_json` says how often it was `resumed`, when it was.
   - **The new carrier does not hold the content** (decision 0004: `Modified`, `Missing`, `New`, `TypeChanged`, `Unreadable`; also `SkippedMountPoint` and `Unrepresentable`): the relocation ends. As soon as the comparison finds the first such entry, the attempt writes `differs` into the record, and each later write of the record by that attempt (its `updated`, its stop mark) repeats it, each made when the database allows; it reads on, to list every entry that differs. An attempt interrupted after that (cancelled, its device removed) ends the same way, with the entries found so far: it is not left for `--resume`. It is recorded `INCOMPLETE` in the history first, as every interrupted attempt is (below), with those entries. An entry that could not be read (`Unreadable`) is such an entry only once the comparison has read everything: a disk that drops off usually fails the read in flight with an I/O error (`Unreadable(media)` or `Unreadable(other)`) before the next open finds the device removed. So the record is not marked when such an entry is found, and an attempt interrupted after entries that could not be read, and no other entry that differs, is interrupted (below): its record says nothing of `differs`, and a resume reads those entries again, as they never matched. A comparison that read everything and could not read an entry ends the relocation. The record and its checkpoints go, in a transaction that waits for the write lock as the record does (decision 0023). What the operator does next, copying again, changes the carrier: what matched before then says nothing, and the next relocation reads it all. Where they still cannot be removed (the lock held for longer still, or the removal failing), the record keeps `differs`, which `--resume` and a new relocation refuse (points 3 and 4), and the refusal says so, with the cause, and how to go on: "the new carrier does not hold the recorded content; nothing was changed, but the relocation's saved progress could not be removed: C; abandon the relocation with `archive abandon-relocation U` before relocating again" (exit 3).
   - **Its revision recorded by another operation** (a registration of N+1 that recorded first, an import): it ends and records nothing. Decision 0023, point 3, now holds for relocations too: what records revision N+1 also removes, in its transaction, the record and checkpoints of a relocation that creates N+1. A registration does; an import does not (as it leaves plans), and then the relocation's next checkpoint, record or resume removes it.
   - **Made for an older revision** (decision 0030, point 6): another operation, such as an import of N+2, gave the archive a later revision than N while N+1 is not recorded. The attempt stops at its next checkpoint or at its record, records nothing and no run, and says so (exit 12, in the words of point 3). So does an attempt that is interrupted, or that a failure stops, once the archive has such a revision: it names no resume, which would be refused, and records no run (decision 0030, point 6). Its record, which says that it stopped, and its checkpoints stay until the operator abandons the relocation (point 6); no resume and no new relocation takes it over (points 3 and 4).
   - **Interrupted** (cancelled, also while its record waits for the write lock, or its device removed, also after entries that could not be read: above), **stopped by a failure** (a scan of the new root that failed, a manifest that could not be written or named, a record that could not be written, such as a database that stayed locked), or **refused because a registration of N+1 has saved files** (decision 0023): the record and its checkpoints stay, the record says it stopped, and `--resume` continues, unless the archive has a later revision than N by then (above). An interruption reads "relocation was interrupted; continue it with `archive relocate U <root_path> --resume`" (exit 4). An interrupted attempt is also recorded in the history, as an interrupted verification is (R-104, §A6): in one write transaction, made when the database allows (as the stop mark), an `INCOMPLETE` FULL verification of revision N, the one it compared the new carrier with (N+1 does not exist yet, and may never), with the items it found and the new carrier's media evidence. Its `run_id` is its own, new for each attempt; `started` is when this attempt started, `finished` when it stopped; its counts and `coverage_bytes` are what its comparison counted, the entries matched by the attempts before included, as a resumed verification counts them; `counts_json.relocation` names the relocation's `run_id`. It is appended to the hash chain as every run is (R-18, R-90). An attempt interrupted after it found an entry that differs is recorded so too, though it ends the relocation (above); an attempt whose comparison read everything and found the carrier differing records no run (no `FAILED` one: the rejected alternatives). An attempt cancelled before it read anything (while its record waits for the write lock at the start) changed nothing and records nothing; a failure or a refusal records no run either. A failure keeps its cause and its exit code (11, as a registration's failures do), and says the same way on: "cannot record the relocation: C; the relocation keeps its saved progress: continue it with `archive relocate U <root_path> --resume`", and so after "cannot scan the new root: C" and "cannot write the manifest: C". The refusal now says both ways on: "an unfinished registration of archive U (root "R") is creating revision N; this relocation recorded nothing (finish that registration with `register --resume`, then relocate again; or abandon it with `archive abandon-registration U`, then continue this relocation with `archive relocate U <root_path> --resume`)" (exit 12). A registration that finishes takes N+1, which ends the relocation (above).

6. **Abandoning: `archive abandon-relocation <uuid> [--confirm] [--json]`**, a command of its own, and **Abandon...** on the row of a stopped relocation in the Archive List. In one write transaction it removes the relocation's record and its checkpoints, and nothing else: no archive, revision, index, manifest, media evidence or history (R-18), nothing on the media (R-03). It follows decision 0025's rules: refused (exit 12) while its attempt's process runs on this computer ("the relocation of archive U is still running in process N (P) on this computer; abandon it once that process has ended"), or, where no process can be checked, while its record said less than 3 minutes ago that it works ("an attempt of the relocation of archive U said it was working less than 3 minutes ago; abandon it once it has stopped"); the GUI names the relocation it showed (its `run_id`), and one that is another by now is refused ("the unfinished relocation of archive U is no longer the one shown ..."). Without `--confirm` the CLI shows what abandoning removes (the archive, the revision it verifies, the new root, when it started, the files and bytes matched) and exits 12; with `--json` it prints that as `relocation`. With `--confirm` it exits 0, and `--json` prints the relocation abandoned. An attempt of a relocation abandoned where this computer cannot see it stops at its next checkpoint or at its record: "the unfinished relocation of archive U was abandoned while this attempt ran; nothing was changed" (exit 12).

   `archive abandon-registration` does not cover it:
   - A relocation is not a registration (the glossary: relocation's _Avoid_ is re-registration): it reads a copy and records the hashes of rN, it never takes a baseline.
   - An archive can have both at once: an unfinished `register --new-revision` of N+1 and an unfinished relocation against N, the case of decisions 0023 and 0025. One command would have to guess which the operator means, and the wrong guess throws hours of reading away.
   - What they remove differs: a registration's plan, its checkpoints in `files`, and with a first registration the archive's record (decision 0025); a relocation's record and its checkpoints in `verify_progress`, never the archive, which has revision N.

7. **Where it shows.**
   - **The Archive List** (decision 0022) shows an unfinished relocation as an operation of its own kind, "Relocation": the archive's label, the new root, its progress from its checkpoints (each entry's size from the index of rN, as a verification's) against its record's totals, and its state, which its record and its process tell as a registration's plan does (decision 0024): `running`, `no progress reported for N minutes; its process is still running`, `stopped; it can be resumed`, or, while the archive is at another revision otherwise than below, `stopped; it cannot be resumed (the archive has a newer revision)`. One whose record says `differs` reads, once its attempt has stopped, `new carrier differs; abandon it`, a state of its own, as it is never resumed (while its attempt still runs, listing the entries that differ, it reads as any attempt that runs); one made for an older revision, whose archive has a later revision than N while N+1 is not recorded, reads `archive has a later revision; abandon it` the same way (decision 0030, point 7). Its tooltip names the process. The note above the table says "Registrations, verifications and relocations that have not finished, ...". A stopped one has **Abandon...**, which reads what abandoning removes with the CLI's check (`abandonable_relocation`) and then asks, with **Cancel** as the default: "Abandon the unfinished relocation of ARCH-0007? ... Its saved progress will be lost: N files (S) matched so far. It cannot be resumed afterwards. The archive, its revisions, manifests and the verification history are not changed."
   - **`archive list --json` and `archive show <uuid> --json`** give an archive whose relocation is unfinished the member `unfinished_relocation`: `root`, `revision` (the one it verifies), `run_id`, `started`, `updated`, `stopped` (null while it may run), `differs` (null unless the record says it), `state`, `resumable` (false also where the record says `differs`), `files_done` and `bytes_done` (null where the checkpoints are not counted, decision 0022), `files_total`, `bytes_total`, and `process` (`id`, `program`, `host`) when the record names one. `state` is judged from this one read as the Archive List judges its first: `running`, `held_up` (its process runs, its record has said nothing for minutes), `stopped`, `differs` (stopped, and its new carrier differs), `later_revision` (stopped, and made for an older revision, decision 0030, also for a registration's `unfinished_registration`); for a record that says nothing of its state, `waiting`, `quiet` or `unknown`. `archive relocate --json` says whether it was `resumed`.
   - **`archive list` and `archive show <uuid>`** without `--json` show, under the archive, one line for its unfinished registration and one for its unfinished relocation, from the one read that `--json` makes and in the Archive List's words: "Unfinished registration of revision N from "R": P; S" (N the revision it creates) and "Unfinished relocation of revision N to "R": P; S" (N the revision it verifies), with P what it has done against its totals ("3.00 GiB of 8.00 GiB (37%) · 1200 of 3000 files", or `not counted`) and S the state that `--json` gives, worded (`running`, `stopped; it can be resumed`, `new carrier differs; abandon it`, ...); no speed or time left, which one read cannot tell. To match it, `--json` gives an archive whose registration is unfinished the member `unfinished_registration`: `root`, `revision` (the one it creates), `started` (when its plan was made), `updated`, `stopped`, `state`, `files_done` and `bytes_done` (null where the checkpoints are not counted), `files_total` and `bytes_total` (null until its scan has counted them), and `process` when its plan names one.
   - **The history** (`history <uuid>` and the GUI's Verification History) marks the runs of a relocation, from what the relocation wrote into their counts (`counts_json`): the `INCOMPLETE` run of an interrupted attempt reads "interrupted relocation attempt", as it read the new carrier and claims nothing about the archive's carrier, and the FULL verification that recorded r(N+1) reads "relocation from rN". The CLI writes the mark after the run's id; the GUI writes it in a column "Note", shown only for an archive that has such runs, so the history of an archive never relocated looks as before. `history --json` gives each run `relocation`: null, or the relocation's `run_id`, `from_revision` (N) and `interrupted_attempt`. The FULL verification of a relocation recorded by a version before this decision names N in its counts too, and is marked the same way.

8. **`repo rebuild-index` keeps saved progress.** It rebuilt the index by emptying `files` and `archive_revisions` and inserting what the manifests define (R-80). The rows that an unfinished registration saved under the revision it creates went too, though they are no index and no manifest holds them. It now rebuilds only what the manifests define: in its one transaction it reads the registrations' plans, and keeps the rows under the revision each creates while its plan exists and no manifest it reads defines that revision. Every other row of `files` goes and is rebuilt from the manifests, as before; a plan whose revision a manifest defines is done with (decision 0023), and its rows are that manifest's index. The saved progress of verifications and relocations is in `verify_progress`, which rebuild-index never touched; their progress counts each entry's size from the index of the revision they verify, which it rebuilds. `repo rebuild-index` says how many registrations kept their saved progress (`saved_progress_kept` with `--json`).

## Why

Relocation exists so that an archive stays verifiable when its carrier goes away (decision 0004, §A1); the new carrier is read in full, which for a whole disk takes many hours. A relocation that starts over after every interruption, a USB disk that drops off or a computer that restarts, is the relocation that never finishes. Verification and registration have resumed since M4 (R-104); relocation, the same FULL read, now does too.

The record follows a registration's plan, which the Archive List, decisions 0024 and 0025 already read, judge and abandon: one per archive, under the archive's uuid, so the operator finishes or gives up the relocation by naming the archive, and saved progress always belongs to one relocation. Its checkpoints follow a verification's: the relocation's FULL verification of the new carrier is what they record.

A checkpoint says that this entry, in this folder of this volume, matched when it was read. R-104 trusts it only while that still holds: the archive at the same revision, the same identity. A disk formatted or replaced since, another disk, or another folder says nothing of what matched before, so the resume compares the volume with the evidence recorded at the start, by the rules that identify archives (§B10), and the folder on it. `MIGRATED` (the same filesystem in another enclosure since the start) is refused as well: the relocation records the carrier it reads, and its checkpoints were made on another; starting again settles it. The path is left out, as drive letters and mount points change between attaches.

A copy that differs ends the relocation for the same reason: the operator's next step is to change the carrier, and only a full read proves what it then holds. Resuming would accept a damaged second copy of a file that matched in the first. So its record goes also while another operation holds the write lock for longer than a statement waits for it (`repo rebuild-index`, the record of a large archive), and where it still cannot go, it says `differs`, so that no resume and no new relocation take it for one that can go on, and the Archive List says to abandon it. The mark is written when the first entry that differs is found, usually long before the reading ends, and again with each later write of the attempt: it needs the write lock at many moments, not only at the one when the removal could not get it, and it is there also when the attempt is killed before its end. An attempt interrupted after it found such an entry ends the relocation too: a resume would read that entry again and end the same way, unless the operator had copied it again meanwhile, and then it would accept the second copy without reading again what matched in the first. An entry that could not be read counts as one that differs only once the comparison has read everything. A USB disk that drops off, the interruption this decision is for, usually fails the read in flight with an I/O error before the next open finds the device removed: taken for a difference at once, that read would end the relocation and throw its saved progress away for a loose cable, and a process killed after it would leave a record that no resume takes. Until everything is read, a read that failed says nothing of what the disk holds, and a resume reads that entry again in any case, as it skips only entries that matched; a comparison that read everything and still could not read an entry has found a carrier that does not hold the content.

An interrupted attempt read the new carrier, as an interrupted verification read its carrier, and R-104 records such a run `INCOMPLETE`: the history then shows every attempt that was interrupted, and what the relocation had matched when it stopped. That includes an attempt that ends the relocation because it found an entry that differs before it was interrupted: its `INCOMPLETE` run makes no claim, so it says nothing against the archive, whose carrier is still the old one and was not read, where a `FAILED` run would (the rejected alternatives). Its runs are steps of one relocation, not runs to add up: each `INCOMPLETE` run counts what had matched by its stop, as a resumed verification counts what the run before it matched, and the FULL verification that records r(N+1) counts all of it. That FULL verification keeps the relocation's start: its result rests on what every attempt matched, also the attempts that left no run (a process killed, a failure), and the `INCOMPLETE` runs within its time say when each interrupted attempt read. The chain orders runs as they are recorded (R-90), not by `started`, so a FULL verification that started before the runs recorded ahead of it breaks nothing.

Whatever records N+1 makes a relocation against N impossible (R-15 never lets the archive go back to N). Removing its record then, in the same transaction, is decision 0023's rule for plans: otherwise the record and its checkpoints would stay, listed as a relocation that cannot be resumed, until the operator abandoned it or the archive's next relocation began.

`archive list` and `archive show` are where the command line shows an archive's state (R-21). An archive under registration or relocation for hours showed nothing of it there, and `--json` showed only the relocation: one line each, worded as the Archive List's row and judged as `--json` judges it, says it where the operator looks, and the two outputs say the same.

R-80 makes the index rebuildable from the manifests. Saved progress is no index: no manifest holds it, and only the repository keeps it. Emptying it with the index cost a re-read of everything an unfinished registration had hashed, hours for a whole disk, while `repo rebuild-index` is the remedy for a damaged index, which an unfinished registration does not cause.

Rejected alternatives:

- A verification's record (`run:<run_id>`) for the relocation: `verify --resume` would take it over, and decision 0022 hides a run that a later verification of the archive supersedes, and one whose archive has another revision once it has said nothing for 5 minutes.
- Checkpoints in `files` under N+1, as a registration's: a relocation leaves N+1 to a registration that has saved files there (decision 0023) and would take its own for one; the two would mix; and `repo rebuild-index` rebuilds `files` from the manifests (point 8 keeps only a registration's saved progress there).
- Keeping in `repo rebuild-index` every row of `files` that no manifest defines: rows left by a crash of an older version, or by a manifest that can no longer be read, would stay as if they were index; only the rows a plan names are saved progress.
- `archive abandon-registration` for relocations as well (point 6).
- Resuming after a copy that differed, so that a repaired copy is read only where it failed: the rest of the carrier may have changed with the repair, and a relocation is accepted only when every entry matches (decision 0004).
- Marking the record of a relocation whose copy differed only when its removal fails: the mark would need the write lock at the moment the removal could not get it, and a process killed before its end would leave no mark at all.
- Counting an entry that could not be read as one that differs as soon as it is found, as this decision first did: the read in flight when a disk drops off fails before the next open finds the device removed, so the relocation would end, and its saved progress go, for a loose cable, and a process killed after that read would leave a record saying `differs`, which no resume takes.
- Stopping the reading at the first entry that differs: the operator copies again from the list of every entry that differs; a relocation that stopped at the first would need one more copy and one more full read for each damaged file.
- Letting a new relocation, or a resume, remove a record that says `differs` and begin: the operator would not see that the relocation was dropped, possibly one to another disk; abandoning stays the operator's step (point 6).
- Comparing the root path at a resume: a drive letter that changed would refuse it for nothing; the volume's identity and the folder on it are what the checkpoints depend on.
- Exit 4 and the interruption's words for a failure that stops a relocation: its cause, which the operator deals with before a resume can finish (a folder that cannot be listed, a repository that cannot be written), would not show, and a registration's failures exit 11 as well.
- Recording nothing in the history until r(N+1) is recorded, as this decision first did: an interrupted attempt read the new carrier as an interrupted verification read its carrier, which R-104 records `INCOMPLETE`, and the history would show neither the attempts nor when they read.
- A FULL verification that starts when its own attempt started, as a resumed verification's does: an attempt whose process was killed, or that a failure stopped, records no run, so what it matched would lie in no run's time, though the result rests on it.
- The checkpoints of each attempt under a `run_id` of its own, moved at each resume as `verify --resume` moves a run's, so that every run, the FULL verification too, is one attempt: the record's `run_id` would change at every resume, so that an attempt of the same relocation on another computer would find its record gone and stop as if abandoned, and **Abandon...**, which names the relocation by its `run_id`, would refuse after every resume; moving millions of checkpoints would hold the write lock at every resume as well.
- Recording a `FAILED` verification for a relocation that a failure stopped, or whose new carrier differs: "Last verify" would say FAILED for the archive, whose carrier is still the old one, which was not read.
- Letting a new relocation take over a stopped one's record without `--resume`: the operator would not see that an earlier relocation, possibly to another disk, is dropped with its saved progress.

## Consequences

- Amended by decision 0022 (2026-10-27, point 9): the way on of an interrupted or stopped relocation, the refusal while a relocation of the archive is unfinished, and `verify --resume` of the run of an interrupted attempt name the relocation's new root as it can be typed (`archive relocate U "L:\Photos 2026" --resume`), where they said `<root_path>`; a relocation that a registration of the archive holds up names that registration's root too (`register R --resume`). Closing the console window, signing out and shutting down interrupt a relocation as Ctrl+C does (point 8 there).

- Amended by decision 0030: an import that records revision N+1 also removes, in its transaction, the record and saved progress of a relocation that creates N+1, as a registration does (point 5). A relocation made for an older revision, whose archive has a later revision than N while N+1 is not recorded (an import of N+2), never records (decision 0030, point 6): its checkpoints and its record stop it, and its resume and a relocation without `--resume` are refused in words that name abandoning only (points 2 to 5); an attempt that is interrupted, or that a failure stops, after such an import ends in those words too, recording no run (point 5), and `verify --resume` of the `INCOMPLETE` run of an earlier attempt names the abandon instead of the resume (below); a relocation that is not a resume writes its record only while the archive is still at the revision it read (point 4); once stopped it reads `archive has a later revision; abandon it`, with the state code `later_revision` (point 7); the tests are decision 0030's. And a relocation's record removes the rows under N+1 that no plan names, as an import's does (point 5), where it refused over them (exit 12), naming an unfinished registration with an empty root that nothing could finish or abandon.
- Tests of those rows: a relocation whose revision holds rows that no plan names (one of a file of the manifest with other hashes, one of a file it does not hold), which records the revision with its manifest's index and verifies, leaving the plan of another revision. A mutation check removed their removal, and the plan's hold on the revision (`a_relocation_leaves_a_registration_its_saved_files`), in turn, and each removal failed the test meant for it.
- Amended by decision 0028: `repo rebuild-index` keeps the record of the archive's revision whose manifest it cannot read with its rows of `files`, which are then that record's index (point 8; the rejected alternative on rows that no manifest defines means rows that no record kept names either), and keeps the archive at that revision: an archive goes back below the revision N that an unfinished relocation verifies only where neither the manifest of N nor its record nor a verification of N that read the media is left (point 3).
- Amended by decision 0032: a relocation compares by chunk, as a FULL verify does, and accepts what a FULL verify accepts (operator, 2026-10-17). A file whose chunks all match while a file hash recorded does not, or whose content matches its file hashes while a chunk hash recorded does not, is `Verified` with the warning a FULL verify gives, which makes the relocation and the FULL verification it records `PASSED_WITH_WARNINGS` (point 5), and is carried with rN's hashes (decision 0004). Its checkpoints leave such a file out, as a FULL verify's do (point 2), so that a resume reads it again and warns again (point 3). The refusal of a new carrier that differs names the chunks of a file that differ, as a FULL verify names them. `archive relocate` prints the warnings, and `--json` gives them as `warnings`.
- Amended by decision 0046: a relocation runs in the window, where the window had none: Relocate to a new carrier... in an archive's details starts it as a job, and a stopped one is resumed with Resume on its row under Unfinished operations, from the details, or in the relocation dialog at another root, exactly as `archive relocate <uuid> <root> --resume` (`app::relocation_resume`); where another operation refuses it, the window names its own ways on. A relocation that finds its archive retired when it records records nothing, and a stopped one of an archive retired since reads `archive is retired; abandon it`. History shows a relocation's marks among the notes of each of its rows, where the Verification History had a column Note only for an archive with such runs.
- Amended by decision 0033: a relocation without `--resume` is also refused while a FULL verify that adds chunk hashes to the archive is unfinished, in that verification's words; such a verification is refused while a relocation of the archive is unfinished, in point 4's words. `archive list` and `archive show` show such a verification under the archive as they show a relocation (point 7), and their `--json` gives it as `unfinished_verification`. Verify Archive, refused while a relocation of the archive is unfinished, words the refusal in its own terms: a relocation has no window, so the command line continues it (`archive relocate U <root_path> --resume`), and Abandon... in the Archive List gives it up; one whose new carrier differs is only abandoned; while one read finds its attempt running or held up, as the Archive List judges it (point 7), the window says so and to wait for it, and a record that says nothing of its state, which one read cannot judge, is not taken for running.
- No change to the database schema, the manifests or the golden test: `settings` gains `relocation:<archive_uuid>` records, and `verify_progress` holds relocations' checkpoints beside verifications'.
- Core: `RelocateRequest::resume`, `RelocateServices::processes` (the composition root passes `platform::make_process_table()`), `RelocateOutcome::resumed`; `ArchiveStore::unfinished_relocations`, `update_relocation`, `relocation_recorded`, `remove_relocation`, `remove_relocation_creating`, `progress_count`, `verify_run` (a run by its id); `Heartbeat::only_while_relocating`; `abandonable_relocation` and `abandon_relocation`; a checkpoint of `compare_with_manifest` returns whether the comparison goes on, and `ComparisonInput::on_item` tells the relocation each entry that is not `Verified` as it is classified; `UnfinishedRelocation::differs`; `relocation_part`, the part a recorded run plays in a relocation, which `verify --resume` and the history read. - The FULL verification a resumed relocation records spans its attempts: `started` is the relocation's, and the entries matched before a resume were read then, not at `finished`; the `INCOMPLETE` runs of its interrupted attempts lie within its time, and are recorded before it.
- An `INCOMPLETE` run of a relocation is a verification of the archive recorded after any verification before it: the Archive List no longer lists a stopped verification recorded before it (decision 0022, point 5), which `verify --resume` still continues where R-104 allows. It changes neither "Last FULL verify", "Last verify" nor OVERDUE (an `INCOMPLETE` run makes no claim, §A6), and `verify --resume` refuses its run: a relocation continues with `archive relocate --resume` alone.
