# The Archive List shows the registrations and verifications that have not finished

Registering an archive that is a whole volume reads every byte on it and takes many hours, and so does its FULL verification. The operator often starts them from the command line, which shows their progress only in its own window, and the GUI showed nothing of them: an archive whose first registration is under way appeared as `REGISTRATION_INCOMPLETE` with revision 0, and a verification not at all until it ended. The repository already holds what they have done: both checkpoint into it (R-104) and keep a record in `settings` so they can be resumed.

## Decision

1. **Section.** Above the archives, the Archive List view (§B17) shows the registrations and verifications that have not finished, wherever they were started (this window, another one, the command line): label, root, operation, progress and state, one row per operation. The section is hidden when there are none. It scrolls when it holds more than about three rows, so it never pushes the archives out of the window. The operations that run, or may, come first, then those that stopped; the newest first within each. Each read also takes a mark of the archives that the table shows (`ArchiveStore::archive_list_mark`: each archive's label, root, revision and status, and the count and newest row of the revisions and of the verifications recorded), in the same read transaction as the operations. When it differs from the mark taken when the table was drawn, the archives are read again as well. So the table follows every operation that ends or begins, also one that did both between two reads (a small folder registered from the command line, a verification of seconds), which the section never shows.
2. **Reading.** While the view is shown, every 5 seconds, on a thread of its own, through a read-only connection: watching never creates a database (a missing one is an error) and never writes to one. A read that took long spaces out the next ones, so reading keeps the database busy at most a tenth of the time. In WAL mode (R-91) one read transaction gives a consistent picture. In the rollback-journal mode, which a database keeps where WAL cannot be turned on, a reader's lock keeps the operations from committing for as long as it is held, and they wait for it at most 5 seconds: there each query reads on its own, and the checkpoints are not counted at all, because one count holds the lock for its whole length, and that of an operation with millions of checkpoints can take longer. Such a row says `not counted` instead of its progress and shows the state its record gives; one of an older version, whose record says nothing of its state, `not known`. A verification recorded `INCOMPLETE` never changes, so its checkpoints are counted once.
3. **Progress.** Done is what the checkpoints hold (counted in WAL mode, see 2): for a registration the files indexed under the revision being created, for a verification the entries in `verify_progress`, and their bytes. The totals come from the operation's record (below). Until a registration's scan has counted them, the total of a registration whose root is a whole volume is the space in use on that volume, shown as "of about"; for records written before this decision a verification's totals are its manifest header's counts. The share counts bytes when their total is known, otherwise files. It stays below 100 % until every file is done, and an approximate total never shows 100 %. Speed and time left come from the steps of the counts (app::ProgressEstimate); they are shown only while the counts moved in the last 3 minutes. So that a stop is not taken for slow work, the estimate starts afresh at a step when, since the step before, the operation was seen stopped or this window itself was held up, its 5-second timer ticking over a minute after the tick before (the computer slept, and the operations on it with it; where the clock stops while the computer sleeps, as on Linux, the steps' gaps leave the sleep out anyway), or when the gap since the step before is long for the operation's own pace (at least 10 minutes) and brought little work for its length (less than a quarter of the bytes that the fastest of its last few steps would read in that time). A stop, a sleep or the scan of a resumed registration brings almost nothing; a large file brings its bytes, so a video read in 11 minutes between photos checkpointed 40 seconds apart is work, not a stop. A stop in the middle of a large file brings that file's bytes too: one that this window cannot tell, such as an older version's process killed and resumed (it writes no `updated`) or a sleep while the list was not shown, passes for work when it lasted less than about three times that file's read, and the time left then reads too long, up to about four times, until the next steps replace it. The fastest step, not the estimate's speed, is the measure: a stop only ever makes a step slower, and so does the last checkpoint of a cancelled attempt, written early without the large file it was reading, which would pull the estimate's speed down until a resume two hours later, reading that file again, passed for work. The pace is learned from every gap between steps but one in which the operation was seen stopped or this window was held up, and the estimate counts at least the last three steps however far apart they come (one large file each), also right after one step much later than the others, so steps minutes apart, and small files between large ones, still give a speed and a time left.
4. **State**, worded so that it claims no more than the repository shows:

   | State | When |
   |---|---|
   | `waiting for progress...` | no `updated`; first seen, and not yet seen to move |
   | `running` | its record's `updated` is at most 3 minutes old; without `updated`, its counts moved in the last 3 minutes. A registration whose plan says it stopped, but whose counts moved after this window first saw that, counts as one without `updated`: an attempt that does not write the plan took it up (see the consequences) |
   | `no progress seen for N minutes` (`hours`) `; it may have stopped` | no `updated`, and the counts have not moved for 3 minutes or more since this window first looked |
   | `not known` | its checkpoints are not counted (see 2), and no `updated` |
   | `stopped; it can be resumed` | a verification recorded `INCOMPLETE`, a registration whose plan says it stopped (`stopped`) and whose counts have not moved since this window first saw that, or its record's `updated` is more than 3 minutes old |
   | `stopped; it cannot be resumed (the archive has a newer revision)` | a verification never recorded whose record's `updated` is more than 3 minutes old, and whose archive has another revision now (R-104); once that `updated` is 5 minutes old it is no longer listed, nor is one recorded `INCOMPLETE` (see 5) |

5. **Which verifications are listed.** Every interrupted verification keeps its record for `verify --resume`. One is not listed when it cannot be resumed (R-104), a later verification of the same archive supersedes it, or its archive is retired with its latest manifest lost:
   - its archive has another revision now (R-104 refuses the resume), and it was recorded `INCOMPLETE` or, never recorded, its record's `updated` is 5 minutes old (its process was killed);
   - a later verification of the same archive supersedes it: one recorded after it, or one started after it was recorded, or, for a run that was never recorded (its process was killed), one that started, worked (its `updated`) or was recorded when the run's record had said nothing for 5 minutes, or, once the run's record has said nothing for 5 minutes, one that started after the run's last `updated` and was recorded `PASSED` or `PASSED_WITH_WARNINGS` (a restart that passed within minutes of the kill, as one of a small archive does; one that `FAILED` at once, on the wrong media say, did not do the run's work, and one that started before the run last said anything ran beside it). A run that works writes its `updated` every 30 seconds, so it is never left out this way. A run of an older version never says it works; for it only a verification started 5 minutes or more after it counts.
   - its archive is retired and its latest manifest is lost (decision 0028, point 9; operator, 2026-10-20, narrowing the operator's word of 2026-10-17, which left out every retired archive's), as where the archive was retired because its latest manifest and its device were lost: a resume of it can only fail on that manifest, which nothing puts back, so it can never be resumed, however its run stopped (recorded `INCOMPLETE`, killed, or of an older version) and whether it stopped before or after the archive was retired. The manifest is judged as the archive lists judge it (decision 0028, points 5 and 9: line 1 alone, or the whole manifest where the last `repo rebuild-index` skipped it, and the whole manifest behind an index that seems to be all that is wrong), so that an archive shown with "Manifest lost: " has no unfinished verification listed; put back, the manifest is no longer lost, and the run is listed again. A run that still works on such an archive, having read its manifest before it was lost, is left out as well; it goes on, and records its result as any run. A retired archive whose latest manifest is not lost can still be verified, by its folder or volume, by its uuid and in Verify Archive (decision 0003), and its unfinished verifications are listed, and resumed from the list, as any archive's.

   The history keeps every run (R-18), and a run left out keeps its record and its checkpoints as they are; `verify --resume` of a run not listed still works where R-104 allows it.
6. **The records** in `settings` (§B8) are JSON objects. Members are added, none is removed or changes its meaning, and a reader ignores members it does not know:

   | Record | Members before | Added |
   |---|---|---|
   | `registration:<archive_uuid>` (the plan) | `archive_uuid`, `revision`, `label`, `created`, `new_archive`, `root`, `copy_of` | `files_total`, `bytes_total`: after the scan, every readable file it found, those an interrupted attempt hashed included (they count as done; that attempt's checkpoints the scan does not reuse, of files gone or changed since, are deleted first, as they would count work never done or done again); `updated`; `stopped`: when an attempt ended without finishing (cancelled, its device removed, a failure), written last by that attempt, never after the registration is recorded (the record removes the plan, and a plan under the key by then is the archive's next registration's); the attempt that `register --resume` starts writes the plan without it |
   | `run:<run_id>` | `archive_uuid`, `revision`, `started` | `root`: where the run reads (UTF-8); `files_total`, `bytes_total`: the files and streams of the revision whose content has hashes, which are what the checkpoints count (an entry unreadable at registration has none, decision 0010); `updated` |

   `updated` is a UTC timestamp in the form of the manifest's `created` (`2026-10-15T08:30:00Z`): when the operation last worked; `stopped` has the same form. The engines write it when the record is made or taken up again by a resume (a registration's plan in the very transaction that makes it or takes it over, decision 0024, point 3), when the totals are known, and while they work at least every 60 seconds: every 30 seconds, with the progress reports, which come often enough (scanning reports at least once a second while it finds entries, hashing about five times a second, also while one large file is read). A write is one small `UPDATE` of the record and best effort: one that fails is left out, and the operation goes on. It only changes a record that exists, so it cannot bring back the record of an operation that has finished.
7. **Resume** (operator, 2026-10-16). The row of a verification that reads `stopped; it can be resumed` has **Resume**, and so has the row of a registration that reads it, beside its **Abandon...** (decision 0025), wherever the operation was started: in this window, another one, or on the command line, also before VeriArchive was closed. It continues the operation exactly as its command-line resume does, as the window's one operation, and brings into view the view where an operation of its kind started in the window shows its progress and its report, with **Cancel**:
   - A verification as `verify --full <archive_uuid> --resume <run_id>` does (`app::verification_resume`): its archive named by its uuid, and so looked for among the attached volumes, never at the root the run read before; and its run, whose verified entries are not read again (R-104). In Verify Archive, whose report names the run it continued ("Resumed from run: R"), and whose **Resume interrupted run** then offers the run that this one leaves interrupted, as after a verification started there.
   - **Cloned media.** A resume by the archive's uuid, from the list and on the command line alike, looks at every attached volume. Where more than one holds the archive (its identity matches, `MATCH` or `MATCH_WEAK`, and its root folder is there), as a device and a sector clone of it attached at once, it stops before it reads anything, as `AMBIGUOUS` does (an operator decision, exit 12): "AMBIGUOUS: archive U is on more than one attached volume (cloned media): "R1", "R2"; run X read "R": resume it at the root on the volume it read with `verify --full <root_path> --resume X`, or detach the other volumes and resume it again", R being the root the run's record names (a record of an older version names none, and the message then leaves it out: "...; resume run X at the root on the volume it read with ..."). It changes nothing, so the run stays stopped and resumable, by its root or, once the other volumes are detached, by its uuid. A verification by the archive's uuid that is not a resume, `verify --full <archive_uuid>` and Verify Archive (which verifies an archive chosen in "Verify:" by its uuid), stops the same way (operator, 2026-10-17): it looks at every attached volume too, and where more than one holds the archive, it never picks one by itself. It reads nothing, records nothing (no run, no media evidence), and says "AMBIGUOUS: archive U is on more than one attached volume (cloned media): "R1", "R2"; verify it at the root on one of them with `verify --full <root_path>`, or detach the other volumes and verify it again" (exit 12). Verify Archive says it in words of its own, with each root on a line of its own: "Not verified: archive L (U8) is on more than one attached volume, as a device and a sector-by-sector clone of it would be: ... VeriArchive cannot tell which one to verify. Type the folder of the one to verify above, or choose it with "Choose...", choose "The folder or volume above" under "Verify:" and press "Verify (FULL)"; or detach the other volumes and verify the archive again." (U8 the first 8 characters of the uuid). The engine gives the roots apart from its message (`VerifyStop::roots_found`), so that the view words them so. Where a device and a clone of it are registered as two archives (decision 0005) and attached together, a verification by the root on either matches both archives (`AMBIGUOUS`, §B10), and its message, which said only to select one with `verify --full <archive_uuid>`, sent the operator to a verification by uuid that stops as above, whose message sends the operator back to the root: it now says, too, to detach the other volumes first, "AMBIGUOUS: the media matches several archives (cloned media); select one with `verify --full <archive_uuid>`; where the volumes of those archives are attached together, detach all but the volume of the one to verify, then verify it".
   - A registration as `register <root> --resume` does (`app::registration_resume`): the root its plan names, made as the command line makes a root typed there (`app::typed_root`), and checked before anything of it is read as that command checks it (`app::root_not_taken`: a refused root, decision 0029, and one that is no folder, "not a directory: R", the root named as the plan names it). In Register Archive.

   Resume has no options of its own, and takes none from the check boxes of Verify Archive and Register Archive, which are not on the Archive List, where it is clicked: it continues the operation exactly as the command-line resume given no option does. Its whole request is made in `app` (`verification_resume`, `registration_resume`), and the GUI runs it as it is. So a registration writes no media copy (no `--write-media-copy`: the media is written only where the operator asks for it, R-03), and a verification fails metadata differences only where the policy says so (`policy.strict_metadata`, as without `--strict-metadata`). A media copy for a registration being resumed is asked for in Register Archive, whose resume box and media copy box are then both in view. A label and the options of a new registration take no part in a resume, as on the command line. What the command-line resume refuses or fails on, Resume refuses or fails on in the same words, in that view's report: an archive on no attached volume, a media identity or revision that changed (R-104), a manifest that cannot be used (decision 0028), an attempt whose process still runs (decision 0024), a registration made for an older revision by now (decision 0030).

   It is not offered where the operation cannot be resumed, or may still work, but on an older version's registration that may have stopped (below): a verification that reads `stopped; it cannot be resumed (the archive has a newer revision)`, a registration that reads `archive has a later revision; abandon it` (decision 0030), every operation that reads `running`, `waiting for progress...`, `not known` or `no progress reported ...; its process is still running` (decision 0024), and a verification that reads `no progress seen ...; it may have stopped`. While the window runs another operation, it starts nothing and says "Another operation is running.". A relocation has no Resume (see the rejected alternatives): it is resumed with `archive relocate <uuid> <root_path> --resume`, the root being the new root its row shows.

   **An older version's registration** (operator, 2026-10-17). A registration that reads `no progress seen for N minutes; it may have stopped` has **Resume**, as a stopped registration has, which asks first where its plan names no process (below), and **Abandon...** (decision 0025). It reads so only where its plan never says when it worked (no `updated`: a version before this decision wrote it, or such a version's resume took it up, see the consequences; or, rarely, every write of it by a later version failed, below) and its checkpoints have not moved for 3 minutes since this window first looked, as long as an attempt that says it works may be silent before the list calls it stopped (point 4): nothing it records tells more, and whether the older version still runs it is the operator's to know. Its resume continues it as `register <root> --resume --confirm` does once the operator chose Resume in its question (below), taking up a plan that names no process (decision 0024, point 3), and as `register <root> --resume` does where its plan names a process, which the resume asks nothing of (below). It has no Resume where it can no longer finish, made for an older revision (decision 0030) or over the lost manifest of an archive retired since (decision 0028, point 9): there **Abandon...** alone. A row that reads `not known` (the rollback-journal mode, point 2) offers neither: nothing counted shows it quiet.

   **Where the older version still runs it.** Nothing that the list or the resume reads tells such an attempt from one that has stopped: an older version reads a large file, or scans the whole root, for minutes without a checkpoint. Nothing in such a plan keeps a resume from going ahead, as it names no process (decision 0024, point 3), so a resume of it asks first, in the list and on the command line (below). A version that writes no `updated` comes before decision 0023 as well, and names its manifest before it records it (decision 0023, consequences). Where the older attempt records first, the resume records nothing (decision 0023, point 2); but where the resume records the revision first and the older attempt still runs, that attempt, as it finishes, renames its own manifest over the one that the resume recorded, and its own record then fails on the revision recorded. Its manifest's first line differs from the one recorded (its `created`, at least), so from then on every verification of the archive fails on that manifest ("has changed: its first line differs from what the repository recorded", `ManifestHashMismatch`), and every registration of a new folder or volume and every relocation stops on it (decision 0028, point 4); unless a backup of the repository holds the manifest that the resume recorded, that manifest is lost (decision 0028, point 8). Abandoned under it, the older attempt goes on and may record the revision all the same (decision 0025, consequences). So the guides tell the operator to make sure that no program of the older version still runs the registration before pressing either button, and what happens otherwise.

   **Resume asks first** (operator, 2026-10-20). On such a row, Resume does not resume at once: it asks, with **Cancel** as the default, "Resume the unfinished registration of L?", with the root the row shows, and says what a resume costs where the older version still runs the registration: "An older version of VeriArchive left this registration unfinished. That version does not record when it last worked, so VeriArchive cannot tell whether it has stopped or is still reading a large file or scanning the folder or volume. If it is still running, it will later record its own manifest over the one that this resume records: from then on every verification of the archive fails, and other registrations and relocations stop until the archive gets a new revision. Make sure that the older version's window or command has been closed before you resume." (`app::older_version_resume_warning`; the archive gets that revision in a registration over the manifest so changed, decision 0028, point 8, unless a backup of the repository holds the manifest that the resume recorded, which `import` puts back, point 7). Only **Resume** there resumes, exactly as above; **Cancel**, or closing the question, changes nothing, and the row stays as it was. The row asks so where it offers Resume on a registration that reads `no progress seen ...; it may have stopped` (`OperationRow::resume_asks_first`) and whose plan names no process, as an older version's, and on one that an older version's resume took up after this version stopped it (see the consequences; `OperationWatch::taken_up`): either may be run by that version still. On a quiet registration whose plan names a process, of a version since decision 0024 whose writes of `updated` all failed (below), on another computer or where this computer's process table cannot tell, Resume resumes at once: the resume itself asks nothing of such a plan (below), as its version names its manifest in the transaction that records it. A registration that reads `stopped; it can be resumed` resumes at once, as before, and so does a stopped verification: its plan says that its attempt stopped (its stop mark), names a process that has ended (decision 0024), or has said nothing for more than 3 minutes though its version says every 30 seconds that it works, where its process cannot be judged (another computer, a version before decision 0024, a process table that cannot tell; point 4). The last is not certain to have ended: an attempt on another computer, or one held up by a read from a failing device, may still run. But the versions that write these plans name their manifest in the transaction that records it (decision 0023), so that a resume beside such an attempt costs no manifest: the first to record the revision wins, and the other records nothing. The exceptions are the development builds of 2026-10-15 made before decision 0023 (below), and an older version's resume that took the registration up after this version stopped it, until the list sees its checkpoints move (see the consequences). While the question is shown, another operation may start in the window: Resume in it then starts nothing and says "Another operation is running.".

   **The command line asks for `--confirm`** (operator, 2026-10-20: the command line says the same warning; how it asks is decided here). `register <root> --resume` of a registration whose plan never says when it worked (no `updated`, or one that is no time) and names no process (no `process`, decision 0024), as a version before this decision writes it, stops before it reads anything of the media and changes nothing, in the transaction that would take the registration over (decision 0024, point 3), after the refusals that end the registration or keep it for an attempt that runs: a plan whose revision is recorded goes as before (decision 0023, point 3), without a question, and one made for an older revision (decision 0030) or over the lost manifest of an archive retired since (decision 0028, point 9) is refused as before, naming the abandon, without a question: it can no longer finish, and confirmed it would be refused all the same. It prints what it would take up and the list's warning, then stops (exit 12) with "run the command again with --confirm to resume it":

   ```text
   Unfinished registration of ARCH-0007 (<archive_uuid>), revision 1
     root: <root_path>
     started: 2026-10-14 09:30:00 (UTC+8)
   An older version of VeriArchive left this registration unfinished. ... Make sure that the older version's window or command has been closed before you resume.
   error: run the command again with --confirm to resume it
   ```

   With `--json` it prints that error with the registration as `registration` (`archive_uuid`, `label`, `revision`, `root`, `started`), as `archive abandon-registration` does without `--confirm`. With `--confirm` it resumes, exactly as before. `register` takes `--confirm` with `--resume` as well as with `--lost-manifest` (decision 0028, point 8); with neither it stays a usage error (exit 10, "--confirm is used only with --lost-manifest or --resume"). A resume of a plan that says when it worked, or names a process, needs no confirmation and takes one, as it changes nothing there: this version's plan, which says when it worked from the transaction that makes it (point 6); one that names its process but never says when it worked, as a version since decision 0024 left one where every write of `updated` failed, as while another connection held the write lock past its 5-second wait (those writes were the heartbeat's, which are best effort, until `updated` came with the plan itself on 2026-10-20); and one of a version before decision 0024 or of another computer, whose attempt nothing here can see either. The versions since decision 0023 name their manifest in the transaction that records it (decision 0023), so that the first to record the revision wins and nothing is replaced, and every version that names a process came after it. The development builds of the evening of 2026-10-15, made between this decision and decision 0023, are the exception: their plans say when they worked, but they name their manifest before they record it, so a resume beside one of them that still runs can lose the manifest that it records, unasked; nothing in their plans tells them from a later version's, and decision 0023's advice stands for them. A plan that never says when it worked and names no process is of a version before this decision, or of one between this decision and decision 0024 whose writes of `updated` all failed, which is asked about as well. So the scripts that resume this version's registrations are not affected, and a script may pass `--confirm` to every resume. The confirmation is the resume's own (`RegisterRequest::confirmed`; refused, `RegisterFailure::older_version`), so the window's resumes carry it as well: the Archive List's Resume after its question (above), and a resume that Register Archive starts unconfirmed (its resume box, or the list's Resume where the registration of the row's root is by then another one, which an older version began) shows the warning in its report ("Not registered: An older version of VeriArchive left ...") and asks the list's question; Resume there runs it again, confirmed, and Cancel leaves it as it is. A registration that this version stopped and an older version's resume took up says when this version's attempt worked: the command line, which reads it once, cannot tell that the older version took it up, and resumes it without the question; the list, which sees its checkpoints move under the stop mark, asks (above). Decision 0023's advice stands for it: do not resume a registration with an older version while an attempt of it may still run, in either direction.

## Why

The operator needs to see how far an operation of many hours is, and whether it still works, without the window that started it. The Archive List is where the archives' state is shown (R-21), and an archive under registration is part of it.

The checkpoints are the only progress the repository has, and they move only when a file finishes: a large file or the scan holds them back for minutes. The state therefore says only what can be known. `updated` is the operation's own sign of life; without it (an operation of an older version), quiet minutes say only that it *may* have stopped, counted from when this window looked. A cancelled verification is recorded `INCOMPLETE` and reads as stopped at once; a cancelled registration keeps only its plan, whose `updated` is then still fresh, so the plan says itself that the attempt stopped (`stopped`).

Watching must not change the repository it watches. A read-write connection creates an empty database where the file is missing (moved or being restored), and the last one to close writes the WAL into the database file.

Every cancelled verification keeps its record for a resume, so a list of all of them grows with every Ctrl+C and pushes what runs out of sight. A finished later verification gives the archive a newer result, and a newer attempt is the one to follow.

An archive retired because its latest manifest and its device were lost (decision 0028, point 9) kept a run listed as stopped and resumable for good: a resume of it can only fail on its manifest, which nothing puts back, and only a later verification that read the media, which needs that manifest, supersedes a run (point 5). The section shows what runs and what waits for the operator, and such a run waits for nothing. Retiring an archive says that the operator no longer expects to verify it (decision 0003), not that it cannot be verified: one whose latest manifest can be used is still verified by its folder or volume, by its uuid and in Verify Archive, and an interrupted verification of it can be resumed, so the list keeps it, as any archive's (operator, 2026-10-20). The manifest is judged as the archive lists judge it, so that what the view shows of the archive and of its runs agrees: shown with "Manifest lost: ", it has none listed. Leaving runs out of the list changes nothing of them, as watching writes nothing (point 2).

The totals must count what the done figures count. A manifest header counts the entries unreadable at registration as files, and they are never checkpointed, so a verification never reached its total; the files reused by a resumed registration are in the checkpoints, so they belong in its total, and the checkpoints it does not reuse go, or a folder moved while the device was away would count twice and show 100 % with files left.

A verification started on the command line or in another window, and one that this window lost when VeriArchive was closed (a restart of the computer), read `stopped; it can be resumed`, but the GUI continued only a verification interrupted in the same window, before another one ran there (Verify Archive's **Resume interrupted run**); the guides sent the operator to the command line, with the archive's uuid and the run's id from the row's tooltips. The list holds both, and the root a stopped registration reads, so its row continues the operation (point 7). The command-line resume is the one the engines and the decisions define and the tests prove (R-104; decisions 0024 to 0030): continuing exactly as it does keeps one behaviour for both interfaces, and one meaning for `stopped; it can be resumed`. A verification is continued by its archive's uuid because the root its run read is a path of the moment it ran: the drive letter may name another volume by now, while the identity of the media tells where the archive is, which R-104 requires to match anyway, as long as one attached volume holds the archive. Where more than one does, identity cannot tell which one the run read: a sector clone has the volume's filesystem id, and behind USB bridges that give no serial both read `MATCH_WEAK` (§B10). The run's checkpoints say what matched on the volume it read; continued on another volume, the resume would join the two into one result, a FULL pass for entries never read on the volume it reads. A sample clone whose last three files were damaged, interrupted halfway and continued by its uuid on the device it was cloned from, passed so, while the same run continued at its own root failed on those three files.
