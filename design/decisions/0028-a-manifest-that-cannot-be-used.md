# What is done with a recorded manifest that cannot be used

A revision is recorded with the BLAKE3 of its manifest's line 1 and of its body, and the index keeps a copy of that line 1 (`archive_revisions.header_json`, §B8). Validated manifest bytes are the truth (R-14), so a recorded manifest can be used only while it is there and can be read, its line 1 is the one recorded and its body the one that line names, and the index agrees with that line: its copy of the header is the line, and its record names the archive, revision and body that the line names (CLAUDE.md rule 3, never one in place of the other). A manifest that fails one of these cannot be used: it is gone, it cannot be read, it has changed, or the index says otherwise of it. Until now a verification of its archive stopped (exit 11) and recorded nothing, so the history kept the archive's last result as if nothing were wrong. The operator decided how such a manifest is handled.

`repo rebuild-index` rebuilt the index from the manifests alone (R-80): the record of a revision whose manifest it could not read went with the rest of the index, and the archive was set to the latest revision whose manifest it could read. Where it could read none of an archive's manifests, the archive kept a revision of which the index had no record: it was listed with no volume and no problem, its verification by uuid said "no registered archive", and a verification of its root could find no archive there (`UNKNOWN`, which offers `register`: a second identity for the same media). Where only the latest manifest could not be read, the index took the earlier revision for the archive's current one, with its earlier carrier and entries. The operator decided (2026-10-16) that an archive whose latest manifest the rebuild skipped is handled as one whose manifest cannot be used, also where the manifest of an earlier revision is intact.

## Decision

1. **The first line before parsing.** Wherever the manifest of a recorded revision is read, its line 1 is compared with the BLAKE3 that the repository recorded for it before it is parsed (`ManifestReader::open` given that BLAKE3; `load_recorded_header`). A line 1 that differs is a changed manifest, whatever it holds: also one that no longer parses, one cut off before its newline, the line recorded without its newline (the body gone with it), and an empty file. Where a whole manifest is loaded (verification, `archive compare`, `export`, `export-sha256sums`, relocation), it fails with `ManifestHashMismatch`, as a body that does not match its header does (R-62), never as a malformed manifest or with `RepositoryError`; where line 1 alone is read (identity matching, the archive lists), it has changed in the same words (point 2). A file that cannot be opened or read is no answer either way and stays a failure to read it. An import's source and `repo rebuild-index` have no record to compare line 1 with, and read it as before.

2. **What cannot be used, and the message.** The manifest of a recorded revision, or of the revision that the archive's record says, cannot be used when it is:

   | The manifest | Failure class (§A6) | What is wrong | How to fix it |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | gone | `RepositoryError` | "is missing" | put it back (below) |
   | not readable | `RepositoryError` | "cannot be read: C" (the file system's cause) | put it back |
   | changed: line 1 or its body not the one recorded (point 1, R-62), or other bytes when it is read again for its entries, also bytes that make a line there that does not parse or comes out of canonical order | `ManifestHashMismatch` | "has changed: its first line differs from what the repository recorded" (or "its body does not match its header", "it gave other bytes when it was read again") | put it back |
   | the one recorded (its line 1 has the BLAKE3 recorded for it), but the index says otherwise of it: its copy of the header is not that line, or the record names another body, archive or revision than that line | `RepositoryError` | "the repository's index differs from the manifest ..." | rebuild the index with `repo rebuild-index` |
   | of the archive's revision as the archive's record says it, of which the index has no record (as where `repo rebuild-index` could not read it and no record of it was left, point 6) | `RepositoryError` | "the repository's index has no record of the manifest ...", then what the file at its place (R-63) is, read as `repo rebuild-index` reads a manifest, as no record vouches for it: nothing more where it reads as the manifest of that revision of that archive, ", which is missing", or ", which cannot be read: C" (also "header does not match its file name") | rebuild the index with `repo rebuild-index` where the file reads as the manifest; otherwise put it back, which records it |

   To put a manifest back is to import its file, `import <folder>/r0002.vam`, from where it is kept besides the repository (R-86): a backup of the repository, an export of that revision (R-87), or the media copy in the archive's `.veriarchive` folder, where registration wrote one (R-89). The import puts back the manifest recorded (point 7), and records it where the index has no record of it. The message says so first. Of the archive's latest revision it then names the last resort, where none of these holds the manifest: the archive's next revision, registered from what its root holds now in a registration over the lost manifest (point 8), and after it, where the device that held the archive is gone as well, retiring the archive (point 9); unless the archive is retired, which takes no new revision: its message ends with the import. An earlier revision has a later revision already, and its record serves matching alone, for the earlier carriers (decision 0004), which stops on it (point 4): where none of these holds its manifest, its message names `repo rebuild-index`, which leaves that revision out of the index (point 6), "...; if none of these holds it, leave revision 1 out of the index with `repo rebuild-index`".

   The bytes come before their structure in the read of the entries too, as in line 1 (point 1): a line that does not parse, comes out of canonical order or ends the file without its newline is a malformed manifest only in the body validated. The reader reads on to the end of the body, and where what it read is not that body, the manifest gave other bytes when it was read again (`ManifestReader::next` fails with `BodyMismatch`). This holds for every manifest read (decision 0030): an import of such a source says that the manifest changed while it was imported, and `repo rebuild-index` skips such a manifest as one that changed while it was read.

   Every use of such a manifest says the same plain message: what is wrong, naming the manifest by its revision and its place in the repository, as `archive show` lists it, then how to fix it, as "the manifest of revision 2 (manifests/<archive_uuid>/r0002.vam) is missing; put it back with `import <folder>/r0002.vam` from a backup of the repository, an export of revision 2, or the media copy in the archive's .veriarchive folder, where registration wrote one; if none of these holds it, register revision 3 from what the archive's root holds now with `register <root_path> --lost-manifest <archive_uuid>`; if the device that held the archive is gone as well, retire the archive with `archive retire <archive_uuid> --confirm`", or "the repository's index differs from the manifest of revision 2 (manifests/<archive_uuid>/r0002.vam); rebuild the index with `repo rebuild-index`". Of a manifest that the index has no record of, the first words are "the repository's index has no record of the manifest of revision 2 (manifests/<archive_uuid>/r0002.vam)", followed by ", which is missing" and the same steps, or by "; rebuild the index with `repo rebuild-index`" where the file reads as the manifest. Registration, relocation, `archive compare`, `export` and `export-sha256sums` give it too, after their own words, where a manifest they need cannot be used (`UnusableManifest`, `plain_message`).

   Of a manifest that the index has no record of, identity matching reads line 1 alone, as it reads every manifest, and so do the archive lists until `repo rebuild-index` has skipped the manifest (point 5); a verification reads it whole, as the rebuild reads it: its body against its hash, and every entry to the end of the body (point 3). Where only its body does not match its header, or an entry does not parse, the lists say to rebuild the index until the rebuild has skipped the manifest, and then, reading it whole, what the verification says: that it cannot be read and why, with the steps to put it back, which records it; the rebuild, skipping it again, says why. So a whole read never says to rebuild the index alone where the rebuild would skip the manifest.

3. **A verification that knows its archive records the failure.** An archive named by its uuid (`verify --full <archive_uuid>`), by the run of it that is resumed (`verify --resume <run_id>`), or chosen in the GUI's Verify Archive (which verifies by uuid, also when it resumes) is known before anything is looked for on the media. The manifest of its current revision is checked first, up to its body's hash (`check_recorded_manifest`), and one of which the index has no record to its last entry, as the rebuild reads it (point 2), which until now stopped the verification where the media was looked for, or with "no registered archive", recording nothing. When it cannot be used, the verification FAILS at once:
   - It is recorded as a FULL verification of that archive and revision, `FAILED`, with the failure class of point 2, nothing covered and no items, appended to the history's hash chain as every run is (R-18, R-90). Its counts say that it read no media (`counts_json` holds `"media_read":false`). It keeps no record of its own for a resume, as it read nothing.
   - It reads nothing of the media, and records no media evidence: it observed none (R-96).
   - It says so. The CLI prints "media: not read, as the manifest of this revision cannot be used" in place of the root, the identity, the coverage and the items, and the plain message of point 2 as the manifest's warning; it exits 3, as every `FAILED` verification does. `--json` gives `media_read: false`, with `root` and `identity` null; every verification's `--json` gives `media_read`. The GUI's report says "Media: not read, as the manifest of this revision cannot be used" and the same warning.
   - A run that it was to resume stays as it was, its record and checkpoints kept: once the manifest is restored, `verify --resume` continues it without reading again what it verified (R-104), and the GUI's Verify Archive still offers **Resume interrupted run** for it. The Archive List still lists it with the unfinished operations (decision 0022), stopped and resumable, also after the GUI was closed: a verification that read no media supersedes no unfinished verification of its archive (decision 0022, point 5). It is left out only where the archive is retired with its latest manifest lost (point 9), whose unfinished verifications can never be resumed (decision 0022, point 5). The same holds for a run interrupted before a verification of the archive by its uuid that failed this way, the step that the message by root names (point 4).

   A manifest that can be used is read again with its entries once the media is found, as before. A verification by root finds its archive by matching (point 4). Once matching has found the archive, a manifest whose body does not match, or that gives other bytes when it is read for its entries, fails the run as before: the media was looked at, and its evidence is recorded.

4. **A verification by root stops only where the root could be the archive whose manifest cannot be used, and names it.** A verification by root finds its archive by matching the root's media evidence and folder against every archive's latest carrier (§B10), which only each archive's latest manifest records; a retired archive whose latest manifest is lost is left out (point 9). While one of them cannot be used, that archive's carrier and root are unknown, and the root may be its, unless the root is plainly another archive's (operator, 2026-10-16): matched as a verification by root matches, against the latest carriers of the archives whose manifest can be used, its media evidence is a `MATCH` of one of them at the root's folder (the same root subpath), the filesystem id and a device being the ones that archive's latest manifest records, and no identity file on the media names another archive. Such a root is verified as usual, as that archive's: the archive whose manifest cannot be used cannot be at it (see the reasons). Every other root stops, as before, with exit 11, and records nothing: one that matches no archive at its folder (`UNKNOWN`, or an earlier carrier, `PREVIOUS_CARRIER`), one that matches only weakly (`MATCH_WEAK`: no device, or no filesystem id, to compare), another device (`MIGRATED`), another filesystem on a known device (`MISMATCH`) or several archives (`AMBIGUOUS`), and one whose identity file names another archive than the one matched. To decide, the verification looks up the root's volume and reads its identity file first, as a verification by root does once it has its candidates: a root whose volume cannot be identified stops with that, as where every manifest can be used. Its message says which archive, why, and how to go on, in that order, the steps being those of the plain message (point 2): "this root may belong to archive ARCH-0007 (<archive_uuid>), whose manifest cannot be used: the manifest of revision 2 (manifests/<archive_uuid>/r0002.vam) is missing; verify that archive by its uuid (`verify --full <archive_uuid>`) to record the failure, then put its manifest back with `import <folder>/r0002.vam` from a backup of the repository, an export of revision 2, or the media copy in the archive's .veriarchive folder, where registration wrote one; if none of these holds it, register revision 3 from what the archive's root holds now with `register <root_path> --lost-manifest <archive_uuid>`; if the device that held the archive is gone as well, retire the archive with `archive retire <archive_uuid> --confirm`", so also where the index has no record of the manifest and the file at its place is missing or cannot be read, as the import records it (point 7); or, where only the index differs from the manifest (its copy of the header, or a record that names another body, archive or revision than its line 1), or has no record of a manifest that the file at its place reads as, "..., then rebuild the index with `repo rebuild-index`". The archive named is the first, in the order of the labels, whose latest manifest cannot be used; `archive list` shows every one (point 5). A verification of an archive named by its uuid, or by the run it resumes, is never stopped so: it reads no other archive's manifest, and its own is point 3. A manifest of an earlier revision, which matching reads only where the earlier carriers decide the outcome (decision 0004), stops it with the plain message of point 2 alone: a verification by uuid verifies the latest revision, and would record nothing of an earlier manifest.

   A registration of a new revision, `register <root_path> --new-revision`, goes ahead by the same rule (operator, 2026-10-17): its root, with the identity file on it, matched against the archives whose latest manifest can be used (`plainly_another_archives_root`, which the verification by root asks too), is a `MATCH` of one at its folder, and the registration creates that archive's next revision as where every manifest can be used. Every other registration stops with the plain message of point 2 (exit 11), as before: a new revision of any other root, and every registration of a new folder or volume, also with `--force-new-archive`, as the folder could be the archive whose manifest cannot be used; and so does every relocation, as its new root must overlap no archive's root on its volume (decision 0001), and that archive's root is unknown.

5. **One damaged archive never stops the list.** `archive list` (text and `--json`), `archive show <archive_uuid>` and the GUI's Archive List read each archive's latest header from its manifest (R-14). An archive whose latest manifest cannot be used is listed like every other: its record (label, uuid, revision, status) and its last FULL verification, last verification and OVERDUE, which the repository holds without the manifest. What only the manifest holds (the volume, model and capacity, `copy_of`, and the identity and counts of `archive show --json`) is left out, and the problem stands in its place, in the plain message of point 2, which ends with how to fix it:
   - In text, one more line under the archive: "problem: the manifest of revision 2 (manifests/<archive_uuid>/r0002.vam) is missing; put it back with `import <folder>/r0002.vam` from ...; if none of these holds it, register revision 3 ... `register <root_path> --lost-manifest <archive_uuid>`; if the device that held the archive is gone as well, retire the archive with `archive retire <archive_uuid> --confirm`" (in zh-TW "問題：…"): the last resort, and retiring where the device is gone as well, are named where the problem is shown. A retired archive whose latest manifest is lost has "manifest lost: " in its place (point 9).
   - In `--json`, the member `manifest_problem`: `kind` (`missing`, `unreadable`, `changed`, `index_differs` or `not_indexed`, the last where the index has no record of the manifest, whatever the file at its place is), `revision`, `manifest` (its place in the repository) and `message` (the plain message). Only such an archive has it, as only an archive with an unfinished relocation has `unfinished_relocation`; a retired archive whose latest manifest is lost has `manifest_lost` (true) beside it (point 9).
   - `archive show <archive_uuid>` also reads line 1 of the manifest of each other revision of the archive, which matching reads for the earlier carriers (decision 0004) and which stops it where it cannot be used (point 4). Under such a revision in its list of revisions it prints the problem line ("problem: the manifest of revision 1 (manifests/<archive_uuid>/r0001.vam) is missing; put it back with `import <folder>/r0001.vam` from a backup of the repository, an export of revision 1, or the media copy in the archive's .veriarchive folder, where registration wrote one; if none of these holds it, leave revision 1 out of the index with `repo rebuild-index`"), and `--json` gives that revision `manifest_problem`.
   - The exit code is 2, in text and `--json` alike, for `archive list` while any archive listed has such a manifest, and for `archive show` of such an archive or of one whose earlier revision has such a manifest: the listing worked and is complete, and an archive needs the operator. A retired archive whose latest manifest is lost needs nothing of the operator any more, and leaves the exit code 0 (point 9). `repo rebuild-index` exits 2 likewise after it skipped a manifest that it cannot read, or could not read the manifest of a revision that something else names (point 6).
   - The GUI shows "Problem: " and the plain message under the archive's row, from the Volume column on, in red, in the interface language, the last resort and the retirement included, which are the command line's (points 8 and 9); a retired archive whose latest manifest is lost shows "Manifest lost: " and its message, not in red (point 9). The archive stays one to choose in Verify Archive (its choices read no manifest), and verifying it there records the failure (point 3).

   Only a failure of the database still stops the list. The list reads line 1 alone (point 1), but for an archive whose revision's manifest the last `repo rebuild-index` skipped (point 6), whose manifest it reads as the rebuild reads one: its body against its hash, and every entry. So a manifest whose line 1 is intact but whose body has changed, or one of whose entries does not parse, is listed without a problem until a rebuild skips it, while the verification of its archive fails on it (point 3); once a rebuild has skipped it, it is listed with what is wrong and how to fix it, in the words of point 2, until a rebuild reads it.

6. **`repo rebuild-index` keeps what it cannot rebuild.** It rebuilds the record and the index of every revision from its manifest (R-80), as before. Each archive is at the latest revision that a manifest in its folder names by its file name, whether the rebuild could read it or not (revisions start at 1, so a file named for revision 0 names none), or that the record of a revision whose manifest it cannot read names, where that record names the manifest at its revision's own place (R-63, `manifests/<archive_uuid>/r0002.vam`; one that names another place is the index differing, point 2, rebuilt from the manifests), and never below the latest revision that a verification of the archive which read its media names in the history (R-18), which no rebuild changes: that verification compared the media with the manifest of that revision, which the repository had recorded. A verification that read no media (point 3) names the revision that the archive's record said, and proves nothing of it. Where the rebuild cannot read the manifest of that revision, as it is missing or the rebuild skips it (it cannot be opened or read, does not parse, is another archive's or revision's, its body does not match its header, or an entry does not parse), the revision keeps its record and its index (its rows of `files`) as they were, in the rebuild's transaction (`ArchiveStore::clear_index` leaves them): no manifest can rebuild them, and the record is what the manifest is checked against once it is restored (R-14). The record of an earlier revision whose manifest the rebuild cannot read goes with its index, as every record that no manifest rebuilds, as before this decision: its earlier carrier is then no longer known (decision 0004), until the manifest, put back, is indexed again by the next rebuild. An archive of which none is left keeps its record as it is, but for a later revision that its history names, and an archive without a record is given one only from a manifest read (its label and creation are in that manifest's header). The archive's root (`archives.root_subpath`, which `archive show` shows) is the one that the manifest of its revision records where the rebuild read that manifest; otherwise the archive's record keeps its own. A registration's plan of a revision whose record is kept is no saved progress (decision 0026, point 8): its rows are that revision's index.
   - So the archive stays at the revision whose manifest the rebuild could not read, never taken back to an earlier one, and that manifest cannot be used: with its record kept, it is missing, cannot be read or has changed (point 2), and restoring it from a backup, or importing an export or a media copy of it (point 7), is all it needs; without one (the database lost that record as well, a file only bears a manifest's name, or only the history names the revision, as where a version before this decision set the archive back), the index has no record of it.
   - The rebuild names each revision whose manifest it could not read, by what became of its record: "kept as recorded: ARCH-0007 (<archive_uuid>): the manifest of revision 2 (manifests/<archive_uuid>/r0002.vam) is missing" for the archive's revision, "left out of the index: ..." for an earlier one, and "not in the index: ..." for the archive's revision of which no record is left; each with its archive (label and uuid) and what is wrong with the file at its place in the words of point 2 ("is missing", or "cannot be read: C", C the cause on its "skipped: ..." line). `--json` gives them as `revisions_kept`, `revisions_left_out` and `revisions_not_indexed` (`archive_uuid`, `label`, `revision`, `manifest`, and `missing`, false where the file was skipped). It exits 2 then too, also where a manifest is missing, which it never lists as skipped; `archive list` shows what is wrong with the manifest of each archive's revision and how to fix it.
   - The rebuild marks each archive whose revision's manifest it skipped, in its transaction, with a `settings` record `rebuild-skipped:<archive_uuid>` that names the revision (`{"revision":2}`), and removes the marks of the rebuild before. While the mark stays, the lists read that archive's manifest whole (point 5). The mark says only which manifest to read so: what is wrong is what that read finds now, and a manifest restored meanwhile is listed without a problem. A missing manifest needs no mark: line 1 alone says that it is missing.

7. **An import puts back the manifest recorded.** R-87 compares an import of a revision that is recorded with the manifest that the repository holds: the same bytes are already present, and other bytes are a `ManifestHashMismatch`. Where that manifest is missing or cannot be read, there was nothing to compare with, and the import failed ("cannot compare with the stored manifest"); where it was not the one recorded any more, the import refused the file that is. So neither an export of the revision (R-87) nor its media copy (R-89), the places besides a backup of the repository that hold a manifest (R-86), could restore it, and once `repo rebuild-index` kept the revision's record (point 6) the file could only be copied into the repository by hand. Such an import now puts the file back where it is the one recorded: its line 1 has the BLAKE3 recorded for it (point 1), and its body is the one that line names (R-62, checked when the file is opened and again in the copy). As an import names a manifest (decision 0030), the file is copied under a temporary name and flushed without the database lock, then renamed to its place (R-63) in a write transaction that finds the record still naming that line 1; the record, its index and the archive's record stay as they are. It says so, "Restored the manifest of ARCH-0007 (<archive_uuid>) revision 2: the file is the one the repository recorded." (exit 0, `restored` in `--json`), and the archive's manifest can be used again, without a rebuild. Any other file is refused as before, "revision 2 is already known with different bytes" (`ManifestHashMismatch`, exit 3), and nothing is written. Where the record changed while the file was copied (it is gone, or names another line 1), nothing is put back: "the repository's record of revision 2 of archive <archive_uuid> changed while this import ran; nothing was imported (import it again)" (exit 12). An index that differs from the manifest (its copy of the header, or a record that names another body) does not keep the file out: put back, the manifest reads as one that the index differs from, and the rebuild repairs the index (point 2). The plain message of point 2 names the import first, with the places that keep a manifest's file.

8. **A lost manifest: a registration over it** (operator, 2026-10-17). Where nothing puts the manifest of an archive's latest revision back, as no backup of the repository, export of that revision or media copy holds it, the manifest is lost (GLOSSARY). Until it is back, the archive cannot be verified (point 3), and where the manifest's line 1 cannot be used (it is missing, its line 1 cannot be read, or line 1 has changed), every registration of a new folder or volume and every relocation in the repository stops on it, as matching reads line 1 of every archive's latest manifest (point 4); a manifest changed only in its body stops none of them, and its revision stays in the archive's list of revisions as an earlier one after the registration over it (below). The last resort registers the archive's next revision from what its root holds now: `register <root_path> --lost-manifest <archive_uuid> [--confirm]`, a registration over the lost manifest.

   - **It says so.** The CLI prints the registration as any, and where the record of N left the index, "revision N, whose manifest was lost, was left out of the index; its verifications stay in the history"; `--json` gives `lost_manifest` (`revision`, `left_out`), given only to such a registration. 9. **The device gone as well: the archive retired** (operator, 2026-10-17). Where the latest manifest of an archive is lost and the device that held the archive is gone as well (broken, lost or wiped), no root is left for a registration over the lost manifest (point 8), and where the manifest's line 1 cannot be used, the lost manifest stopped every registration of a new folder or volume and every relocation for good (point 4). One that matching can use is not lost so, and neither is one of which only the index says otherwise, or has no record while the file at its place reads as that manifest, as a rebuild gives it back: the archive is retired as before, without a confirmation ("Retired L (U).    - **Retiring.** Confirmed, it sets the archive `RETIRED` in one write transaction that finds the archive still at revision N, and otherwise writes nothing: "archive U now has revision M, not revision N; nothing was changed (run the command again)" (exit 12). It writes nothing either (exit 12) while a registration of the archive is unfinished, which would give it a new revision, in the words in which a registration of the archive meets it (decision 0025): "an unfinished registration of archive U (root "R") is creating revision N+1; nothing was changed (finish it with `register --resume` first, or abandon it with `archive abandon-registration U`)", or, for one made for an older revision, those of decision 0030. Retired, it says "Retired L (U), whose manifest of revision N is lost. One whose body has changed, or whose body or an entry cannot be read, is lost all the same, and the archive is no candidate either: the rebuild skips such a manifest and keeps the index as it was, so no rebuild repairs that index. Only an index that stands before an intact manifest, which a rebuild repairs, still stops them, as for any archive. Only a retired archive's manifest is read so, and only while its index is wrong. In matching by root, `load_candidate_pool` leaves such an archive out, neither a candidate nor an archive whose latest manifest cannot be used: it stops no verification by root, no registration and no relocation, and no root is matched as its carrier, nor as one of its earlier carriers.    - **No new revision.** It takes none (decision 0003): a registration over its lost manifest refuses it (point 8), as `register --new-revision` and `archive relocate` refuse a retired archive.    All of this follows from the state, an archive retired and its latest manifest lost, never from a record of the retirement: it holds for an archive retired while its manifest could be used, whose manifest is lost later, and it ends where the manifest is put back.

## Why

The BLAKE3 recorded for line 1 is what the repository knows of it. Other bytes are a changed manifest whether or not they still parse: a brace that a failing device flipped, or a header cut short, says no more about its archive than a changed volume label does. Parsing first made such a change read as a defect of the manifest's format ("invalid manifest header: parse error ...", `RepositoryError`), as if the repository could not read what it had written, and hid that the manifest is not the one recorded. The same holds for an entry line that a second read gives otherwise: called a manifest that cannot be read, it gave the parser's words ("parse error at line 1, column 78"), whose line is not the manifest's, and `RepositoryError`, for bytes that are not the ones validated.

A verification that stops records nothing: the archive's history kept its last result, `PASSED` say, and "Last verify" said nothing of a manifest that no verification can use any more. A verification that cannot check its archive against the recorded manifest has failed one of the checks of §A6 (the manifest's hash, R-14), and recording it is what makes the history and the Archive List say so (R-21). The archive is known without its media, and the manifest's check needs none: looking for the media first would at best find a carrier that nothing can be compared with, and would stop without recording anything whenever the media is not attached.

Such a verification read nothing of the carrier, so it did none of the work of a run it was to resume, and that run, not the failed one, is the attempt to follow once the manifest is restored. Decision 0022 lets a later verification supersede an unfinished one because it gives the archive a newer result or is the newer attempt, and for that reason never lets one that FAILED at once, on the wrong media, supersede a killed run: it did not do the run's work. Superseded, the run would drop out of the Archive List, and once the GUI was closed nothing would offer it again (Verify Archive offers **Resume interrupted run** only while it stays open), though its checkpoints are kept for a manifest that the operator restores in minutes.

The Archive List and `archive list` are where the operator sees the state of every archive (R-21). Stopping them for one archive hid all the others, also those that need attention, and left the damaged one no place to say what is wrong with it. A manifest that cannot be used is a defect that the operator must repair before any verification of the archive can pass, so the list does not exit 0 as if nothing were wrong; the listing itself worked and is complete, so it does not exit 11 either, which a script takes for a repository it could not read.

Whether a root could belong to an archive is told from what the repository trusts: the archive's carrier and its root are recorded in its latest manifest alone (R-14, decision 0004), and the index's copy of that header is never taken in its place (CLAUDE.md rule 3), least of all where the two may disagree. Without the manifest, the root could be that archive's. Matching without the archive could then take the root for another archive's (two archives on media cloned sector by sector, with no device serial to tell them apart, would read as one `MATCH_WEAK` rather than `AMBIGUOUS`), for an earlier carrier, or for no archive's at all (`UNKNOWN`, which offers `register`: a second identity for the same media). Stopping keeps the verification from answering wrongly; the message tells the operator the two steps that end it, in the order that records the failure first.

A root that is a `MATCH` of another archive at its folder is that archive's, and the archive whose manifest cannot be used cannot have its root there. The root's volume has the filesystem id and a device that the other archive's latest manifest records, and the folder is that archive's root on it. VeriArchive never gives two archives roots on one volume that are the same or contain each other (decision 0001): registration refuses a root that overlaps the root of an archive on the same volume (`MATCH`, `MATCH_WEAK` or `MIGRATED`), and relocation refuses a new root that overlaps the root of any archive on its volume. So the archive whose manifest cannot be used is at another root of that volume, which matching never takes for this folder (it narrows the candidates by their root first), or on another volume, whose filesystem id or device is not this one's. The rules leave one opening, and only on purpose: the operator may register the same folder twice (`--force-new-archive`, R-97), as for a clone of the media made sector by sector, which keeps its filesystem id, or import another repository's archive of it. Such twins read as `AMBIGUOUS` while both manifests can be used, a clone on another device by its `MIGRATED` match. Where one twin is the archive whose manifest cannot be used, a `MATCH` of the other still finds the root on that other archive's device and filesystem, at its root: its carrier, verified as its own, also where both twins were registered at the very same folder, whose content is then both archives'. A `MATCH_WEAK` rules nothing out: with no device to compare, a clone of the matched archive's volume, which may be the other twin, reads the same as that volume; with no filesystem id to compare (decision 0018), the device and its size do not tell the matched archive's filesystem from a later one on the same device, which registration takes as another archive (a `MISMATCH` is no overlap). So the stop remains for it, as for every other outcome, where the root has no owner that rules the archive out.

The failure classes are §A6's own. Bytes that are not the ones recorded are a manifest hash that does not match (R-14, R-62). A manifest that is gone or cannot be read, and an index that differs from it, are failures of the repository, as decision 0020 classes a manifest file that cannot be read. The message names the manifest by its place in the repository because that is what the operator restores: a backup of the repository holds it at the same place (R-86). Rebuilding the index from a manifest that is missing or has changed would take what the file holds now for the truth, so only an index that differs from an intact manifest is rebuilt. A manifest whose line 1 has the BLAKE3 recorded for it is intact by that very record: the line is the one recorded, and it names its body, which every whole load checks against it (R-60, R-62). A record that then names another body, archive or revision than that line contradicts the line it vouches for itself, as a copy of the header that is not the line does: the index differs, and rebuilding it from the manifest gives back the record that was written with the line, while restoring the manifest would only bring back the same bytes.

A manifest that `repo rebuild-index` cannot read is no sign that its revision is gone. R-15 has the repository hold the highest revision it knows of each archive, and only the operator steps back to an earlier one (`--force-rollback`). Dropping the record of the latest revision made the index take the earlier revision for the archive's current one: its earlier carrier for the archive's carrier, its earlier entries for what the archive holds. A verification then compared the archive with a baseline it no longer has, a verification of the latest carrier's root found no archive there and offered `register`, and with no manifest read at all the archive was listed as if nothing were wrong. Kept, the record says what the manifest was, and the archive is what this decision already handles: a manifest that cannot be used, restored from a backup and checked against that record (R-14), with nothing left to rebuild. The revision is told from what is left of it, a manifest under its name, a record of it or a verification of it that read the media (below), never from the archive's record alone: the rebuild sets that record from the manifests, as the index's own (an index whose record names another revision than its line 1, point 2, is rebuilt so), and a revision that nothing else names may be one the database made up. A manifest under its name names its revision even where it cannot be read: the repository gives a manifest its name only in the transaction that records its revision (decisions 0023, 0030). Where nothing of the revision's record is left, as when the database lost it too, the index has no record of the manifest, and the archive is still not taken for one at an earlier revision: the message then reads what the file at its place is, as the rebuild would, since no record vouches for it, and says the step that records it again, a rebuild, after the manifest is restored where it cannot be read.

An earlier revision is another matter. Its record serves matching alone, which reads the earlier carriers (decision 0004) to tell media that held an earlier revision of an archive from media that is no archive's. Kept with a manifest that cannot be used, it would stop every verification by root and every registration whose matching reads the earlier carriers (point 4; for an archive at a volume's root, a registration of any folder on another volume) until the manifest is restored, and for good where no backup, export or media copy holds it, while the archive itself, at its own revision, can be used. Left out, as before this decision, the earlier carrier is no longer told from media that is no archive's (`UNKNOWN`, not `PREVIOUS_CARRIER`), which is all that its manifest gave. The operator decided for the archive's own revision (2026-10-16); whether an earlier revision's record should be kept as well, at that cost, is an operator decision still open.

The verification history is a witness of its own. A run that read the media was compared with the manifest of its revision, which the repository had recorded then, and the history is no index: nothing rebuilds it, and its hash chain (R-18) shows a row removed from its middle. A version before this decision took an archive back below its latest revision where it could not read that manifest; with the record of that revision dropped, nothing but the history still names it, and the archive was verified against the earlier revision's manifest, failing on what the later revision added, or passing on the earlier carrier. The rebuild puts such an archive back at the revision that its history names, as one whose manifest the index has no record of, to be restored and indexed. A run that read no media names what the archive's record said when it failed on its manifest (point 3), possibly a revision that only a stray file under a manifest's name gave it: counted, such a file, once removed, would leave the archive for good at a revision of which nothing exists. And where nothing else is left of an archive's revisions, its record keeps its own revision unless the history names a later one: the history raises the archive, never lowers it.

A manifest that nothing puts back kept its archive from being verified for good, and, where its line 1 cannot be used, every registration of a new folder and every relocation of the repository with it (point 4), though the archive's media may be intact and attached. The operator decided (2026-10-17) that the messages say first how to get the manifest back, as the import now puts back the manifest recorded (point 7), and that where nothing holds it, an explicit way out takes what the root holds now as the archive's next revision. That is a registration of a new revision, as `register --new-revision` makes one, compared with nothing, and it resumes and is abandoned as one; only its archive is named, as matching cannot find it at a root. A baseline taken without a comparison hides any damage since the lost revision was registered, so the operator confirms after reading so, as when abandoning a registration's saved progress (decision 0025). A manifest of which only the index says otherwise is not lost, as a rebuild gives it back, and neither is one whose media copy lies at the very root given, which the import puts back: a new baseline would throw their comparison away for nothing. The record of the lost revision then follows the rule of point 6 for an earlier revision: kept with a manifest whose line 1 cannot be used, it would stop matching for good, while one changed only in its body still serves matching with its line 1. A retired archive does not take the way out (point 9): it takes no new revision, and its lost manifest stops nothing, as a retired archive whose latest manifest is lost is no candidate of matching.

The root of the way out is refused only where another archive is plainly at it, by the rule that point 4 gives a verification by root. A `MATCH` of that archive, its filesystem id and a device that its latest manifest records, rules the lost archive out of every folder that contains that archive's root or lies in it, as roots never overlap on one volume (decision 0001), and a registration there would take that archive's content, or a part of it, for the lost archive's baseline. It does not rule the lost archive out of that very root, where the rules leave one opening on purpose, the same folder registered twice (R-97; point 4's reasons), as below. A `MIGRATED` or `MATCH_WEAK` overlap rules nothing out (point 4). A device and a sector-by-sector clone of it, registered as two archives (decision 0005; `--force-new-archive`, as R-97 allows), read so to each other, as each archive's latest manifest records its own volume: `MIGRATED` where the clone's device has a serial of its own, `MATCH_WEAK` behind bridges that give none, and whether the clone is attached or not. Refused there, the way out refused the lost archive's own volume and every folder of it for good, and nothing else ended the lost manifest: no backup holds it, which is what makes it lost; `archive retire` leaves the clone's archive a candidate of matching (decision 0003); and no command removes an archive that has a revision; retiring the lost archive itself (point 9) ends the stop only by giving the archive up, while its own device is there. So the lost manifest stopped every registration of a new folder or volume and every relocation for good, the dead end that this point exists to remove, and the refusal called another device "the same volume". Taken, such a root may still be the other archive's own, seen through another enclosure or behind a bridge that gives no serial: the confirmation names that archive as possibly a clone (at that archive's very root, as the archive whose root it is, with the match, as twins may stand there too), and the operator, who knows which device is which, as with cloned media in decision 0022 (point 7), confirms only where the root is not that archive's.

Where the device is gone as well, nothing is left from which to register the archive's next revision, and its lost manifest stopped the repository's registrations and relocations for good, as matching cannot tell without that manifest where the archive's carrier and root are (point 4). The operator decided (2026-10-17) to declare it by retiring the archive. Retirement already says what the operator declares: the archive is no longer expected to be verified, takes no new revision, and keeps its history (decision 0003). It kept a retired archive in matching so that its media, attached again, is recognised rather than registered a second time; without its latest manifest, matching cannot recognise that media at all, and keeping the archive a candidate only stopped every registration of a new folder or volume and every relocation. So a retired archive whose latest manifest is lost leaves matching, which is all that ends the stop, and keeps its record, its revisions and its verification history with its hash chain (R-18). Its media, should it turn up, is no longer told from media that no archive has; the operator accepts that by declaring the device gone, and the confirmation says what retiring the archive then means before anything is written, as it changes what matching does. Retirement is the archive's own record (`archives.status`, which `repo rebuild-index` keeps), and the lost manifest is what the manifest's place holds: the state needs no record of its own, holds as well for an archive retired before its manifest was lost, which would otherwise stop every registration and relocation again with no way on, as it takes no new revision, and ends of itself where the manifest is put back. Such an archive needs nothing of the operator any more, as OVERDUE leaves a retired archive out (R-105): `archive list` exits 0 for it, and the line under it still says what is wrong and how the manifest is put back, should a backup turn up.

An index that is wrong is no sign that the manifest behind it is intact. Line 1, which matching reads alone, vouches for the body only by a hash that the body must still match, and says nothing of an entry. Where the index differs from a manifest whose body has changed as well, or has no record of a manifest whose body or entries cannot be read (as where a version before this decision dropped the record of a revision whose manifest its rebuild could not read), line 1 alone says that a rebuild repairs the index, while `repo rebuild-index` skips that manifest and keeps the index as it was (point 6), so the index stays wrong for good. Retired, such an archive was told lost by `archive retire` and by the lists once a rebuild had marked it, which read the manifest whole, and kept as a possible owner by matching, which read line 1 alone: every registration of a new folder or volume and every relocation stopped for good, the dead end that this point exists to remove, and the confirmation had said that the archive stops nothing. So matching reads a retired archive's latest manifest whole where line 1 alone says that only the index is wrong of it, and the lists judge it alike, whatever the last rebuild marked. The read is the rebuild's, every entry included, as a rebuild repairs an index only where it reads the manifest to its last entry; it costs one more pass over that manifest at each matching, and only while a retired archive's index is wrong.

`archive retire` judges the manifest as matching will judge it once the archive is retired, so that it asks for the confirmation, and says that the archive stops nothing and is no longer taken as a possible owner, only where retiring takes the archive out of matching. Judged as a verification by uuid checks it (`check_recorded_manifest`), as point 9 first did, a manifest changed only in its body was lost: retiring asked for a confirmation and said that the archive was no longer a possible owner, while matching, which reads its line 1, went on recognising it, its folder taking no new archive. Such a manifest stops nothing (point 8), and its media is recognised, as decision 0003 wants: its archive is retired as any, and the lists, once a rebuild has marked it, show its manifest lost (point 5), which says nothing of matching.

A retired archive no longer takes the way out of point 8. It took it while its lost manifest, as a candidate of matching, stopped every registration and relocation, the only way to end that stop; a retired archive whose latest manifest is lost stops nothing now, and its retirement says that it takes no new revision, which the confirmation repeats. A registration over the lost manifest that a version before point 9 let begin for a retired archive is refused when it is resumed, and waits to be abandoned, as one made for an older revision (decision 0030): listed so, in a state of its own ("archive is retired; abandon it"), it offers no Resume that is always refused, where it read "stopped; it can be resumed"; and retiring an archive whose latest manifest is lost waits until no registration of the archive is unfinished, which would give it a new revision.

The plain message is where the operator meets a lost manifest: in the Archive List, `archive list` and `archive show`, and in the refusal of every command that needs it. It named the registration over the lost manifest, which reads the archive's root, as the last resort, and not retiring the archive, the way out where the device that held the archive is gone as well, which only the user guides told: an operator whose device is gone was sent to a registration with no root to read. Which way out fits depends on whether the device is there, which only the operator knows, so the message names both, each with its condition, in that order, as retiring gives the archive up (operator, 2026-10-17).

The same folder registered twice on purpose (R-97, `--force-new-archive`) gives each twin the filesystem id and the device of that folder's volume: where one twin's latest manifest is lost, its own root is a `MATCH` of the other twin at that twin's root, and the way out refused it as plainly the other's. Nothing that the repository trusts tells a twin from an archive whose root lies elsewhere: only the lost manifest recorded the lost archive's carrier and root, the index's copy of its header and `archives.root_subpath` are never taken for it (CLAUDE.md rule 3), and media evidence records the volume and no folder. So the refusal kept the lost twin from its own folder for good, and where its line 1 cannot be used, its lost manifest stopped every registration of a new folder or volume and every relocation (point 4), with retiring the twin (point 9) the only way on while its folder is there. The operator decided (2026-10-17) to take such a root where the operator names the archive by its uuid and confirms. The confirmation names the archive whose root it is, so that the operator, who knows that the folder was registered as both, confirms only for a twin; the very root of an archive that the archive named is no twin of, as the own volume of a clone's archive, a `MATCH` of it where the clone's device has a serial of its own, is taken only after that confirmation as well. Twins at one folder write one identity file (R-89), the last one written, which may name either; one that names a third archive still refuses the root, as it says that the folder is neither twin's. Registered, the lost twin is at its folder again beside the other, as before its manifest was lost, and a verification by root matches both (`AMBIGUOUS`). Twins on a volume that shows no device serial (behind a bridge that gives none, or on Linux without the udev database, decision 0021), or that shows another serial than when they were registered (another enclosure), match each other's folder only as `MATCH_WEAK` or `MIGRATED`, which also reads a clone of that volume, or that volume itself: named as possibly a clone, as point 8 first named them, the twin was told to confirm only where the root is not that archive's, which for a twin it is, and an identity file naming the other twin refused the root. So the very root is taken whatever its match, and the line says the match and both cases in which to confirm.

An export and a media copy hold a manifest's very bytes (R-87, R-89), and the record says which bytes those are: the BLAKE3 of line 1, a line that names the body by its own BLAKE3 and length. A file whose line 1 has that BLAKE3, and whose body that line names, is the recorded manifest, wherever it comes from; put back, it is checked against the record as every recorded manifest is (R-14), and nothing of the record or the index needs to change. The import still compares the bytes first (R-87), so a manifest that the repository holds intact is never replaced, and a file that is not the one recorded is refused. The record is read again in the transaction that renames the file, where decision 0030 has an import find its revision not recorded, so that no file is put under a record that changed meanwhile.

Rejected alternatives:

- Parsing line 1 first and comparing its hash afterwards, as a whole manifest was loaded until now: a header changed into text that no longer parses never reaches the comparison.
- Comparing the hash in every reader of manifests: an import's source and `repo rebuild-index` read manifests that no record vouches for, where a line 1 that does not parse is what is wrong with them.
- Taking every entry line of a recorded manifest that does not parse, or comes out of canonical order, for other bytes, since a recorded body was parsed in full when it was recorded or imported: it rests on every recording having parsed its whole body, and it would leave an import's source and `repo rebuild-index` to call such a read a malformed manifest. Reading on to the end of the body tells it for every manifest read, at the cost of reading the rest of the body once, and only when a line fails.
- Looking for the media first, and recording media evidence with the failure: the verification would stop, recording nothing, whenever the media is not attached, and the evidence would say only that the media was there.
- Recording `INCOMPLETE`: an `INCOMPLETE` run makes no claim (§A6), and this verification has found something wrong.
- Loading the whole manifest before looking for the media, and keeping it for the comparison: for an archive whose media is not attached, millions of entries would be parsed for nothing. The check reads the manifest without its entries; they are read once the media is found.
- Taking over the run being resumed (its checkpoints moved to the failed run): the work it did would be lost for a manifest that the operator restores in minutes.
- Letting the failed verification supersede the run it was to resume, as decision 0022 lets every verification recorded after a run do: the Archive List would drop a run kept for a resume, and after the GUI was closed nothing would offer it again.
- Telling a verification that read no media by the media evidence it lacks (R-96): `media_evidence` is not indexed by run, so every read of the Archive List would scan it once for each run of the archive; the run's own counts say it, in the hash chain.
- A failure class of its own (`ManifestMissing`): §A6's classes cover it, and a new class is a change for every reader of the history.
- Calling a record that names another body, archive or revision than the line 1 it vouches for a changed first line, as this decision first did: the line is the one recorded, so the message said what was not so, and restoring the manifest, which brings back the same bytes, could not fix it.
- Going on by root when the index's copy of the damaged archive's header (or `archives.root_subpath`) names another volume or folder: it takes the index for the manifest (CLAUDE.md rule 3).
- Matching by root without the archive whose manifest cannot be used, and going on whatever the outcome: its carrier would read as another archive's, an earlier carrier or no archive's, as said above. Only a `MATCH` of another archive at the root's folder rules it out.
- Stopping every verification by root while the latest manifest of any archive cannot be used, as this decision first did: one damaged archive kept every other archive from a verification by its root until the operator repaired it, though a root that is plainly another archive's cannot be the damaged one's.
- Stopping a registration of a new revision of a root that is plainly another archive's, as this decision first did: one damaged archive kept every other archive from a new revision until the operator repaired it, by the same reasoning that let its verification by root go on.
- Letting a registration of a new folder or volume go on where its root is no archive's (`UNKNOWN`) while a latest manifest cannot be used: the folder could be the archive whose manifest cannot be used, whose carrier only that manifest records, and would become a second archive of the same media (as `UNKNOWN` offers `register`).
- Letting a relocation go on while a latest manifest cannot be used: its new root must overlap no archive's root on its volume (decision 0001), and that archive's root is unknown.
- Going on for a `MATCH_WEAK` as well, as a verification by root goes on for one: with no device, or no filesystem id, to compare, the root could be a clone, or a later filesystem on the same device, that is the archive whose manifest cannot be used.
- Ending the message of a verification by root with "restore its manifest from a backup of the repository", as point 4 first did, and with "... and rebuild the index" where the index has no record of the manifest: it named neither the import of an export or a media copy, which point 7 lets put a manifest back, nor the last resort, while every other message of a manifest that cannot be used names both (point 2), and the import that puts back a manifest that the index has no record of records it, so no rebuild follows.
- Recording a `FAILED` run, by root, for the archive that the message names: the root was not found to be its. Only a verification that knows its archive records the failure (point 3); the message names the archive so that the operator makes that verification.
- Naming every archive whose latest manifest cannot be used in the message: a long message for a rare case, which the operator works through one archive at a time anyway; `archive list` names every one.
- `archive list` exiting 0, as it does for an archive that is OVERDUE or whose last verification FAILED: those are states that the history records; a manifest that cannot be used is a repair the operator owes the repository, and a script that checks only the exit code would never see it.
- Exiting 11, or 3: the listing worked and is complete, and no verification failed in it.
- Checking every manifest's body for the list: it would hash gigabytes of manifests for a listing; the body is checked where the archive is verified (point 3).
- Taking the volume, model and capacity of the damaged archive from the index's copy of its header: CLAUDE.md rule 3.
- `manifest_problem: null` on every archive: the members for a state that only some archives are in are given only to them (`unfinished_registration`, `unfinished_relocation`, `copy_of`).
- Dropping the record of a revision whose manifest `repo rebuild-index` cannot read, as before, and keeping only the archive at that revision, as one of which the index has no record: the fix would take two steps (restore, then rebuild) where one does, the restored manifest would be checked against nothing the repository recorded, the lists, which read line 1 alone, could not tell a body that does not match its header and would say to rebuild where the rebuild skips the manifest again, and the records of earlier revisions, which matching reads for the earlier carriers, would go as well.
- Counting every run of the history, also one that read no media: a run that failed on its manifest records the revision that the archive's record said, which a stray file under a manifest's name may have given it (above).
- Telling an archive that a version before this decision set back below the revision its history names in the lists, in matching and in verification, until a rebuild: a second rule beside the rebuild's for a state that only an older version leaves, which one `repo rebuild-index` with this version repairs.
- Keeping each archive at the revision its record says, whatever the rebuild finds: a revision that the database made up, as a record that names another revision than its line 1 (point 2), could never be rebuilt away, and the archive would stay for good at a revision of which nothing exists.
- Keeping the record of every revision whose manifest the rebuild cannot read, earlier ones too, as this decision first did: an earlier one keeps matching from going on (point 4), for good where no backup holds its manifest, for the sake of recognising an earlier carrier; the operator's decision covered the archive's own revision, and the earlier ones are an operator decision still open.
- Recording why the rebuild skipped a manifest, for the lists to show that cause: it would be stale once the manifest is restored, or damaged otherwise since. The mark records only which archive's manifest to read whole; what the file is now says what is wrong and how to fix it.
- Leaving the lists to read line 1 alone, also of a manifest that the rebuild skipped, as this decision first did: the rebuild said once what it skipped, while the lists, where the operator looks (R-21), went on showing the archive as sound, or, for a manifest that the index has no record of, saying to rebuild the index, which skipped it again.
- Telling the operator to copy an export or a media copy into `manifests/<archive_uuid>/rNNNN.vam` by hand: nothing would check the file against the record before it takes the manifest's place, another revision's or archive's file would stand there until a verification failed on it, and the name would be given outside the transaction that orders names with records (decisions 0023, 0030).
- Requiring as well that the file's line 1 be the index's copy of the header, and that it name the body that the index records: those are the index's claims; where they differ from the line whose BLAKE3 is recorded, the index differs (point 2), and the file, which is the one recorded, would be refused, leaving the operator to copy it by hand before the rebuild that repairs the index.
- Importing such a file as the revision anew, its record dropped and written again from the file: the record is what the file is checked against (R-14); written anew from the file, any manifest of that revision would be taken.
- Going back to the previous revision, as the archive's current one, where the latest manifest is lost: the history would go backwards. R-15 lets only the operator step back, with `--force-rollback`, the verifications of the lost revision would follow a current revision older than theirs, and the archive would be compared with the baseline and the carrier of that earlier revision, which it no longer has, as `repo rebuild-index` did before this decision (point 6).
- Leaving no way out until the manifest is back (point 8): where nothing holds it, the repository's registrations of new folders and its relocations stop for good (point 4), and the archive is never verified again.
- Ending an earlier revision's message with the import, as point 2 first did, because the archive has a later revision already: matching still reads that revision's carrier wherever the latest carriers do not decide (decision 0004), so every registration of a new folder or volume whose matching reads it stopped with a message that named no way on where nothing holds the manifest, while the registration over a lost manifest refuses the archive, whose own manifest can be used, and `repo rebuild-index` leaves the earlier revision out (point 6).
- Comparing the root with the index's rows of the lost revision (`files`), which still hold its entries' hashes, before taking the new baseline: the index is never taken for the manifest (CLAUDE.md rule 3, R-14), and the comparison would claim what no validated manifest backs.
- Telling the root of a registration over a lost manifest from the index's copy of the lost revision's header, or from `archives.root_subpath`: the same rule. The operator names the root, which is refused only where it is plainly another archive's.
- Refusing the root of a registration over a lost manifest where it overlaps the root of another archive on a volume that it matches as `MATCH_WEAK` or `MIGRATED`, as a relocation's new root is refused, as point 8 first did: a device and its sector-by-sector clone registered as two archives read so to each other, so the lost archive's own volume was refused for good, the clone attached or not, while nothing else ends a lost manifest; and the refusal called a volume on another device "the same volume".
- Taking such a root without naming the other archive: the root may be that archive's own, seen through another enclosure or behind a bridge that gives no serial, and its content would become the lost archive's baseline; the operator, who can tell the devices apart, sees the archive before confirming.
- Letting an identity file on the root that names the lost archive take a folder that contains another archive's root, or lies in it, past a `MATCH` of that archive, as such a file keeps a root from being plainly another archive's in point 4: there it makes a verification by root stop, which costs nothing, while here the file, a claim and no proof (§B1.2), would give that folder and that archive's content in it to the lost archive as its baseline.
- Refusing the very root of another archive on a volume that is plainly that archive's, as point 8 first did: where the same folder was registered as two archives on purpose, the lost twin's own folder was refused for good, and where its line 1 cannot be used, its lost manifest stopped every registration of a new folder or volume and every relocation.
- Telling a twin from what the index or the history says of the lost revision (the index's copy of its header, `archives.root_subpath`, the media evidence of its registration): the index is never taken for the manifest (CLAUDE.md rule 3), and media evidence records no folder.
- Taking another archive's very root without naming that archive: its content would become the lost archive's baseline where the archive named is not its twin; the operator, who knows which archives the folder was registered as, sees it before confirming.
- Taking a root whose identity file names a third archive, neither the archive named nor one whose very root it is: the file says that the folder is neither twin's.
- Naming another archive at the very root on a volume that matches it only as `MIGRATED` or `MATCH_WEAK` as possibly a clone, as point 8 first did: for twins on a volume without a device serial, or with another serial than when they were registered, the line told the operator to confirm only where the root is not that archive's, which for a twin it is, and an identity file naming the other twin refused the root, the dead end that point 8 removes for a `MATCH`.

## Consequences

- Amended by decision 0017 (2026-10-27): the messages quoted here name no decision record ("a retired archive takes no new revisions", "... whose latest manifest records this volume's filesystem id and device"), and "(ManifestHashMismatch)" no longer follows "manifest body does not match its header" or "the manifest changed while it was read"; `--json` keeps the failure classes.

- Amended by decision 0022 (point 7, operator, 2026-10-20): `register` takes `--confirm` with `--resume` as well, which confirms the resume of a registration that an older version left and that it may still run; with neither `--lost-manifest` nor `--resume`, `--confirm` stays a usage error (exit 10), now "--confirm is used only with --lost-manifest or --resume".
- Amended by decision 0042: the confirmation of a registration over a lost manifest says "damage since revision N was recorded would not be found" (自版次 N 記錄以來), where it said "registered": the lost revision is the archive's current one, which a relocation or a verification adding chunk hashes may have recorded. The words quoted here say so.
- No change to the manifest bytes, the database schema or the golden test.
- Point 8: `register <root_path> --lost-manifest <archive_uuid> [--confirm]` (§B16); the plan of such a registration names `lost_manifest` (§B8); the glossary names a lost manifest. No change to the manifest bytes, the database schema or the golden test. The plain message of a manifest that is missing, cannot be read or has changed, of a manifest the index has no record of where the file at its place is missing or cannot be read too, now says to put it back with `import` of its file, and for the archive's latest revision ends with the last resort (point 2); an earlier revision's ends with `repo rebuild-index`, which leaves that revision out of the index.
- After a registration over a lost manifest, a file left at the lost revision's place that `repo rebuild-index` cannot read is skipped by every rebuild until it is removed (exit 2), as any such file; one that it can read is indexed again as the earlier revision it names. A manifest of the lost revision found later is older than the archive's revision now: `import --force-rollback` records it as the earlier revision it is, and the archive stays at its revision (R-15).
- Point 9: an archive whose latest manifest is lost and whose device is gone as well is retired, `archive retire <archive_uuid> --confirm`, and stops nothing any more, also where its index is wrong as well and the manifest behind it has changed in its body or cannot be read, which stopped every registration and relocation for good: matching reads such a retired archive's manifest whole, one more pass over it at each matching, while its index is wrong. `archive retire` of an archive whose latest manifest is lost so that matching cannot use it exits 12 without `--confirm` (`retirement` in `--json`) and while a registration of the archive is unfinished, where one whose manifest changed only in its body is retired at once, as before point 9; with `--confirm` its `--json` gives `manifest_lost` and `manifest_problem`. A retired archive whose latest manifest is lost is no candidate of matching (§B10, decision 0003), whether it was retired before its manifest was lost or after; it takes no registration over its lost manifest, which point 8 first let it take, and a resume of one that an older version began is refused; its plain message ends with the import; `archive list` and `archive show` list it with "manifest lost:" (`manifest_lost` in `--json`) and exit 0 for it, and the Archive List shows it so, not in red. No change to the manifest bytes, the database schema or the golden test.
- Should the device of a retired archive whose latest manifest is lost so that matching cannot use it turn up again, nothing recognises it, and a registration of it makes a new archive; once the manifest is put back, both archives match that folder (`AMBIGUOUS`).
- `repo rebuild-index` names the lost revision of a retired archive as any revision whose manifest it cannot read, kept as recorded, and exits 2 then too: it reports every manifest that it could not read.
- An interrupted verification of a retired archive whose latest manifest is lost is not listed with the unfinished operations (decision 0022, point 5; operator, 2026-10-17 and 2026-10-20), where it stayed listed, stopped and resumable, as one of any archive whose manifest cannot be used (point 3): it can never be resumed. The manifest is judged as the lists judge it (point 5). A retired archive whose latest manifest is not lost keeps its interrupted verifications listed, as any archive. The record and checkpoints of such a run stay: `verify --resume` of it fails on the manifest, as point 3 records, and continues once the manifest is put back, when the run is listed again.
