# The operator can abandon an unfinished registration

An unfinished registration keeps its plan and its saved progress (the checkpoints, R-104) until it is resumed and recorded. Nothing removed one that can never be finished, as when its media failed: its root took no other registration ("an interrupted registration of this root exists"), the Archive List showed it for good (decision 0022), and the archive of a first registration stayed `REGISTRATION_INCOMPLETE` at revision 0, overdue after 180 days. HANDOFF D11 asked which of a relocation and an unfinished `register --new-revision` of the same archive should create its next revision. Decision 0023 lets the first to record win, except that a relocation leaves the revision to a registration that has saved files for it; but then a relocation, which exists for carriers that go away, is refused for as long as such a registration stays, and with its media gone it stays for ever. The operator decided to keep the rule and to add a way to give a registration up on purpose.

## Decision

1. **The rule stays** (decision 0023): the first to record a revision wins, and a relocation is refused (exit 12) once a registration creating the same revision has saved files for it. Its refusal now names the way out: "... nothing was changed (finish it with `register --resume` first, or abandon it with `archive abandon-registration U`)". So does the refusal of `register <root>` while the registration of that root is unfinished: "an interrupted registration of this root exists (archive U); finish it with --resume, or abandon it with `archive abandon-registration U`".

2. **Abandoning.** `veriarchive-cli archive abandon-registration <uuid> --confirm`, or **Abandon...** on the row of a stopped registration in the Archive List's unfinished operations, abandons the archive's unfinished registration. The archive is named as in `archive show`: its uuid, or a unique prefix of at least 8 characters. In one write transaction:
   - The archive's plan must exist; otherwise "archive U has no unfinished registration" (exit 12). The GUI names the registration it showed (its revision and `created`); when the archive's plan is another one by then (resumed and recorded, and the next one started), nothing is changed: "the unfinished registration of archive U is no longer the one shown (it was resumed, finished or abandoned meanwhile); nothing was changed".
   - Never while an attempt of it runs, judged as decision 0024 judges it: refused (exit 12) with "the registration of archive U is still running in process N (P) on this computer; abandon it once that process has ended". Where the plan names no process this computer can check (an older version's plan, another computer's), an attempt whose plan said it worked less than 3 minutes ago, without a stop mark, may still run, as the Archive List shows it: refused with "an attempt of the registration of archive U said it was working less than 3 minutes ago; abandon it once it has stopped".
   - It removes the plan and the saved progress: the rows of the files table under the revision the registration creates. When that revision is recorded (by an operation that left the plan, decision 0023), those rows are the recorded manifest's index, and only the plan goes.
   - It removes nothing else: no recorded revision or its index, no manifest, no media evidence, no row of the verification history (R-18), no other setting, no other archive; and nothing on the media (R-03).
   - **A first registration**, one whose plan creates revision 1 of a new archive (`new_archive`): the archive's record goes too, while it holds nothing else (point 4).

3. **Confirmation.** Without `--confirm` the CLI shows what abandoning would remove (the archive, the revision, the root, when the registration started, the files and bytes saved, and whether the archive goes) and stops with exit 12, "run the command again with --confirm to abandon it"; with `--json` it prints that error with the registration as `registration`. With `--confirm` it abandons and exits 0; `--json` prints the registration with `archive_removed`, whether the archive's record went with it. The GUI words its question from the same check as the CLI (`abandonable_registration`), read when **Abandon...** is clicked, never from the Archive List's last read: that read may be seconds old, it counts every row under the revision also when they are the index of a recorded revision (and then only the plan goes), and it cannot tell whether the archive's record goes (point 4). The check runs as the window's one operation, with a progress bar ("Reading the registration of ARCH-0007 ..."), as counting millions of saved files takes a while; a refusal (an attempt that runs, another registration by now) shows at once. Otherwise the GUI asks, with **Cancel** as the default: "Abandon the unfinished registration of ARCH-0007? Its saved progress will be lost: N files (S) hashed so far. It cannot be resumed afterwards. Recorded revisions, manifests and the verification history are not changed.", and when the archive's record goes "The archive has no revision yet: it is removed with its registration." The GUI abandons the registration it asked about (its revision and `created`), after the same checks again, as the window's one operation, with a progress bar, and reports the result in a message of its own; it refuses while another operation runs in the window.

4. **An archive whose first registration is abandoned is removed**, in the same transaction: its record in `archives`, which holds nothing else. Only while everything says that the archive never had a revision: the plan creates revision 1 of a new archive (`new_archive`); the archive's record says revision 0 and that it was never registered (`REGISTRATION_INCOMPLETE`, or `RETIRED` since: before this decision, retiring was the only way to keep such an archive from turning overdue); and no revision, verification or media evidence refers to it. The index alone never decides it (validated manifests are the truth, SQLite an index, R-14): `repo rebuild-index` skips a manifest it cannot read and keeps the archive's record, so the index of an archive may hold none of its revisions while its record says revision 1 and the history and the media evidence refer to it. Abandoning that archive's registration of a new revision removes only that registration's plan and saved progress. The repository checks the same again in the statement that removes the record (`ArchiveStore::remove_archive_without_revision`), in the transaction that judged it. The repository is then as if the registration had never been started, except for two things. Its label (`ARCH-NNNN`) is not given out again: it may already be written on the case of the device, and labels are not unique anyway (R-33). And a `.veriarchive/identity.json` that an attempt wrote on the media with `--write-media-copy` (R-89) stays there, as the media is never written to remove it (R-03): it names an archive that no longer exists, and a later registration of that root without a media copy would leave it there, which verification reports as `identity-file-copied` (§B10). So the plan records `media_copy: true` once an attempt writes a media copy, and abandoning a first registration whose plan says so tells the operator to delete the file before registering the root again, unless the new registration writes a media copy itself, which replaces it.

5. **An attempt of a registration that was abandoned** (one whose process the abandoning computer could not see, on another computer) stops at its next checkpoint or at its record, with "the unfinished registration of archive U was abandoned while this attempt ran; this attempt recorded nothing" (exit 12). It writes none of its saved progress and records nothing. Its heartbeat and its stop mark write the plan only while the archive's key holds this registration's plan (the same revision, made at the same `created`), never a later registration's plan under the same key.

6. **One unfinished registration per archive.** A registration that is not a resume never replaces the plan of another unfinished registration of its archive. In the transaction that writes its plan, it looks under the archive's key, and when that holds a plan whose revision is not recorded, it stops (exit 12) and changes nothing, in a relocation's words (decision 0023): "an unfinished registration of archive U (root "R") is creating revision N; nothing was changed (finish it with `register --resume` first, or abandon it with `archive abandon-registration U`)". The root R is the other registration's: this meets a `register <root> --new-revision` through another path to the same volume, as a second mount point, which does not find that registration by its root; one through the same root is refused before, as in point 1. A plan whose revision is recorded is done with (decision 0023) and is replaced, as before.

## Why

A registration whose media failed can never be finished, and only the operator knows that it will not come back. The plan and the checkpoints are the only state of an unfinished registration: removing them ends it, and nothing that a verification relies on is touched. Validated manifests are the truth (R-14); an unfinished registration has none.

An archive whose first registration never finished has no revision: no manifest to verify, compare, export or relocate, and no history. It is not even recognised when its media is attached (identity matching uses the recorded revisions, §B10). Kept, its record would claim an archive that does not exist, stay `REGISTRATION_INCOMPLETE` with nothing able to finish it, and turn overdue. Nothing else would ever remove it: `repo rebuild-index` keeps every archive's record, also one without a manifest, for the label, status and notes that only the database holds.

That an archive never had a revision is read from what the repository keeps besides the index, never from the index alone, which can lose revisions (a manifest that `repo rebuild-index` cannot read). The archive's record says its revision (`current_revision`, written with every revision recorded, and never set back to 0), and media evidence and the verification history are written only for an archive with a revision. Removing the record of an archive that had one would leave its history and evidence pointing at no archive, and lose its label, status and notes for good.

Abandoning is refused while the attempt runs because the attempt would go on: its checkpoints would bring rows back, and its record would add a revision to an archive whose record is gone.

A registration must not replace the plan of another registration of its archive (point 6). An attempt stops once the archive's key no longer holds its own plan (point 5), so the attempt under the replaced plan would stop as if its registration had been abandoned, which nobody did, and the hashing it did would serve no registration. One plan per archive keeps the saved progress with the registration it belongs to, which the operator finishes or abandons by the root the refusal names.

Rejected alternatives:

- Relocation refuses while any registration creating its revision is unfinished (D11 option a): with nothing to end that registration, a relocation could be blocked for good.
- Relocation discards the registration's plan and checkpoints (D11 option b): it throws hours of hashing away unasked, as a side effect of another command.
- Keeping the archive of an abandoned first registration, as `REGISTRATION_INCOMPLETE` or under a new status: a record that nothing can ever change, and a status value that every reader would have to learn for an archive that never was one.
- Marking it `RETIRED` (decision 0003): a retired archive is recognised when its media reappears and keeps its history; this one has neither.
- Removing `.veriarchive/identity.json` from the media: R-03 forbids writing the media unless the operator asks for a media copy, and the device may be dead.
- Naming the registration by its root (`register --abandon <root>`): the root on failed media may no longer exist; the archive's uuid names the registration whatever happened to its root, `archive list` shows the uuid of an archive whose first registration is unfinished, and the relocation's refusal names it.
- An interactive prompt in the CLI: no other command asks, scripts cannot answer, and an explicit flag is the CLI's way for decisions (`--force-new-archive`, `--force-rollback`).
- Abandoning a running registration by cancelling it first: the attempt may run in another process or on another computer, which nothing here can stop.
- Taking an archive whose index holds no revision for one that never had a revision: the index can lose revisions, and the archive would be removed with its history and evidence left behind (point 4).
- Wording the GUI's question from the Archive List's last read: it can promise to remove an archive that stays, or the saved progress of a revision that is recorded, which stays as its index (point 3).
- Refusing a registration through another root path only while the other registration's attempt runs, as a resume is refused (decision 0024): a registration that took over a stopped one's plan would leave that one's root with nothing to resume and its saved progress with no registration that reuses it.

## Consequences

- Amended by decision 0026: the relocation's refusal of point 1 now reads "... this relocation recorded nothing (finish that registration with `register --resume`, then relocate again; or abandon it with `archive abandon-registration U`, then continue this relocation with `archive relocate U <root_path> --resume`)", as the relocation keeps what it matched; an unfinished relocation is abandoned with a command of its own, `archive abandon-relocation`.
- Amended by decision 0030: an unfinished registration made for an older revision than its archive has now, as after an import of a later revision, can no longer finish. The refusals that meet it, of `register <root>` (point 1) and of a registration through another path to the volume (point 6), then name only the abandon: "the unfinished registration of archive U (root "R") would create revision N, but the archive now has a later revision (M); the registration can no longer finish: abandon it with `archive abandon-registration U`" (exit 12). And the transaction of point 6 writes the plan of a registration of an archive only while the archive is still at the revision the registration read when it started; one that another operation overtook meanwhile writes nothing, to be run again (exit 12).
- Amended by decision 0028: `repo rebuild-index` keeps the record and the index of the archive's revision whose manifest it cannot read, so it no longer leaves an archive's index without its revision (point 4, and the consequence on `repo rebuild-index` below): the index of an archive loses its revision only where the database itself is damaged (an earlier revision whose manifest it cannot read still leaves the index). Whether an archive had a revision is still told from its record and what refers to it, never from the index alone; the test of an archive whose index lost its revision removes that revision's record and rows of `files` by hand.
- §B16: `archive abandon-registration <uuid> [--confirm]`, exit `0` abandoned, `12` refused (nothing to abandon, another registration by now, an attempt that runs, or not confirmed), `10` for an unknown or ambiguous uuid, `11` when the repository cannot be read or written. JSON keys: `archive_uuid`, `label`, `revision`, `root`, `started` (UTC), `saved_progress` (`files`, `bytes`), `first_registration` (the archive's record goes with it, point 4), `media_copy`, and with `--confirm` `archive_removed`. `register` exits `12` while another registration of the archive is unfinished, also one through another root path (point 6).
- §B8: a registration's plan gains `media_copy`; no schema change, and the manifests and the golden test are unchanged.
- §B17: a stopped registration's row in the Archive List has **Abandon...**.
- The glossary names it: an abandoned registration.
- The transaction deletes every saved file's row and holds the write lock meanwhile, seconds for millions of files. Other operations' checkpoints that wait longer than 5 seconds fail and are tried again with their next checkpoint; records wait up to 2 minutes (decision 0023).
- Verifications are not abandoned this way: a stopped one is superseded by a later verification of its archive (decision 0022).
- An attempt of a version before this decision does not look for its plan at its checkpoints: abandoned under it (where its process cannot be seen, and its plan has said nothing for minutes), it goes on, and may record a revision of an archive whose record is gone. Do not abandon a registration that an older version may still be running.
- An archive retired while its first registration was unfinished goes with that registration when it is abandoned, as one never retired.
- `repo rebuild-index` empties the index, the saved progress of unfinished registrations included (as before this decision); an archive whose manifests it skips keeps its record, and abandoning its registration of a new revision keeps it (point 4). Amended by decision 0026: rebuild-index now keeps that saved progress, which abandoning then removes.
- Tests: what abandoning a first registration shows and removes, row by row of every table, leaving another archive's record, manifest and history as they were, and the root registered again under a new label; a new revision abandoned while the archive, its revision 1, its index, manifest and history stay, and the archive verifies and takes its next registration; an archive whose index lost its revision (rebuild-index skipped its damaged manifest), whose registration of a new revision is abandoned while its record, history and evidence stay; a plan that does not create the archive, and a first registration's plan whose archive's record says a revision the index lost, each of which keeps the record; refused while the attempt's process runs, then abandoned once it ended or its id belongs to a later process; refused for a plan without a process record, or from another computer, that said it worked a moment ago, and abandoned once it is silent, or for a plan that never said; a stopped plan whose GUI process still runs; a recorded revision's index left alone; another registration than the one shown; nothing to abandon; the media copy reported; a relocation that records once the registration is abandoned; the repository's own guard, for an archive with a recorded revision, a record that says a revision or registered, a verification and media evidence, each of which keeps the record, and for one without anything, also retired, which goes; an attempt that stops when its first registration is abandoned under it, at a checkpoint or at its record, and one of a new revision whose next registration has begun meanwhile, which leaves that registration's plan alone; a registration through a second mount point of the volume, refused while the archive's registration runs (which then records) and after it stopped, as is one through that registration's own root, naming the abandon, and going ahead once it is abandoned, and a plan whose revision is recorded, which is replaced; the CLI end to end (the failed registration listed, its root refused naming both ways out, the confirmation in text and JSON, the archive gone, the root registered again). A mutation check removed each guard in turn (refusal while running, removing the saved progress and only that of the revision it creates, sparing a recorded revision's index, removing the plan, removing the archive of a first registration, a first registration told by its plan and by the archive's record and never by the index alone (also the judgement this record first shipped with, from the index alone), each condition of the repository's guard, an attempt's stop at a checkpoint and at its record, a heartbeat that writes only its own registration's plan, a registration that replaces another's plan, a replaced plan whose revision is recorded, the root's refusal naming the abandon), and each removal failed the test meant for it. The GUI's question has no unit test of its own (GTK): its words come from `abandonable_registration`, which the tests cover.
