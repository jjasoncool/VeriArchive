# A manifest gets its name in the transaction that records its revision

Two attempts of one registration can run at once: `register --resume` while the first attempt still runs (decision 0022 shows an attempt as stopped once its plan has said nothing for minutes, and an attempt of an older version never says anything), or two resumes. Each attempt wrote `manifests/<archive_uuid>/rNNNN.vam` through `IFileWriter`, whose commit replaces the destination (decision 0011), and only the insert of the revision record, whose key is unique, noticed the other attempt. The attempt that finished second therefore replaced the manifest that the first had recorded: the recorded header hash no longer matched the file, and every later verification of the intact archive failed with `ManifestHashMismatch` (R-14). The attempts also shared the body file `rNNNN.vam.body`, so one could copy the other's body behind its own header. Relocation writes its revision the same way and had the same defect; and the revision it records, the archive's next, is also the one that an unfinished `register --new-revision` of the archive creates.

## Decision

1. **Written without a lock, named under it.** The manifest writer streams the body into a file of its own (`rNNNN.vam.body-<random uuid>`), writes header and body into a temporary file beside `rNNNN.vam`, and flushes that file to stable storage (`IOutputFile::flush`). The transaction that records the revision (`BEGIN IMMEDIATE`) first checks that the revision is not recorded, then writes the record as before, then renames the temporary file to `rNNNN.vam` (`commit`, which after the flush has only the rename left), and commits. While one connection holds the write transaction, no other can record the revision or, following the same steps, rename a manifest there. So the manifest of a recorded revision is never replaced, and a revision is recorded only with the bytes this attempt wrote under its name.
2. **The first to record wins; the others stop.** An attempt that finds its revision recorded stops with "revision N of archive U is already recorded (by another attempt of this registration or by another operation, such as a relocation); this attempt recorded nothing" (exit 12, like the other refusals of `register`): it cannot tell which of them recorded it. Its temporary files go with it. It finds out at its next checkpoint (every 1 000 files or 60 seconds, R-104), which looks in its own transaction before it writes; at the record; or, for a resume that read the plan just before the other attempt recorded it, at its start, before it reads the media. It changes nothing of the recorded revision: not the manifest, not its index rows, not the archive record. The media copy of the manifest (R-89) is written only by the attempt that recorded, after recording.
   - **The record waits for the lock.** A statement waits for the write lock 5 seconds (`busy_timeout`), and the record of a large archive holds it longer: about 3 seconds per million entries (its index) on a fast disk. So the record's transaction begins with `ArchiveStore::begin_waiting`, which tries again while another connection holds the lock, for up to 2 minutes (`record_lock_wait`): an attempt that reaches its record while another records then finds the revision recorded (exit 12), not the database locked (exit 11). When the lock stays taken for longer still, it looks once more whether the revision is recorded before it fails. Cancelled while it waits (Ctrl+C, R-94), it stops waiting within one try and ends as interrupted (exit 4), recording nothing.
   - **identity.json.** Every attempt with `--write-media-copy` makes sure before its scan that `.veriarchive/identity.json` says what the plan says (verification reads only its archive uuid). The attempts of one registration write the same bytes, so an attempt writes the file only where the media does not hold them already, and when its write fails it looks again 100 ms later, up to three times: two attempts that replace the file at the same moment fail on Windows (`MoveFileExW`, access denied), and by then the other's write is done.
3. **The plan is left to its registration until its revision is recorded.** The heartbeat and the stop mark of an attempt (decision 0022) change the plan only while its revision is not recorded, in one statement (`UPDATE … WHERE NOT EXISTS`): recording removes the plan, and the key may then hold the plan of the archive's next registration. A plan whose revision is recorded can never be finished, and it would keep its root from every other registration (each resume stops at its start, and `register` asks for `--resume`). So whatever records revision N of an archive removes the plan of revision N in the same transaction, if the key holds it: a registration its own, a relocation that of a registration creating the same revision (point 5). An operation that records a revision without doing so (an import, a version before this one) leaves the plan; the attempt that then finds its revision recorded removes it, in one transaction, while the key still holds the plan of that revision.
4. **Leftovers never block.** A crashed attempt leaves its temporary files under names of its own, which no later attempt uses. A `rNNNN.vam` left by a crash between the rename and the commit is not recorded, and the next attempt replaces it. Nothing removes a crashed attempt's temporary files: an attempt in another process may still be writing them.
5. **Relocation** (decision 0004) records its revision the same way, and its record waits for the lock as a registration's does. When another operation recorded that revision first (another relocation of the archive, or a registration of its next revision), it stops with "revision N of archive U was recorded by another operation while this relocation ran; nothing was changed" (exit 12). A registration creating the same revision (`register --new-revision`, unfinished) competes for it the same way, first to record wins, until that registration has checkpointed files it hashed under the revision (R-104). From then on the relocation leaves the revision to it: recording over those rows would mix them into the relocation's index or fail its insert, and would lose the hashing done. It stops with "an unfinished registration of archive U (root "R") is creating revision N; nothing was changed (finish it with `register --resume` first)" (exit 12). Which of the two should win is an operator decision still open (HANDOFF Part D, D11).

`register --resume` while another attempt runs stays allowed: whether to refuse it is an operator decision still open (HANDOFF Part D, D10), and an attempt whose process was killed cannot be told from one that works.

## Why

Validated manifest bytes are the truth (R-14): a record whose file another attempt replaced makes every verification of an intact archive fail. The rename is the only step that must be ordered with the record. Writing and flushing a manifest takes seconds to minutes for a large archive, and the other operations of the same repository wait for the write lock at most 5 seconds (`busy_timeout`) before their checkpoints fail. A record waits longer: it comes after the whole scan and hashing, and one that fails costs a new scan and manifest; 2 minutes cover the record of tens of millions of entries.

A plan whose revision another operation recorded is the registration's end, not its interruption: the revision number is taken, and R-15 never lets the registration go back to it.

Rejected alternatives:

- Holding the write lock while the manifest is written: the other registrations' checkpoints and records would fail.
- Renaming without replacing (`MoveFileExW` without `REPLACE_EXISTING`, `renameat2` with `RENAME_NOREPLACE`): a manifest left by a crash would block every later attempt unless an attempt could tell it from a recorded one, which again takes the lock; and `RENAME_NOREPLACE` is not available on every filesystem.
- A manifest name of each attempt's own, recorded in `manifest_relpath`: R-63 fixes the names, and rebuild-index, export and the media copy rely on them.
- Reading the named manifest back before recording it: within the transaction no attempt can replace it, and reading a manifest of gigabytes would hold the lock.
- A refusal that names who recorded the revision: an attempt cannot tell another attempt of its registration from another operation.
- A relocation that refuses while any registration creating its revision is unfinished, or one that discards that registration's checkpoints: the first can block a relocation for good, since nothing removes an unfinished registration whose root is gone, and relocation exists for carriers that go away; the second throws away hours of hashing unasked. Both are left to the operator (D11).
- Writing identity.json under a lock, or under a name of each attempt's own: the attempts write the same bytes, so an attempt needs only the media to hold them.

## Consequences

- §B9: `IOutputFile` gains `flush()` (Windows `FlushFileBuffers`, Linux `fsync`); a `commit()` with nothing written since only renames. `ManifestWriter::finish` returns the manifest flushed and not yet named; its caller commits it.
- `ArchiveStore::open` takes how long a statement waits for the write lock (5 seconds; shorter only in tests), and `ArchiveStore::begin_waiting` begins the transaction of a record. Core, no platform code.
- A registration that loses its revision to a relocation ends: its plan is gone, so the Archive List no longer shows it, `register --resume` of its root finds nothing to resume, and the root takes a new registration.
- No change to the manifest bytes or the database schema; the golden test is unchanged.
- An attempt of an older version still names its manifest before it records it, and can still replace a recorded manifest when it loses. Do not resume a registration with an older version while an attempt of it may still run, in either direction.
- `import` still copies a manifest to its name before it records it (R-87): two imports of the same revision at once can still replace a recorded manifest. It also records without removing the plan of the revision it records (the registration's next attempt removes it, point 3), and over a registration's checkpoints of that revision it fails on their keys, as relocation did. To be changed the same way.
- An attempt that loses while it hashes stops at its next checkpoint: after at most 1 000 more files or a minute, but not before the file it reads is done, so a large file is read to its end first.
- Amended by decision 0026: whatever records a revision also removes the record and saved progress of a relocation that creates it (point 3); a relocation that leaves the revision to a registration (point 5) keeps its own saved progress for `archive relocate --resume`, and its refusal says so; two relocations of one archive no longer run side by side, as a relocation that is not a resume is refused while another is unfinished, but attempts of one relocation on two computers still meet at the record.
- Tests: two attempts of one registration interleaved at the manifest, in both orders, with a media copy, and the attempts after them (a third `--resume`, then `--new-revision`); an attempt that reaches its record while the other holds the lock in its own record, for longer than a statement waits; one cancelled while it waits for the lock (interrupted, then a resume records); an attempt that loses while it hashes, with a file read differently and the plan of the next registration written meanwhile; a resume that finds its revision recorded reads nothing and removes the plan left by another operation; a crashed attempt's leftovers (a manifest never recorded, temporary and body files, the body file of an older version) do not block a resume; the heartbeat of a recorded revision's plan writes nothing; a resume that finds identity.json on the media does not write it, and a write that fails while another attempt writes the same bytes counts; two relocations of one archive; a relocation that records the revision of a registration that is hashing (the registration ends, and the archive takes its next registration) or that was interrupted in its scan (its plan goes), and one that leaves it to a registration that has checkpointed files (nothing changed, then the registration and the relocation both record); a relocation whose record waits for the lock, then records, finds the revision recorded, or is cancelled; two writers of one revision keep their own body; `flush()` of the platform writer. A mutation check removed each guard in turn, and each removal failed the test meant for it.
