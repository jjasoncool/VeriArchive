# An imported manifest gets its name in the transaction that records its revision

Decision 0023 made registration and relocation give their manifest its name inside the write transaction that records its revision, and left `import` to be changed the same way. `import <file.vam>` copied the manifest to `manifests/<archive_uuid>/rNNNN.vam` through `IFileWriter`, whose commit replaces the destination (decision 0011), and recorded the revision afterwards. Two imports of one revision at once (two exports of it, from two repositories), or an import while a registration or a relocation records the same revision, could therefore replace the manifest that the other had recorded: the recorded header hash no longer matched the file, and every later verification of the intact archive failed with `ManifestHashMismatch` (R-14). An import also left the plan of a registration and the record of a relocation that create the revision it recorded, though neither can finish any more (decisions 0023, 0026), and over a registration's saved files of that revision its index insert failed in SQLite's own words (`UNIQUE constraint failed: files...`, exit 3).

## Decision

1. **Copied without a lock, named under it.** `import` validates the manifest and checks R-15 as before (R-87), then copies its bytes into a temporary file beside `rNNNN.vam` and flushes it to stable storage (`IOutputFile::flush`), without holding the database lock. The copy is the bytes validated: line 1 is the header line read, and the rest has the header's body BLAKE3 and length; otherwise nothing is staged: "the manifest changed while it was imported; nothing was imported" (`ManifestHashMismatch`, exit 3). So are the entries indexed, which the manifest reader reads from the source again after validating it: it hashes what it reads them from, and they are used only when that has the header's body BLAKE3, length and lines (R-62); otherwise nothing is staged either, in the same words. The transaction that records the revision waits for the write lock as a registration's record does (decision 0023: `begin_waiting`, up to 2 minutes), then finds the revision not recorded, checks R-15 again (point 5), leaves the revision to a registration with saved files of it (point 3), removes what can no longer finish (point 4), writes the archive record, the revision record and its index, renames the temporary file to `rNNNN.vam` last, and commits. So an imported manifest never replaces a recorded one, and a revision is recorded only with the bytes this import validated under its name. V1.1's signature file (`rNNNN.vam.sig`, R-63) is to be staged and named the same way, in the same transaction.
2. **The first to record wins.** An import that finds its revision recorded, by another import, a registration or a relocation, stops: "revision N of archive U was recorded by another operation while this import ran; nothing was imported (import it again to compare it with the recorded manifest)" (exit 12). It changes nothing: not the recorded manifest, its index or the archive record, nor any plan or relocation record. Its temporary file goes with it. Imported again, the manifest is compared byte for byte with the recorded one (R-87): identical bytes are already present, other bytes are a `ManifestHashMismatch`.
3. **A registration's saved files keep the revision.** As a relocation does (decision 0023, point 5), an import leaves the revision to an unfinished registration creating it once that registration has saved files hashed for it (R-104): "an unfinished registration of archive U (root "R") is creating revision N; nothing was imported (finish that registration with `register --resume`, or abandon it with `archive abandon-registration U` and import again)" (exit 12). Rows under the revision that no plan names are no registration's saved progress (decision 0026, point 8), left by a crash: the revision's index replaces them.
4. **What can no longer finish goes with the record.** Once the revision is recorded, a registration creating it that has saved nothing, and a relocation creating it, can never finish: the registration's plan, and the relocation's record and saved progress, are removed in the record's transaction, as a registration's and a relocation's own records remove them (decisions 0023, 0026). An archive whose first registration in this repository is unfinished (`REGISTRATION_INCOMPLETE`, revision 0) is `REGISTERED` once a revision of it is imported; it keeps its own label (R-33).
5. **R-15 in the record's transaction.** A higher revision of the archive may be recorded while the manifest is copied. Without `--force-rollback`, the transaction refuses an older revision as the check before the copy does: "revision N is older than the known revision M; pass --force-rollback to import it anyway" (`ManifestRevisionRollback`, exit 3).
6. **A plan made for an older revision never records** (operator, 2026-10-16). A registration creating revision N+1 is made while the archive is at N, and a relocation against revision N creates N+1. An import of N+2 or later gives the archive a later revision than N while N+1 is not recorded, and point 4 removes only what creates the revision imported; recording N+1 then would set the archive back (R-15). So neither records any more:
   - **Where it is judged.** In the transaction that records N+1, after it found N+1 not recorded and the operation still its own (decisions 0023, 0025, 0026), the archive must still be at no later revision than N (`ArchiveStore::later_revision`: the archive's record says a revision after N while N+1 is not recorded). Every checkpoint (R-104) looks the same way in its own transaction, and `register --resume` and `archive relocate --resume` in the transaction that takes the operation over, before they read anything. So do a registration of the archive that is not a resume, through the registration's root or through another path to its volume (decision 0025, points 1 and 6), and a relocation that is not a resume (decision 0026, point 4), whose refusals would otherwise name a resume as the way on.
   - **The refusal.** Each stops (exit 12) and records nothing: "the unfinished registration of archive U (root "R") would create revision N+1, but the archive now has a later revision (M); the registration can no longer finish: abandon it with `archive abandon-registration U`", or "the unfinished relocation of archive U (to "R") verifies revision N, but the archive now has a later revision (M); the relocation can no longer finish: abandon it with `archive abandon-relocation U`, then relocate again". The registration's plan, or the relocation's record, and its saved progress stay until the operator abandons it (decisions 0025, 0026); an attempt stopped at a checkpoint or at its record says last that it stopped (decision 0022), and records no run.
   - **What is not a later revision.** N+1 recorded means the operation's own revision was taken (decision 0023), and is handled as before. An archive whose record went back below N, as when `repo rebuild-index` skipped the manifest of N that it could not read, has no later revision either: a registration's record goes ahead as before, as the revision it creates is above every revision the index has, and so does a relocation's record, while a relocation's resume is refused as R-104 requires (decision 0026, point 3). Once the manifest is restored and the index rebuilt, the archive is at N again.
7. **Shown in a state of its own.** The Archive List (decisions 0022, 0026), and `archive list` and `archive show` (decision 0026, point 7), show a registration or relocation made for an older revision, once its attempt has stopped (its stop mark, its process ended, or silent for minutes, decision 0024), as `archive has a later revision; abandon it`, with the operations that stopped, and offer it for abandoning (**Abandon...**); `--json` gives it the state `later_revision`. While its attempt still runs, it is shown as any attempt that runs: it stops at its next checkpoint (point 6). A relocation whose new carrier differs keeps its own state, `new carrier differs; abandon it`, when both hold. The repository's read of the unfinished operations judges them as point 6 does (`PendingRegistration::later_revision`, `UnfinishedRelocation::later_revision`).

## Why

Validated manifest bytes are the truth (R-14): a record whose file another operation replaced makes every verification of an intact archive fail, and a recorded revision whose file is not the validated one cannot even be imported again (R-87 finds other bytes recorded). The rename is the one step that must be ordered with the record; copying a large manifest takes a while, during which the other operations' checkpoints must not wait for the lock.

The entries and the copy are checked because an import reads its source three times: to validate it, to read its entries, and to copy it. A file still being written, or removable media read again, can give other bytes the next time; entries from such a read would index content that the manifest recorded does not hold.

An import that loses stops as a registration or a relocation that loses does (exit 12, nothing changed): it cannot tell which operation recorded the revision, and the operator decides what comes next. The comparison of R-87 is the next import's step.

A registration with saved files of the revision has hashed what may have taken hours; a relocation leaves the revision to it for that reason (decisions 0023, 0025), and an import does too. A plan or a relocation record whose revision is recorded is the operation's end, not its interruption (decision 0023): R-15 never lets it go back to that revision.

A plan made for an older revision is at its end as well, though its revision is free: R-15 never lets the archive go back below the revision it has. Before point 6 its record found N+1 not recorded and set the archive back from N+2 to N+1, after which every verification of the intact archive failed with `ManifestRevisionRollback`. The record's transaction is where this is judged for certain: while it holds the write lock no other operation can give the archive a revision, as no other can record N+1 (decision 0023). The checkpoints and the resumes look as well, so that an operation that can no longer finish stops reading within a minute, or reads nothing, rather than at its record after hours. Its saved progress stays because the operator decides when it goes: abandoning is the operator's step (decisions 0025, 0026), as it is for a relocation whose new carrier differs. The list says so in a state of its own (point 7) for the same reason as for that relocation: `stopped; it can be resumed` would invite a resume that is refused, and `it cannot be resumed (the archive has a newer revision)`, which a verification's row says, would not say what to do.

Rejected alternatives:

- Holding the write lock while the manifest is copied: the other operations' checkpoints and records would wait for it and fail (decision 0023).
- A losing import that compares its bytes with the recorded manifest at once and reports identical bytes as already present (exit 0): the import that lost would read the recorded manifest in full as a side effect of losing, and it would end otherwise than a losing registration or relocation. The operator imports again to compare.
- Discarding a registration's saved files to take the revision: throws away hashing unasked (decision 0025, D11 option b).
- Refusing over rows that no plan names: no registration exists to finish or abandon, so nothing would ever let the import record; `repo rebuild-index` removes such rows as well (decision 0026, point 8).
- Copying from the bytes read for validation instead of reading the source again: a manifest of gigabytes is read as a stream and not kept in memory.
- An import that also removes the plans and relocation records of the revisions below the one it records (the other remedy the operator was asked about): the operator chose refusing them; removing them would throw away hours of hashing or matching as a side effect of another command (decision 0025, D11 option b), whether their attempt still runs or not.
- Recording N+1 below the later revision, leaving the archive at N+2: a revision recorded after a later one fits no line of revisions (N+2 already follows the N+1 of the repository it came from, with that one's content), and only the operator steps back to an older revision (R-15, `--force-rollback`).
- Refusing only at the record: an attempt would read the whole carrier first, hours on a large one, and a resume would take the operation up for nothing.
- Counting an archive whose record went back below N (`repo rebuild-index` skipped a manifest) as one at a later revision: the operator would be told to abandon the operation, though it can finish once the manifest is restored and the index rebuilt, and abandoning would throw its saved progress away for a damaged index.

## Consequences

- Amended by decision 0028: in the read of the entries, a line that does not parse, comes out of canonical order or ends the file without its newline fails with `BodyMismatch` as well when what was read, read on to the end of the body, is not the body validated; only in the body validated is it a malformed manifest. An import whose source gives such a line in that read is refused as one that changed while it was imported (`ManifestHashMismatch`).
- No change to the manifest bytes, the database schema or the golden test.
- Core: `ImportFailure::Kind` (`Failed`, `Refused`); `import_manifest` stages its copy and names it in its record. CLI: `import` exits `12` for a refusal, without a failure class in its message.
- `ManifestReader::next` hashes what it reads the entries from and fails at the end of the body (`BodyMismatch`, "the manifest changed while it was read") when that is not the body validated, for every manifest read: an import's source, a recorded revision's manifest (verification then fails with `ManifestHashMismatch`, as for a body that does not match its header), and `repo rebuild-index`, which skips such a manifest. A caller uses no entry before the end of the body.
- `import` takes no `CancelToken`, as before: Ctrl+C while its record waits for the write lock ends the process, and its temporary file stays as a crashed attempt's does (decision 0023, point 4).
- An import of a version before this decision still names its manifest before it records it: do not import with an older version while another operation may record the same revision.
- An archive at revision N that imports revision N+2 or later keeps what creates N+1, another revision than the one recorded: the plan and saved progress of an unfinished registration of N+1, and the record and saved progress of a relocation against N, also one that runs meanwhile. Until point 6, the record of either found N+1 not recorded and set the archive back to N+1, after which every verification of it failed with `ManifestRevisionRollback`; a relocation resumed after the import was refused, a resumed registration was not. The operator decided the remedy (2026-10-16): neither ever records, and both stay for the operator to abandon (points 6 and 7).
- Core: `ArchiveStore::later_revision(archive, made_for)`. The engines refuse a registration or relocation made for an older revision as `RegisterFailure::Kind::Refused` and `RelocateFailure::Kind::Refused` (exit 12). App: `OperationState::LaterRevision` (state code `later_revision`), which the Archive List orders and offers for abandoning as a stopped operation.
- An attempt of a version before point 6 does not look at the archive's revision: resumed with such a version after an import of a later revision, a registration or relocation still records N+1 and sets the archive back. Do not resume them with an older version.
- Decision 0023, point 3 ("an import ... leaves the plan") and its consequence on `import`, and decision 0026, point 5 ("an import does not"), no longer hold: an import removes them in its record.
- Tests: two imports of one revision at once, interleaved at the manifest in both orders (the second refused, the recorded manifest, its record and index the first's, no file left behind, and each imported again compared with the recorded manifest); an import that reaches its record while another holds the write lock for longer than a statement waits (refused, not locked); a registration with saved files of the revision (refused, naming both ways on, nothing changed, then recorded once the registration is abandoned); the plan of a first registration, of a later registration and the record and saved progress of a relocation removed with the revision they create, those of another revision kept, rows that no plan names replaced by the index, and the archive of an unfinished first registration registered under its own label; a higher revision recorded while the manifest is copied (refused unless forced); a source that changes between validation and copy, in its body or its header, and one that gives other bytes only in the read of its entries (nothing imported, nothing left behind); the reader's own check, and a verification whose manifest gives other entries when read again (`ManifestHashMismatch`, recorded); the CLI end to end (a registration of revision 2 stopped at its record, the import of revision 2 refused with exit 12, then recorded once that registration is abandoned). A mutation check removed each guard in turn (the revision found not recorded, naming the manifest only in the record, waiting for the write lock, R-15 in the transaction, the refusal over saved files, the removal of rows no plan names, of the plan and of the relocation record, the archive registered, both checks of the copy, the refusal of a lost import, the reader's check of the entries' read and its failure class in an import and a verification), and each removal failed the test meant for it.
- Tests of point 6: a `register --new-revision` creating revision 2 that was interrupted, and a relocation against revision 1 interrupted at about half, whose archive then imports revision 3 (an export of another repository, sample data): their resumes are refused, reading nothing, and so are a registration of the root without `--resume`, one through a second mount point of the volume and a relocation without `--resume`, each saying that the archive has a later revision and naming the abandon; the plan or the record and the saved progress stay, the archive stays at revision 3 and verifies, and once they are abandoned the archive records revision 4 by registration and by relocation; an import of revision 3 while the registration or the relocation writes its manifest (refused at the record: no revision 2, no manifest under its name, no run, the stop mark written, every file saved); an import at the 101st of 2 540 files (the registration and the relocation stop at their next checkpoint with less than half of the rest read); a plan whose revision is recorded keeping the words that name its resume; the store's judgement (a later revision while the next one is not recorded; not the same revision, not once the next one is recorded, not for an archive without a record); both refusals in Chinese; the CLI end to end (in the second repository a relocation and a registration of revision 2 stopped at their record, revision 3 imported, both resumes, the folder's registration and a relocation without `--resume` refused with exit 12 in their words, the archive at revision 3 verifying, both abandoned). A mutation check removed each guard of point 6 in turn (at the record, at the checkpoints and at the resume of each operation, the words of a registration of the root, of one through another path and of a relocation without `--resume`, and both conditions of the store's judgement), and each removal failed the test meant for it.
- Tests of point 7: a registration and a relocation made for an older revision, which read as running while their attempt runs, and once it has stopped (its stop mark, its process ended, or silent for minutes) in their own state, offered for abandoning, in both languages, with the state code `later_revision`, after an operation that runs, and on the command line's line in both languages; read from a repository where both were interrupted (stopped and resumable), and then, after an import of revision 3, made for an older revision; the CLI end to end (`archive show --json` and its two lines). A mutation check removed each part in turn (the repository's judgement of each operation and its passing on, the state, its waiting for the attempt to stop, its place with the stopped operations and its Abandon, its state code and its words), and each removal failed the test meant for it.
