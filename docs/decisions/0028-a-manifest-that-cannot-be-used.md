# What is done with a recorded manifest that cannot be used

A revision is recorded with the BLAKE3 of its manifest's line 1 and of its body, and the index keeps a copy of that line 1 (`archive_revisions.header_json`, §B8). Validated manifest bytes are the truth (R-14), so a recorded manifest can be used only while it is there and can be read, its line 1 and its body are the ones recorded, and the index's copy of its header is its line 1 (CLAUDE.md rule 3, never one in place of the other). A manifest that fails one of these cannot be used: it is gone, it cannot be read, it has changed, or the index holds its header otherwise. Until now a verification of its archive stopped (exit 11) and recorded nothing, so the history kept the archive's last result as if nothing were wrong. The operator decided how such a manifest is handled.

## Decision

1. **The first line before parsing.** Wherever the manifest of a recorded revision is read, its line 1 is compared with the BLAKE3 that the repository recorded for it before it is parsed (`ManifestReader::open` given that BLAKE3; `load_recorded_header`). A line 1 that differs is a changed manifest, whatever it holds: also one that no longer parses, one cut off before its newline, the line recorded without its newline (the body gone with it), and an empty file. Where a whole manifest is loaded (verification, `archive compare`, `export`, `export-sha256sums`, relocation), it fails with `ManifestHashMismatch`, as a body that does not match its header does (R-62), never as a malformed manifest or with `RepositoryError`; where line 1 alone is read (identity matching, the archive lists), it has changed in the same words (point 2). A file that cannot be opened or read is no answer either way and stays a failure to read it. An import's source and `repo rebuild-index` have no record to compare line 1 with, and read it as before.

2. **What cannot be used, and the message.** The manifest of a recorded revision cannot be used when it is:

   | The manifest | Failure class (§A6) | What is wrong | How to fix it |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | gone | `RepositoryError` | "is missing" | restore it from a backup of the repository |
   | not readable | `RepositoryError` | "cannot be read: C" (the file system's cause) | restore it from a backup of the repository |
   | changed: line 1 or its body not the one recorded (point 1, R-62), or other bytes when it is read again for its entries | `ManifestHashMismatch` | "has changed: its first line differs from what the repository recorded" (or "its body does not match its header", "it gave other bytes when it was read again") | restore it from a backup of the repository |
   | the one recorded, but the index's copy of its header differs | `RepositoryError` | "the repository's index differs from the manifest ..." | rebuild the index with `repo rebuild-index` |

   Every use of such a manifest says the same plain message: what is wrong, naming the manifest by its revision and its place in the repository, as `archive show` lists it, then how to fix it, as "the manifest of revision 2 (manifests/<archive_uuid>/r0002.vam) is missing; restore it from a backup of the repository" or "the repository's index differs from the manifest of revision 2 (manifests/<archive_uuid>/r0002.vam); rebuild the index with `repo rebuild-index`". Registration, relocation, `archive compare`, `export` and `export-sha256sums` give it too, after their own words, where a manifest they need cannot be used (`UnusableManifest`, `plain_message`).

3. **A verification that knows its archive records the failure.** An archive named by its uuid (`verify --full <archive_uuid>`), by the run of it that is resumed (`verify --resume <run_id>`), or chosen in the GUI's Verify Archive (which verifies by uuid, also when it resumes) is known before anything is looked for on the media. The manifest of its current revision is checked first, up to its body's hash (`check_recorded_manifest`). When it cannot be used, the verification FAILS at once:
   - It is recorded as a FULL verification of that archive and revision, `FAILED`, with the failure class of point 2, nothing covered and no items, appended to the history's hash chain as every run is (R-18, R-90). It keeps no record of its own for a resume, as it read nothing.
   - It reads nothing of the media, and records no media evidence: it observed none (R-96).
   - It says so. The CLI prints "media: not read, as the manifest of this revision cannot be used" in place of the root, the identity, the coverage and the items, and the plain message of point 2 as the manifest's warning; it exits 3, as every `FAILED` verification does. `--json` gives `media_read: false`, with `root` and `identity` null; every verification's `--json` gives `media_read`. The GUI's report says "Media: not read, as the manifest of this revision cannot be used" and the same warning.
   - A run that it was to resume stays as it was, its record and checkpoints kept: once the manifest is restored, `verify --resume` continues it without reading again what it verified (R-104), and the GUI's Verify Archive still offers **Resume interrupted run** for it.

   A manifest that can be used is read again with its entries once the media is found, as before. A verification by root finds its archive by matching (point 4). Once matching has found the archive, a manifest whose body does not match, or that gives other bytes when it is read for its entries, fails the run as before: the media was looked at, and its evidence is recorded.

4. **A verification by root names the archive that stops it.** A verification by root finds its archive by matching the root's media evidence and folder against every archive's latest carrier (§B10), which only each archive's latest manifest records. While one of them cannot be used, the root may belong to that archive, whatever the others say, so the verification stops, as before, with exit 11, and records nothing. Its message says which archive, why, and how to go on, in that order: "this root may belong to archive ARCH-0007 (<archive_uuid>), whose manifest cannot be used: the manifest of revision 2 (manifests/<archive_uuid>/r0002.vam) is missing; verify that archive by its uuid (`verify --full <archive_uuid>`) to record the failure, then restore its manifest from a backup of the repository", or, where only the index's copy differs, "..., then rebuild the index with `repo rebuild-index`". The archive named is the first, in the order of the labels, whose latest manifest cannot be used; `archive list` shows every one (point 5). A verification of an archive named by its uuid, or by the run it resumes, is never stopped so: it reads no other archive's manifest, and its own is point 3. A manifest of an earlier revision, which matching reads only where the earlier carriers decide the outcome (decision 0004), stops it with the plain message of point 2 alone: a verification by uuid verifies the latest revision, and would record nothing of an earlier manifest.

5. **One damaged archive never stops the list.** `archive list` (text and `--json`), `archive show <archive_uuid>` and the GUI's Archive List read each archive's latest header from its manifest (R-14). An archive whose latest manifest cannot be used is listed like every other: its record (label, uuid, revision, status) and its last FULL verification, last verification and OVERDUE, which the repository holds without the manifest. What only the manifest holds (the volume, model and capacity, `copy_of`, and the identity and counts of `archive show --json`) is left out, and the problem stands in its place, in the plain message of point 2, which ends with how to fix it:
   - In text, one more line under the archive: "problem: the manifest of revision 2 (manifests/<archive_uuid>/r0002.vam) is missing; restore it from a backup of the repository" (in zh-TW "問題：…").
   - In `--json`, the member `manifest_problem`: `kind` (`missing`, `unreadable`, `changed` or `index_differs`), `revision`, `manifest` (its place in the repository) and `message` (the plain message). Only such an archive has it, as only an archive with an unfinished relocation has `unfinished_relocation`.
   - The exit code is 2, in text and `--json` alike, for `archive list` while any archive listed has such a manifest, and for `archive show` of such an archive: the listing worked and is complete, and an archive needs the operator. `repo rebuild-index` exits 2 likewise after it skipped a manifest that it cannot read.
   - The GUI shows "Problem: " and the plain message under the archive's row, from the Volume column on, in red, in the interface language. The archive stays one to choose in Verify Archive (its choices read no manifest), and verifying it there records the failure (point 3).

   Only a failure of the database still stops the list. The list reads line 1 alone (point 1): a manifest whose line 1 is intact but whose body has changed is listed without a problem, and the verification of its archive fails on it (point 3).

## Why

The BLAKE3 recorded for line 1 is what the repository knows of it. Other bytes are a changed manifest whether or not they still parse: a brace that a failing disk flipped, or a header cut short, says no more about its archive than a changed volume label does. Parsing first made such a change read as a defect of the manifest's format ("invalid manifest header: parse error ...", `RepositoryError`), as if the repository could not read what it had written, and hid that the manifest is not the one recorded.

A verification that stops records nothing: the archive's history kept its last result, `PASSED` say, and "Last verify" said nothing of a manifest that no verification can use any more. A verification that cannot check its archive against the recorded manifest has failed one of the checks of §A6 (the manifest's hash, R-14), and recording it is what makes the history and the Archive List say so (R-21). The archive is known without its media, and the manifest's check needs none: looking for the media first would at best find a carrier that nothing can be compared with, and would stop without recording anything whenever the media is not attached.

The Archive List and `archive list` are where the operator sees the state of every archive (R-21). Stopping them for one archive hid all the others, also those that need attention, and left the damaged one no place to say what is wrong with it. A manifest that cannot be used is a defect that the operator must repair before any verification of the archive can pass, so the list does not exit 0 as if nothing were wrong; the listing itself worked and is complete, so it does not exit 11 either, which a script takes for a repository it could not read.

Whether a root could belong to an archive is told from what the repository trusts: the archive's carrier and its root are recorded in its latest manifest alone (R-14, decision 0004), and the index's copy of that header is never taken in its place (CLAUDE.md rule 3), least of all where the two may disagree. Without the manifest, the root could be that archive's. Matching without the archive could then take the root for another archive's (two archives on cloned media would read as one `MATCH` rather than `AMBIGUOUS`), for an earlier carrier, or for no archive's at all (`UNKNOWN`, which offers `register`: a second identity for the same media). Stopping keeps the verification from answering wrongly; the message tells the operator the two steps that end it, in the order that records the failure first.

The failure classes are §A6's own. Bytes that are not the ones recorded are a manifest hash that does not match (R-14, R-62). A manifest that is gone or cannot be read, and an index that differs from it, are failures of the repository, as decision 0020 classes a manifest file that cannot be read. The message names the manifest by its place in the repository because that is what the operator restores: a backup of the repository holds it at the same place (R-86). Rebuilding the index from a manifest that is missing or has changed would take what the file holds now for the truth, so only an index that differs from an intact manifest is rebuilt.

Rejected alternatives:

- Parsing line 1 first and comparing its hash afterwards, as a whole manifest was loaded until now: a header changed into text that no longer parses never reaches the comparison.
- Comparing the hash in every reader of manifests: an import's source and `repo rebuild-index` read manifests that no record vouches for, where a line 1 that does not parse is what is wrong with them.
- Looking for the media first, and recording media evidence with the failure: the verification would stop, recording nothing, whenever the media is not attached, and the evidence would say only that the media was there.
- Recording `INCOMPLETE`: an `INCOMPLETE` run makes no claim (§A6), and this verification has found something wrong.
- Loading the whole manifest before looking for the media, and keeping it for the comparison: for an archive whose media is not attached, millions of entries would be parsed for nothing. The check reads the manifest without its entries; they are read once the media is found.
- Taking over the run being resumed (its checkpoints moved to the failed run): the work it did would be lost for a manifest that the operator restores in minutes.
- A failure class of its own (`ManifestMissing`): §A6's classes cover it, and a new class is a change for every reader of the history.
- Going on by root when the index's copy of the damaged archive's header (or `archives.root_subpath`) names another volume or folder: it takes the index for the manifest (CLAUDE.md rule 3).
- Matching by root without the archive whose manifest cannot be used: its carrier would read as another archive's, an earlier carrier or no archive's, as said above.
- Recording a `FAILED` run, by root, for the archive that the message names: the root was not found to be its. Only a verification that knows its archive records the failure (point 3); the message names the archive so that the operator makes that verification.
- Naming every archive whose latest manifest cannot be used in the message: a long message for a rare case, which the operator works through one archive at a time anyway; `archive list` names every one.
- `archive list` exiting 0, as it does for an archive that is OVERDUE or whose last verification FAILED: those are states that the history records; a manifest that cannot be used is a repair the operator owes the repository, and a script that checks only the exit code would never see it.
- Exiting 11, or 3: the listing worked and is complete, and no verification failed in it.
- Checking every manifest's body for the list: it would hash gigabytes of manifests for a listing; the body is checked where the archive is verified (point 3).
- Taking the volume, model and capacity of the damaged archive from the index's copy of its header: CLAUDE.md rule 3.
- `manifest_problem: null` on every archive: the members for a state that only some archives are in are given only to them (`unfinished_registration`, `unfinished_relocation`, `copy_of`).

## Consequences

- No change to the manifest bytes, the database schema or the golden test.
- §B10, R-96: a verification that failed on its manifest before it looked for the media records no media evidence.
- §B10: while the latest manifest of any archive cannot be used, every verification by root stops, also of a root that is clearly another archive's; the other archives verify by their uuid meanwhile, as a verification by uuid reads no other archive's manifest. Registration and relocation, which match every archive too, stop with the plain message of point 2.
- §B16: `verify --full <archive_uuid>` and `verify --resume` exit 3 with a recorded `FAILED` run where they exited 11; the `--json` of every verification gives `media_read`. `archive list` and `archive show` exit 2, where they exited 11, while an archive listed has a manifest that cannot be used; their `--json` gives it `manifest_problem`, and their text a "problem:" line.
- §B17: the Archive List shows the problem under the archive's row. The GUI has no unit test of its own (GTK): its words are `plain_message`'s, which the tests cover in both languages.
- The mark of the archives that the Archive List takes with every reading of the unfinished operations (decision 0022) does not follow the manifests' files: a manifest that goes missing shows in the list when the list next reads the archives (**Refresh**, or any operation recorded meanwhile, as a verification of the archive).
- A `FAILED` run is the archive's last FULL verification and its last verification (R-21), as every `FAILED` run is: the Archive List shows it in red, and OVERDUE is reckoned from it (R-105).
- The manifest of a named archive that can be used is read twice: checked before the media is looked for, then read with its entries; one more pass of BLAKE3 over the manifest, seconds for millions of entries.
- Core: `ManifestFailure::Kind::HeaderMismatch` and `ManifestFailure::io_kind`; `ManifestReader::open` takes the BLAKE3 recorded for line 1; `UnusableManifest`, `failure_class_of`, `what_is_wrong_with` and `plain_message`; `ManifestLoadFailure::unusable`; `check_recorded_manifest`; `load_recorded_header` fails with an `UnusableManifest`; `VerifyOutcome::media_read`; `load_candidates` fails with a `CandidatesFailure` (the manifest that cannot be used, and its archive's label); `archive_statuses` gives such an archive `ArchiveStatus::unusable_manifest` and fails only with the database.
- Tests: the reader given the BLAKE3 recorded opens the manifest recorded, and fails with `HeaderMismatch` for a line 1 that no longer parses, one cut off, the line recorded without its newline, an empty file and another header that parses, while a file that is gone stays an I/O failure and, without the BLAKE3, a line 1 that does not parse stays a malformed manifest; a recorded manifest whose header no longer parses, is cut off before its newline, or is empty fails `load_recorded_manifest`, `archive compare`, `export` and a verification by uuid with `ManifestHashMismatch`, and line 1 alone in the same words; an archive named by its uuid whose manifest is missing, cannot be read, or has a changed header line or body, verified while its media cannot even be listed: `FAILED` with the class and the message of point 2, no byte read, no media evidence, the run recorded (FULL, its revision, nothing covered) in an intact chain, nothing left to resume, and verified once the manifest is restored; an index whose copy of the header differs, verified by uuid (`FAILED`, `RepositoryError`, rebuild the index) and stopping every other use; a resume whose manifest is missing, `FAILED` while the interrupted run keeps its record and checkpoints, then resumed reading only the rest; another archive named by its uuid that verifies; a verification by root stopped by an archive whose manifest is missing, has a changed header line, or whose record or index differs, naming the archive, what is wrong and the two steps (restore, or rebuild the index), recording nothing; a named archive whose manifest cannot be read after its check, which stops with the plain message alone; a second read that differs, by root, with the media read; registration, relocation and the unfinished verifications' totals in the new words; the list of three archives, one with its header, one whose manifest is missing and one whose header line changed, each with its kind, failure class and message and the history it has, also as `archive show` reads one, while the labels alone show no problem; an index that differs and a manifest that is not the one recorded, listed; the zh-TW wording, also of the message by root for each kind; the CLI end to end (a changed header line verified by uuid: exit 3, `FAILED`, `media_read` false, `root` null, the message in English and Chinese, the run in the history; by root: exit 11, naming the archive by its label and uuid; `archive list` exiting 2 with the problem line in English and Chinese and every other archive, `manifest_problem` in `--json`, none for the copy, `archive show` exiting 2 with the line). A mutation check removed each guard in turn (the comparison before parsing, the comparison of a line 1 cut off or empty, the BLAKE3 given by `load_recorded_manifest`, its failure class, line 1 alone of a manifest cut off, the check before the media, recording the failed run, `media_read`, the run being resumed left alone, the failure class of a changed manifest, a missing manifest told from an unreadable one, the manifest named in the message, the index rebuilt rather than restored, a second read told from a body, the index's copy compared in a whole load, `root` null and the line that says the media was not read in the CLI, the message by root and its archive's label, the step to verify by uuid, the index rebuilt by root, and that message for a named archive, the list that stops on a damaged archive or forgets its problem, exit 2 of `archive list` and `archive show`, the `--json` member and its kind, and the text line of `archive list` and `archive show`), and each removal failed the test meant for it.
